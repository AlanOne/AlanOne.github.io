MIDI and instruments

Add an instrument track

Track > New Instrument Track..., CtrlAltSOptionCmdS, opens a menu:

  • Synth (built in).
  • Drum Machine (built in) with a kit: Vintage Kit (the usual), Club Kit, Rock Kit, or the synthesised Electronic Kit.
  • Keys (built in): Grand Piano, Soft Grand Piano, Upright Piano, Electric Grand, Pianet, Wurlitzer, Drawbar Organ.
  • Bass (built in): Jazz Bass, Picked Bass, Upright Bass, Electric Bass.
  • Guitar (built in): Steel-String, Nylon and Clean Electric Guitar.
  • Strings and Pads (built in): Tremolo Strings, Sweep Pad, Choir Pad.
  • Sampler (built in)...: your own sample or .sfz instrument.
  • Plugins: your scanned instrument plugins.

Dropping an .sfz file onto the song also makes an instrument track that plays it.

Write notes in the piano roll

Double-click an instrument track's lane to make a MIDI clip; the MIDI editor opens below (also View > MIDI Editor, P, or double-click any MIDI clip). In the piano roll:

  • Click the empty grid to add a note; drag a note to move it (all selected ones; CtrlCmd, ignores the grid); drag its end to resize it.
  • Shift-click adds to the selection; drag the empty grid to select several.
  • Backspace or Delete removes the selected notes; CtrlACmdA in the editor selects every note.
  • Drag the bars in the velocity lane to change velocities.
  • Click a key on the keyboard to hear it (louder further right).

Piano Roll / Drums / Notation switch the view.

Quantise notes

Press Q (Edit > Quantise) to snap the selected notes (or all of them) to the snap grid, with its feel. For more control, click Quantise in the MIDI editor: outlines show where notes will go before you apply it. Lower Strength to move them part of the way, narrow Window so only notes near the grid move, tick Note ends too to square off lengths, or press Align for a gentle pass (MIDI > Align Notes does the same), then Quantise in it (it closes). Each is one undo step.

Quantise to the feel of another part (groove)

Right-click a MIDI clip you played (a drummer's hi-hats, say) > Use as Groove, or a recorded drum take (audio) > Use as Groove (its feel): its hits are found and how far each sits from the grid (set in the Snap box) is the feel. The feel box then shows "Groove: <clip>", and quantising MIDI parts makes them take on that feel, so a programmed bass or synth part sits with the real drummer. Choose Straight in the feel box to go back.

Play notes from the computer keyboard (Musical Typing)

MIDI > Musical Typing, CtrlKCmdK, opens a window with the keys. With an instrument track armed, A S D F... play the white keys and W E T Y... the black keys; Z / X go an octave down / up, C / V change the velocity, Tab is the sustain pedal. Record with it like a keyboard. While it's open, letters play notes instead of running shortcuts (typing in a text box still types). Keys the instrument can't reach are dimmed, and the window says which way to go (for example "Z for lower"). CtrlKCmdK again closes it.

Enter notes one step at a time (step input)

Open a MIDI clip in the editor and choose MIDI > Step Input, AltIOptionI, or its button in the editor. Play notes or chords on your MIDI keyboard: each goes in at the playhead and it moves on a step. Set the step length in the box next to it (1/1 to 1/32, and triplets); Rest leaves a gap, Tie makes the last notes a step longer, Back takes the last step out.

Mute notes without deleting them

Select notes in the piano roll and choose MIDI > Mute Notes (CtrlMControlM): they're drawn faded and don't play, export or show in notation. The same key unmutes them. Useful for trying a part without some notes, or keeping a wrong note to fix later. MIDI > Mute Notes needs the MIDI editor open (P) with notes selected.

Humanise, transpose and change velocities (Transform Notes)

The MIDI editor's transform button (or MIDI > Transform Notes) works on the selected notes, or on every note in the clip: Humanise..., Legato, Remove Doubled Notes, Fixed Length, Scale Velocities..., Compress Velocities..., Set Velocity..., Transpose..., Transpose in Key and Snap to Key. Each is one undo step.

Set the song's key and see notes outside it

Click the Key button in the MIDI editor (or MIDI > Song Key): choose the Key and Scale, or Detect Key from This Clip on a played part. With Shade Notes Outside the Key on, out-of-key rows are shaded in the piano roll and the root is tinted. The key is also used for notation spelling, chord ideas and pitch correction.

Draw pitch bend, mod wheel and other controllers

In the MIDI editor, choose what the bottom lane shows: Velocity, Pitch Bend, Mod Wheel or Other CC.... Drag to draw freehand, Shift-drag for a straight line, AltOption-drag to erase. The instrument follows it on playback.

Add an arpeggiator or chord effect

Select an instrument track and choose MIDI > Add MIDI Effect: Arpeggiator (Pattern: Up, Down, Up & Down, As Played, Random; Rate, Octaves and Gate), Chord (one key plays a chord: major, minor, 7ths, sus, power, octaves) or Transpose (MIDI Modifier). It goes before the track's instrument and its window opens.

Map a controller knob to a fader or plugin (MIDI learn)

Right-click a fader, pan knob or plugin-window control > MIDI Learn, or choose MIDI > MIDI Learn and pick the selected track's volume, pan or a plugin parameter. Then move a knob or fader on your controller: they're linked. Esc cancels while it's waiting. MIDI > MIDI Mappings... lists the mappings and removes them; they're saved with the song.

Import or export a MIDI file

  • Import: drag a .mid file into the song, or File > Import Audio or MIDI.... Each part gets a track (drums on a drum kit), with its controllers and pitch bend. Dropped on an instrument track, it merges into one clip there. Choose "Use the MIDI file's tempo" to take its tempo and time signature.
  • Export: File > Export Other Formats > Export MIDI File... writes the whole song (tempo, time signatures, key, section markers, a track per part, drums as drums). Right-click a track > Export Track as MIDI File..., or a MIDI clip > Export as MIDI File..., for just that.

Write chords in the chord lane

View > Lanes > Chord Lane shows the song's chords under the sections. Double-click to add a chord (type a name like C, Am7, F#dim, Bbmaj7, Dsus4), drag a chord to move it, right-click to change or remove it. From a MIDI clip of chords: select it, right-click the lane > Chords > Detect Chords from Clip. To hear them, Chords > Make a Clip from the Chords: Held Chords, Bass Note and Chord or Arpeggio (eighths). The chords print above the notation.

Get chord progression and melody ideas

Put the playhead in a section (or drag a marquee over the bars), show the chord lane, right-click it > Chords > Chord Ideas for ... and choose a style (Pop, Ballad, Rock, Folk, Jazz; a chord a bar or two): chords in the song's key appear, and choosing again gives another idea. Then Chords > Melody Idea for ... and a contour (arch, rising, falling, wave) and density (sparse, medium, busy): a "Melody idea" clip on the selected instrument track, or a new Melody track. Keep it, edit it like any MIDI, or delete it. CtrlZCmdZ undoes either.

Strum chords, add flams and grace notes, chop and ratchet notes (writing parts faster)

In the MIDI editor, MIDI > Transform Notes (or the editor's own menu) works on the selected notes, or every note in the clip when none are:

  • Strum Chords: each chord's notes one after another like a guitar: Down (low to high), Up (high to low), or Down and Up, Alternating chord by chord; each later string a little softer. Strum Spread... sets how far apart (18 ms by default) and how much softer.
  • Add Flams: a softer hit of the same note 25 ms before each (snares).
  • Add Grace Notes: a short note a semitone or a tone below (or a tone above) leading into each.
  • Chop into Repeats: each note cut into 2, 3, 4 or 8 equal repeats.
  • Ratchets: each note played 2 to 6 times in its length, short, or dying away.
  • Chance (plays some of the time): a note plays 75 %, 50 % or 25 % of the time, the dice rolled again each time round (a hi-hat that varies, a ghost note now and then). Always puts it back. A note with a chance is drawn dashed, with its percentage on it.
  • Euclidean Rhythm...: hits spread as evenly as they go over a number of steps (3 of 8 is the tresillo, 5 of 16 a son clave's feel), turned by a rotation, at a note you name (C1, F#1 or a number), over the whole clip, replacing that note's hits there. Random Rhythm... does the same from a seed and a density: the same seed gives the same rhythm, another seed another one.

Each is one undo step.

Turn a sung or hummed melody into MIDI (Melody to MIDI)

Right-click an audio clip of one voice or instrument (a vocal, a hummed idea, a bass DI, a lead guitar) > Melody to MIDI: its notes, found as the Pitch editor finds them (its splits, joins and octave fixes included), become a MIDI clip on a new instrument track under it, each note rounded to the nearest semitone and as loud as it was sung. It plays on the built-in synth (change the instrument as for any track); the Notation view shows it as notes, and as tab on a guitar or bass part. Short blips (under 70 ms) are left out. The other way, Correct to a MIDI Part (same menu) corrects the clip's pitch to a MIDI part on a track you choose: each sung note goes to the MIDI note playing at its middle (as if each were set by hand in the Pitch editor, where they show and can be changed).

Show a part as sheet music (notation)

Open a MIDI clip and click Notation in the MIDI editor (MIDI > Notation, CtrlShiftNShiftCmdN). Notes show in the song's key and time signature with the chord lane's chords above; Whole Track shows every clip on the track; the box next to it rounds played timing to 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32. Click a note to select it, Up/Down move it a semitone (CtrlCmd: an octave), 2 to 7 set its length, . dots it, Delete removes it, Left/Right go to the next note. The notes playing light up and the page follows the playhead.

Show guitar or bass tab

A MIDI track named like a guitar or bass ("Gtr", "Guitar", "Bass") gets tab by itself. Or right-click the track > Notation > Guitar with Tab or Bass with Tab (Standard for none), and pick a Tuning there (Drop D, a 5-string bass...). Guitar is written in treble clef an octave down with tab below; bass in bass clef with four or five lines. Click a fret number to select its note.

Print sheet music or save it as PDF or MusicXML

File > Export Other Formats > Sheet Music..., CtrlPCmdP, or the MIDI editor's Sheet Music... button. Tick the parts, set the title, paper (A4 or US Letter), shortest note, triplets, chords and sections, and check the preview. Print... prints (on Windows it opens the PDF in your PDF viewer to print from), Save PDF... saves it, Save Parts... saves each ticked part as its own PDF for each player, and Save MusicXML... writes a file for notation apps (MusicXML is the format they share). One part with chords makes a lead sheet. The MIDI itself isn't changed.

Use the Sampler with my own samples

Track > New Instrument Track... > Sampler (built in)... and pick a WAV, AIFF, FLAC or OGG sample (played across the keyboard; a note name in the file name, like "Piano C4.wav", sets its root) or an .sfz instrument (with its zones, velocity layers and loops). The Sampler's window has Attack, Release, Cutoff and more. The sustain pedal works.

Drums

Program a beat on the Drum Machine

Track > New Instrument Track... > Drum Machine (built in) and a kit. In its window (click its plugin slot): click a pad to hear it (harder lower down). The step grid edits the MIDI clip at the playhead a bar at a time: click a step to add or remove a hit, Shift-click an empty step for an accented hit, < and > go to the pattern's other bars. With no clip there, Make a 1-Bar Pattern makes one. The drum editor shows the same notes with the pads' names.

Change a drum pad's sound or kit

In the Drum Machine window: Kit switches the whole kit (Vintage, Club, Rock or Electronic) while it plays. Select a pad to change its Tune, Decay, Pan and Level. Drop a sample on a pad to replace its sound, or right-click it > Load Sample..., Choke Group (pads in a group cut each other off, like the open and closed hi-hat) or Remove Sample. Undo puts each change back.

Edit drums in the drum editor

Open a drum track's MIDI clip (double-click it, or P): the editor shows named drum rows, with hits as diamonds, for the Drum Machine (or click Drums in the MIDI editor for any clip). Add, move and delete hits and change velocities as in the piano roll; Q quantises. Clicking beside a hit that's already in that grid cell selects it rather than adding a second one (the piano roll does the same for notes).

Give the kick, snare and hats their own mixer channels

Right-click the drum track's header > Drum Channels > Kick, Snare, Hats, Toms and Cymbals (or Every Pad): tracks appear under it, one per group, each with its own fader, plugins and sends. Put a compressor on the kick without touching the hats. In the Drum Machine window, a pad's Output moves it to a channel, or New Channel for This Pad. Muting the drum track leaves its channels playing. Drum Channels > All Back to One Output undoes it; deleting a channel puts its drums back on the drum track.

Slice a drum loop onto pads

Import a drum loop, right-click it > Slice at Hits > under Onto Drum Machine pads (up to 16), choose Fewer slices (the main hits), Normal or More slices (quieter hits too). A new track below gets a Drum Machine with a slice on each pad and a MIDI clip that plays the loop as it was; the original is muted. Move or quantise hits in the drum editor to rearrange it. Across Sampler keys puts a slice on each key from C1 instead (good for riffs and vocal phrases).

Add a sample to every kick or snare hit (drum replacement)

  1. Right-click the kick (or snare) mic's track header > Drum Replacement.... With a marquee range on the track, only that range is used.
  2. The picture shows every hit found at its level. Adjust Sensitivity (higher finds quieter hits), Gap (lower for fast double kick) and Leave out quieter than (or drag the line) so only real hits count, not bleed. Each hit's level is measured in its own drum's band (a kick's lows, a snare's crack), where another drum's bleed is far quieter, so the -24 dB it starts at leaves most bleed out and keeps ghost notes.
  3. Choose the Drum and kit (or Sample of Your Own...), and Blend under it or Replace it.
  4. Make Trigger: a "<track> Trigger" track appears right under it, lined up in time and polarity.

Solo the trigger to check every hit. Its track menu > Drum Replacement Settings... changes it later (Update Trigger). One undo step takes it all back.

A drum part for the song before the drummer's heard it (the session drummer)

Track > New Drummer Track adds a drummer: a Drum Machine track (with the Rock Kit) that plays a part for the whole song, following its sections (add section markers first, Track > Add Section Marker; with none it plays the whole song). Press Play. Its window (Track > Drummer..., or the track header's menu > Drummer...) sets how it plays:

  • Style: Rock, Half-Time, Punk, Metal (double kick) or Blast Beat. A section can have its own.
  • Kick follows: a track whose rhythm the kick plays (its MIDI notes, or the hits in its audio), e.g. the bass.
  • For each section: Plays (off for a break), Simple - Busy (which groove and fill), Soft - Loud (how hard; the hats move to the ride when it's loud) and Fill into it (none, the last two beats, or the whole bar before it). Sections start from their names: an intro softer and simpler, a chorus louder and busier with a long fill into it, a break silent.

Every change writes its part again (a MIDI clip per section) as one undo step. Write Again catches up after the song's sections, tempo or the followed track change. Turn into MIDI keeps the part as ordinary clips with every hit, to edit in the drum editor; if you edit the drummer's clips without doing that, it asks before writing over them. A drum track with nothing on it yet: its track menu > Make It the Drummer. Bars not in 4/4 get a plain beat for now. The patterns are in resources/drummer/patterns.json, so styles can be added. The assistant can do all of it ("a punk drummer, busy in the choruses, the kick on the bass").
