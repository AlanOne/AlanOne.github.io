MIDI and instruments

Add an instrument track

Track > New Instrument Track..., CtrlAltSOptionCmdS, opens a menu:

  • Synth (built in): a track named "Synth".
  • Drum Machine (built in) with a kit: Vintage Kit (the usual), Club Kit, Rock Kit, or the synthesised Electronic Kit; a track named "Drums".
  • Keys (built in): Grand Piano, Soft Grand Piano, Upright Piano, Electric Grand, Pianet, Wurlitzer, Drawbar Organ.
  • Bass (built in): Jazz Bass, Picked Bass, Upright Bass, Electric Bass.
  • Guitar (built in): Steel-String, Nylon and Clean Electric Guitar.
  • Strings and Pads (built in): Tremolo Strings, Sweep Pad, Choir Pad.
  • Sampler (built in)...: your own sample or .sfz instrument.
  • Plugins: your scanned instrument plugins, by maker (none until you scan: Settings > Plugins...).

The new track is selected, ready to arm and play; one undo step. Dropping an .sfz file onto the song also makes an instrument track that plays it.

Write notes in the piano roll

Double-click an instrument track's lane to make a MIDI clip; the MIDI editor opens below (also View > MIDI Editor, P, or double-click any MIDI clip). In the piano roll:

  • Click the empty grid to add a note (a grid step long, velocity 100, inside the clip); drag a note to move it (all selected ones); drag its end to resize it (down to a quarter of a grid step). Hold CtrlCmd to add, move or resize off the grid.
  • Shift-click adds a note to the selection or takes it out; drag the empty grid to select several (Shift-drag adds them).
  • Backspace or Delete removes the selected notes; CtrlACmdA in the editor selects every note.
  • Drag the bars in the velocity lane to change velocities (1 to 127): dragging a selected note's bar changes all the selected ones by the same amount. One undo step a drag.
  • Click a key on the keyboard to hear it (louder further right); slide along the keys to play each.
  • AltOption-scroll makes the rows taller or shorter.

Piano Roll / Drums / Notation switch the view; Key, Transform, Step Input and Quantise are next to them.

Quantise notes

Press Q (Edit > Quantise) to snap the selected notes (or all of them) to the snap grid, with its feel (straight or swung, from the feel box). For more control, click Quantise in the MIDI editor: outlines show where notes will go before you apply it. Strength (100% to start) moves them part of the way, a narrower Window (100% to start) moves only notes near the grid, Note ends too squares off lengths; Align sets a gentle pass (60%, notes near the grid, ends left alone) for you to apply with Quantise (it closes). MIDI > Align Notes applies that gentle pass at once. Each is one undo step. Q always goes all the way to the grid; the panel's settings (kept while Take 5 is open) are used when you press its own Quantise. With audio clips selected, Q quantises the audio instead (see "Tighten the timing of a drum take"); on a drum kit in an edit group, it opens Quantise Group.

Quantise to the feel of another part (groove)

Right-click a MIDI clip you played (a drummer's hi-hats, say) > Use as Groove, or a recorded drum take (audio) > Use as Groove (its feel): its hits are found and how far each sits from the grid (set in the Snap box) is the feel. The feel box then shows "Groove: <clip>", and quantising MIDI parts makes them take on that feel, so a programmed bass or synth part sits with the real drummer. The groove applies while the Snap box is on the grid it was taken with (on another grid quantising is straight, until you go back to that one), and it's kept with the song. Choose Straight (or a swing) in the feel box to go back.

Play notes from the computer keyboard (Musical Typing)

MIDI > Musical Typing, CtrlKCmdK, opens a window with the keys. With an instrument track armed, A S D F... play the white keys (A is middle C to start) and W E T Y... the black keys; Z / X go an octave down / up, C / V change the velocity (by 20; 100 to start), Tab is the sustain pedal; the window shows the octave and velocity. It plays as a MIDI keyboard would, so the armed instrument track hears it. Record with it like a keyboard. While it's open, letters play notes instead of running shortcuts (typing in a text box still types). Keys the instrument can't reach are dimmed, and the window says which way to go (for example "Z for lower"). CtrlKCmdK again closes it.

Enter notes one step at a time (step input)

Open a MIDI clip in the editor and choose MIDI > Step Input, AltIOptionI, or its button in the editor. Play notes or chords on your MIDI keyboard: each goes in at the playhead (on the grid; at the clip's start if the playhead isn't in it), as loud as you played it, and it moves on a step. Set the step length in the box that appears (1/1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/8 T or 1/16 T; the snap grid to start); Rest leaves a gap, Tie makes the last notes a step longer, Back takes the last step out. The clip grows if you write past its end. Each step is one undo step.

Mute notes without deleting them

Select notes in the piano roll and choose MIDI > Mute Notes (CtrlMControlM; a real Control key, not the Command key): they're drawn faded and don't play, export or show in notation. With none selected it mutes the whole clip. The same key unmutes them (if all are muted already). Useful for trying a part without some notes, or keeping a wrong note to fix later. It needs the MIDI editor open (P); it's in Transform too.

Humanise, transpose and change velocities (Transform Notes)

The MIDI editor's transform button (or MIDI > Transform Notes) works on the selected notes, or on every note in the clip (the menu's first line says which):

  • Humanise...: small nudges in timing (up to 12 ms) and velocity (up to 10 either way).
  • Legato: each note runs to the next one's start (notes starting together count as a chord).
  • Remove Doubled Notes: the same key twice within 40 ms (a keyboard's double triggers).
  • Fixed Length: 1/1 to 1/32.
  • Scale Velocities... (a percentage and an amount), Compress Velocities... (50% evens them out half way, 100% makes them all the same, below 0 spreads them) and Set Velocity... (1 to 127).
  • Transpose... (semitones, 12 to start), Transpose in Key (up or down a step, a third, a fifth or an octave, in the song's key) and Snap to Key (each note to the nearest one in the key).

Each is one undo step.

Set the song's key and see notes outside it

Click the Key button in the MIDI editor ("Key: C Major") or MIDI > Song Key: choose the Key and Scale (Major, Minor, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian, Harmonic Minor or Melodic Minor), or Detect Key from This Clip on a played part (it says when there aren't enough notes to tell). A new song is in C major; the key is kept with the song, each change one undo step. With Shade Notes Outside the Key on (it is to start with), out-of-key rows are shaded in the piano roll and the root is tinted. The key is also used for notation spelling, chord ideas, Transpose in Key and pitch correction.

Draw pitch bend, mod wheel and other controllers

In the MIDI editor, choose what the bottom lane shows: Velocity, Pitch Bend, Mod Wheel (CC 1), Expression (CC 11), Volume (CC 7), Pan (CC 10), Sustain (CC 64), Breath (CC 2), Brightness (CC 74), Aftertouch, any other controller the clip already uses (marked), or Other CC... (any number, 0 to 127). Drag to draw freehand, Shift-drag for a straight line, AltOption-drag to erase; each stroke is one undo step. Each value holds until the next; pitch bend snaps to the middle near it. The instrument follows it on playback.

Add an arpeggiator or chord effect

Select an instrument track and choose MIDI > Add MIDI Effect (greyed until one is selected):

  • Arpeggiator: Pattern (Up, Down, Up & Down, As Played, Random; Up to start), Rate (1/4 to 1/32, with triplets; 1/16 to start), Octaves (1 to 4) and Gate (how long each note is, 5 to 100%; 80 to start). Hold a chord and it plays its notes in turn, in time with the song.
  • Chord: one key plays a chord of that shape on any key: Major, Minor, 7th, Major 7th, Minor 7th, Sus 4, Sus 2, Power (5th) or Octaves (not tied to the song's key).
  • Transpose (MIDI Modifier).

It goes before the track's instrument (after any other MIDI effect) and its window opens. One undo step.

Map a controller knob to a fader or plugin (MIDI learn)

Right-click a fader, pan knob or a slider in a built-in plugin's window > MIDI Learn..., or choose MIDI > MIDI Learn and pick the selected track's volume, pan or a plugin parameter. Then move a knob or fader on your controller: they're linked (controllers only: notes and pitch bend aren't learnt; one knob for one control). Esc cancels while it's waiting; right-click > Forget unlinks one. A third-party plugin's own window can't be learnt from (use the built-in sliders, or MIDI > MIDI Learn). MIDI > MIDI Mappings... lists the mappings and removes them; they're saved with the song.

Import or export a MIDI file

  • Import: drag a .mid file into the song (new tracks where you drop it, on the song's tempo), or File > Import Audio or MIDI.... Each part gets a track (channel 10's drums on a Drum Machine, the rest on the built-in synth), with its controllers and pitch bend. The import window asks where (at the playhead or bar 1), whether onto new tracks or merged into the selected instrument track, and the tempo: Keep the song's tempo (to start with) or Use the MIDI file's tempo and time signature.
  • Export: File > Export Other Formats > Export MIDI File... writes the whole song (tempo, time signatures, key, section markers, a track per part, drums as drums). Right-click a track > Export Track as MIDI File..., or a MIDI clip > Export as MIDI File..., for just that.

Write chords in the chord lane

View > Lanes > Chord Lane shows the song's chords under the sections. Double-click to add a chord (type a name like C, Am7, F#dim, Bbmaj7, Dsus4), drag a chord to move it, right-click to change or remove it (Change..., Remove); chords snap to the grid. A name it doesn't know says what it does know ("try C, Cm, C7..."). From a MIDI clip of chords: select it, right-click the lane > Chords > Detect Chords from Clip (a chord a bar) or (a chord a beat). To hear them, Chords > Make a Clip from the Chords: Held Chords, Bass Note and Chord or Arpeggio (eighths), on the selected instrument track (or a new "Chords" track), over the marquee or the whole chord lane. Remove All Chords clears it. The same Chords menu is in MIDI > Chords. The chords print above the notation.

Get chord progression and melody ideas

Put the playhead in a section (or drag a marquee over the bars; with neither, the 8 bars from the playhead), show the chord lane, right-click it > Chords > Chord Ideas for ... and choose a style (Pop, Ballad, Rock, Folk, Jazz), a chord a bar or two a bar: chords in the song's key replace the ones there, and choosing again gives another idea. Then Chords > Melody Idea for ... and a contour (Arch (up, then down), Rising, Falling, Wave) and density (sparse, medium, busy): a "Melody idea" clip on the selected instrument track, or a new "Melody" track on the built-in synth. Keep it, edit it like any MIDI, or delete it. CtrlZCmdZ undoes either.

Strum chords, add flams and grace notes, chop and ratchet notes (writing parts faster)

In the MIDI editor, MIDI > Transform Notes (or the editor's own menu) works on the selected notes, or every note in the clip when none are:

  • Strum Chords: each chord's notes one after another like a guitar: Down (low to high), Up (high to low), or Down and Up, Alternating chord by chord; each later string a little softer (18 ms apart, each string 4 softer). Strum Spread... strums down with your own spacing (1 to 200 ms) and softening.
  • Add Flams: a softer hit (60%) of the same note 25 ms before each (snares).
  • Add Grace Notes: a short note (a 32nd) a semitone or a tone below (or a tone above) leading into each.
  • Chop into Repeats: each note cut into 2, 3, 4 or 8 equal repeats.
  • Ratchets: each note played 2, 3, 4 or 6 times in its length, short, or dying away (to 40%).
  • Chance (plays some of the time): a note plays 75 %, 50 % or 25 % of the time, the dice rolled again each time round (a hi-hat that varies, a ghost note now and then). Always puts it back. A note with a chance is drawn dashed, with its percentage on it.
  • Euclidean Rhythm...: hits spread as evenly as they go over a number of steps (3 of 8 is the tresillo, 5 of 16 a son clave's feel), turned by a rotation, at a note you name (C1, F#1 or a number), over the whole clip, replacing that note's hits there (3 of 8 eighths on C1 to start; up to 64 steps). Random Rhythm... does the same from a seed and a density (16 sixteenths, 50%, F#1 to start): the same seed gives the same rhythm, another seed another one. Both fill the whole clip, whatever's selected.

Each is one undo step.

Turn a sung or hummed melody into MIDI (Melody to MIDI)

Right-click an audio clip of one voice or instrument (a vocal, a hummed idea, a bass DI, a lead guitar) > Melody to MIDI: its notes, found as the Pitch editor finds them (its splits, joins and octave fixes included), become a MIDI clip on a new instrument track under it, each note rounded to the nearest semitone and as loud as it was sung, on a track named "<track> Notes" (if no notes are found, the status line says so). It plays on the built-in synth (change the instrument as for any track); the Notation view shows it as notes, and as tab on a guitar or bass part. Short blips (under 70 ms) are left out. The other way, Correct to a MIDI Part (same menu) corrects the clip's pitch to a MIDI part on a track you choose (tracks with MIDI clips): each sung note goes to the MIDI note playing at its middle (as if each were set by hand in the Pitch editor, where they show and can be changed). One undo step each.

Show a part as sheet music (notation)

Open a MIDI clip and click Notation in the MIDI editor (MIDI > Notation, CtrlShiftNShiftCmdN). Notes show in the song's key and time signature with the chord lane's chords above; Whole Track shows every clip on the track; the box next to it rounds played timing To 1/8, To 1/16 (to start) or To 1/32 (kept with the song, and the same as Sheet Music's). Click a note to select it (Shift-click for more, Esc for none), drag it up or down or press Up/Down to move it a semitone (CtrlCmd: an octave), 2 to 7 set its length (2 a 32nd, 3 a 16th, 4 an eighth, 5 a quarter, 6 a half, 7 a whole note), . dots it, Delete removes it, Left/Right go to the next note. Each edit is one undo step. The notes playing light up and the page follows the playhead.

Show guitar or bass tab

A MIDI track with a word like "Gtr", "Guitar" or "Bass" in its name gets tab by itself (Automatic (from the name); drum tracks never do). Or right-click the track > Notation > Guitar with Tab or Bass with Tab (Standard for none), and pick a Tuning there: for guitar standard, half or whole step down, drop D, C#, C or B, DADGAD, open D, E, G or C, and 7- and 8-string tunings; for bass standard, half step down, drop D, BEAD, 5-string (low B or high C) and 6-string. Guitar is written in treble clef an octave down with tab below; bass in bass clef with a line per string. Click a fret number to select its note.

Print sheet music or save it as PDF or MusicXML

File > Export Other Formats > Sheet Music..., CtrlPCmdP, or the MIDI editor's Sheet Music... button. Tick the parts (the MIDI editor's part, or the selected tracks, or all, to start with), set the title, paper (A4 or US Letter), shortest note (eighth, sixteenth or 32nd notes), triplets, chord symbols and section names (kept with the song), and check the preview, page by page. Print... prints (on Windows it opens the PDF in your PDF viewer to print from), Save PDF... saves it, Save Parts... saves each ticked part as its own PDF for each player, and Save MusicXML... writes a file for notation apps (MusicXML is the format they share). One part with chords makes a lead sheet. The MIDI itself isn't changed.

Use the Sampler with my own samples

Track > New Instrument Track... > Sampler (built in)... and pick a WAV, AIFF, FLAC or OGG sample (played across the keyboard; its root note is the one written in the file, or a note name in the file name like "Piano C4.wav", or C4; its loop is used) or an .sfz instrument (with its zones, velocity layers and loops). The Sampler's window has Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release, Cutoff, Resonance, Volume and Velocity (how much playing harder is louder), and can load another sample. The sustain pedal works.

Drums

Program a beat on the Drum Machine

Track > New Instrument Track... > Drum Machine (built in) and a kit: Vintage Kit (the usual), Club Kit, Rock Kit or Electronic Kit (synthesised). In its window (click its plugin slot):

  • The pads: 16, the kick at the bottom left, then the snares, hats, toms and cymbals going up. Click one to hear it, harder lower down on the pad.
  • The step grid edits the drum track's MIDI clip at the playhead (or its first clip), a page of 16 sixteenths at a time (a bar in 4/4): click a step to add or remove a hit, Shift-click an empty step for an accented one. The line above says which clip and bar it is ("Pattern: Drums, bar 1 of 16 (song bar 3)"); < and > go to its other bars (the grid follows the playhead again when you play).
  • New 1-Bar Pattern Here makes a clip a bar long where the playhead is (on a drum track with no clip yet, Make a 1-Bar Pattern). One undo step.

The drum editor shows the same notes with the pads' names (see "Edit drums in the drum editor").

Change a drum pad's sound or kit

In the Drum Machine window:

  • Kit switches the whole kit (Vintage, Club, Rock or Electronic), even while it plays. It replaces every pad's sound and settings (your own samples too); each pad keeps its Output.
  • Select a pad ("Pad 1: Kick") to change its Tune (-24 to +24 semitones), Decay (natural, or cut off after up to 3 seconds), Pan and Level (-24 to +12 dB). On the recorded kits a pad has several samples (soft to hard, and alternating), and these change them all together.
  • Drop a sample (WAV, AIFF, FLAC, OGG or MP3) on a pad to replace its sound, or right-click it > Load Sample..., Choke Group (None or 1 to 4: pads in a group cut each other off, as the open and closed hi-hat do; the hats start in group 1, shown on the pads) or Remove Sample.

The kit and the pads are kept with the song. Undo puts each change back (a slider drag is one step).

Edit drums in the drum editor

Open a drum track's MIDI clip (double-click it, or P): the editor shows a row per drum, named, with hits as diamonds coloured by family (kicks red, snares orange, hats yellow, toms teal, cymbals violet). On a track that isn't a Drum Machine, click Drums in the MIDI editor for the standard drum names (any note the kit doesn't name still gets a row, by its note). Add, move and delete hits and change velocities (the lane at the bottom) as in the piano roll; Q quantises. Hits snap to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, for off-grid), have no length to change, and clicking beside a hit that's already in that grid cell selects it rather than adding a second one (the piano roll does the same for notes). The editor opens in piano roll or drums from the clip's track each time. View > Windows > MIDI Editor in Own Window floats it.

Give the kick, snare and hats their own mixer channels

Right-click the drum track's header > Drum Channels > Kick, Snare, Hats, Toms and Cymbals (or Every Pad): tracks appear under it, one per group, each with its own fader, plugins and sends. Put a compressor on the kick without touching the hats. The groups: the snare with the side stick and clap; the closed, pedal and open hats; the four toms; the crash, ride and ride bell. The tambourine and cowbell stay on the drum track, and only pads that have a sound get a channel (up to 16). In the Drum Machine window, a pad's Output moves it: Drum Track (Main), a channel, or New Channel for This Pad. Soloing a channel plays just it; muting the drum track leaves its channels playing. Drum Channels > All Back to One Output undoes it; deleting a channel puts its drums back on the drum track. Each is one undo step, kept with the song. A drum track with channels can't be frozen (nor a channel on its own), and a channel bounces whole, not a range of it.

Slice a drum loop onto pads

Import a drum loop, right-click it > Slice at Hits > under Onto Drum Machine pads (up to 16), choose Fewer slices (the main hits), Normal or More slices (quieter hits too). A new track below, "<clip> Slices", gets a Drum Machine with a slice on each pad (Slice 1, Slice 2...) and a MIDI clip that plays the loop as it was; the original is muted. With more than 16 hits the strongest are kept, the weaker ones joining the slice before. The slices are taken as the clip plays (its gain, fades, reverse or warp) and saved as WAV files in the song's Audio folder. Move or quantise hits in the drum editor to rearrange it. Across Sampler keys puts a slice on each key from C1 instead (up to 92; good for riffs and vocal phrases). One undo step.

Add a sample to every kick or snare hit (drum replacement)

  1. Right-click the kick (or snare) mic's audio track header > Drum Replacement.... With a marquee range on the track, only that range is used.
  2. The picture shows every hit found at its level ("520 hits in 180 s, 40 quieter ones left out"). Adjust Sensitivity (higher finds quieter hits; 50% to start), Gap (5 to 150 ms, 30 to start: lower for fast double kick) and Leave out quieter than (-60 to 0 dB, or drag the line) so only real hits count, not bleed. Each hit's level is measured in its own drum's band (a kick's lows, a snare's crack), where another drum's bleed is far quieter, so the -24 dB it starts at leaves most bleed out and keeps ghost notes.
  3. Choose the Drum (guessed from the track's name: "Kick In" picks the kick) and kit, or Sample of Your Own... (WAV, AIFF or FLAC), and Blend under it or Replace it (which mutes the original and sets the Level to 0 dB; blended, it starts at -6). Line up with it (time and polarity) is on to start with; off, Flip polarity and Nudge (up to 10 ms either way) are yours to set.
  4. Make Trigger: a "<track> Trigger" track appears right under it, lined up in time and polarity, each hit as loud as the one it's under.

Solo the trigger to check every hit. Its track menu > Drum Replacement Settings... changes it later (Update Trigger finds the hits again: edits you made to the trigger's notes are lost). One undo step takes it all back.

A drum part for the song before the drummer's heard it (the session drummer)

Track > New Drummer Track adds a drummer: a track called "Drummer" at the end, a Drum Machine (with the Rock Kit) that plays a part for the whole song, following its sections (add section markers first, Track > Add Section Marker; with none it plays the whole song). Its window opens; press Play. Track > Drummer... (or the track header's menu > Drummer...) opens it again, for the selected drummer track or the first. It sets how it plays:

  • Style: Rock, Half-Time, Punk, Metal (double kick) or Blast Beat. A section can have its own (Drummer's style follows the drummer's).
  • Kick follows: a track whose rhythm the kick plays (its MIDI notes, or the hits in its audio), e.g. the bass; Nothing (the groove's kick) to start with.
  • For each section: Plays (off for a break), Simple - Busy (which groove and fill), Soft - Loud (how hard; from three-quarters up the hats move to the ride, in a blast beat to the crash) and Fill into it (None, Short: the last two beats, or Long: the whole bar before it). Double-click a slider for the middle. Sections start from their names: an intro softer and simpler, a verse a little quieter, a chorus or solo louder and busier with a long fill into it, an outro loud, a break silent.

Every change writes its part again (a MIDI clip per section) as one undo step; the same settings always give the same part. Write Again catches up after the song's sections, tempo or the followed track change. Turn into MIDI keeps the part as ordinary clips with every hit, to edit in the drum editor; if you edit the drummer's clips without doing that, it asks before writing over them ("Write the drummer's part again?"). Other clips you put on the track are left alone. The settings are kept with the song, by section (renaming a section keeps its settings). A Drum Machine track with nothing on it yet: its track menu > Make It the Drummer. Bars not in 4/4 get a plain beat for now. The assistant can do all of it ("a punk drummer, busy in the choruses, the kick on the bass").
