Tracks

Add an audio track

Click + Track at the top of the track headers (beside the feel and snap boxes), or Project > Add Track > Audio, CtrlAltNOptionCmdN. An audio track records from one input (a guitar, a mic) or holds audio files. The new track goes at the end, named "Audio 1", "Audio 2"..., with the next colour, and takes the first input no other track uses (or none: pick one in its input menu). Project > Add Track > Tracks for Unused Inputs adds a track for every input that hasn't got one, for recording a band. Adding a track is one undo step.

Rename, colour, reorder or delete tracks

  • Rename: double-click the track's name (or right-click the header > Rename).
  • Colour: right-click the header > Colour, one of eleven (blue first, grey last). Project > Colorize by Instrument colours every track, and gives it a picture, from what its name says it is (kick, bass, vocal...).
  • Reorder: drag a header up or down; a line shows where it goes. Several selected tracks move together. The same works in the mixer, dragging a strip by its name.
  • Delete: right-click the header > Delete Track, or select tracks and press Delete. Deleting and moving are each one undo step.
  • Select tracks: click a header; Shift-click or CtrlCmd-click adds or removes tracks. A right-click acts on the selected tracks when the one you click is among them, else on that one alone.
  • Height: Edit > Zoom > Zoom In Tracks / Zoom Out Tracks, Ctrl↓Cmd↓ / Ctrl↑Cmd↑: all tracks, in steps of 20 points, between 60 and 280.

Group tracks into a folder (a drum bus)

Select the tracks and choose Project > Group > Group into Folder, CtrlShiftDShiftCmdD, or right-click a header > Group Selected Tracks into Folder. The folder, named "Folder", takes the first track's place. A folder is also a group bus: everything inside plays through its fader, pan, mute and plugins (put the drum bus compressor there). Click its chevron to collapse or expand it (collapsed, it shows a mini row per track). Drag a track onto the middle of a folder's header to put it in, or onto its top or bottom edge to put it above or below. Right-click the folder > Ungroup Folder removes the folder and keeps the tracks where it was. Project > Group > Group into VCA is the other kind: a folder whose fader moves its tracks' levels without mixing them through it (see "Move several faders together"). Grouping and ungrouping are one undo step each.

Copy tracks with all their settings

Select track headers and Edit > Copy, CtrlCCmdC, then Edit > Paste, CtrlVCmdV: new tracks after the last selected one (in its folder, if it's in one; at the end with none selected), with the same input, plugins and their settings, automation, fader, pan, mute, sends and colour, but no clips. Edit > Paste Tracks with Clips, CtrlShiftVShiftCmdV, brings the clips too. Copying a folder brings its tracks. An input feeds one track at a time, so a copy only gets the input if no other track uses it. Each paste is one undo step.

Save and reuse a track setup (track templates)

Right-click a track's header > Save as Track Template... and name it: its plugins and their settings, automation, fader, sends, colour and input are saved, not its clips (a guitar track with your amp sim, a vocal chain). With several tracks selected (including the one you right-click), or a folder, they're saved together. In any song, Project > From Track Template adds them. Templates are kept with Take 5, not the song, so every song can use them. For whole-song setups, see "Start songs from a template".

Try different versions of a track (alternatives)

Right-click an audio or instrument track > Alternatives keeps several versions of a track's clips to switch between, on the same plugins, settings and automation (only the clips differ):

  • New Alternative (Empty) starts a blank one ("Alt 2"...) to record a different part.
  • Duplicate as New Alternative copies the current clips to edit.
  • Picking a name switches to it. The first is "Main".
  • Rename..., and Delete This Alternative (its clips go with it; greyed for the last one).

Once a track has two or more, its header shows which one is playing. Alternatives are saved with the song and undoable. A bandmate's version of a part arrives as an alternative named after them.

Show only some tracks in a big session (hide tracks, track sets)

The button at the bottom of the track headers (it says "All 75 tracks", or "14 of 75 tracks shown", lit while any are hidden) and Project > Show Tracks choose what's shown in the tracks and the mixer:

  • Show All Tracks brings every one back (also Project > Show All Tracks).
  • Under Show Only, the sets, each with how many tracks: your own, then ones made from the song: each folder's tracks, and tracks grouped by what their names say they are (Drums: kick, snare, toms, hats, overheads, rooms...; Bass, Guitars, Keys, Vocals, and Buses and FX; each only when two or more tracks fit). One click shows just those.
  • Show Only the Selected Tracks and Hide the Selected Tracks; or right-click a track > Hide Track.
  • Save What's Shown as a Set... names what's shown now (a name you've used replaces that set; "Drums" of your own wins over the one made from the names); Delete a Saved Set takes one away.

Hidden tracks still play and are in exports: hiding only tidies the view. What's hidden and your sets are kept in the song; showing and hiding aren't undo steps. Hiding a folder hides what's in it; showing a track in a folder shows the folder too.

Keep a track in view while scrolling (pin it)

Right-click a track > Pin to the Top (stays in view): it moves above the other tracks, under a line, and stays there while they scroll (a lead vocal while editing the band, a guide track). Unpin puts it back in its place. Pinning is kept in the song, and isn't an undo step.

Find what's eating the CPU (Audio Performance)

Click the CPU reading at the bottom right of the window, or Window > Panels > Audio Performance (F12), and play the song: a window lists each track's share of the audio's time (and the master's), the busiest first, with each of its plugins that uses any under it, and a bar for each (red when one takes a quarter of the time alone). It updates three times a second while playing; nothing is measured while the song is stopped. Double-click a plugin to open it; right-click it to Bypass It (or Turn It Back On), freeze its track or deactivate the track (see below).

Free up CPU by deactivating or freezing a track

  • Right-click a track > Deactivate Track: its plugins are unloaded (a plugin running in its own process is closed) and the track is silent, greyed out and left out of exports, its settings kept. Activate Track brings it back exactly as it was. Deactivating a folder takes its tracks with it. Undoable. Use it for a track you're not using now but want to keep.
  • Right-click > Freeze: the track's clips are recorded through its plugins as they sound and the plugins switched off; it still plays, from the recording. Its fader, pan and sends stay live (move them freely). The header says Frozen. If you change its clips or plugins it turns to an amber Changed: choose Freeze Again (it's changed since). Unfreeze brings the plugins back. Use it for a finished track with heavy plugins. Only audio and instrument tracks freeze; drum channel tracks (and a Drum Machine split into them) can't.

Write notes for a song or a track

Window > Panels > Notepad, CtrlAltPOptionCmdP, opens a side panel with the song's notes (lyrics, ideas, what's left to do) and the selected track's notes (mic, amp settings, which take to keep; the box waits until a track is selected). They're saved with the song as you type (not undo steps).

Arranging

Add and name sections (intro, verse, chorus)

Sections show in the sections lane under the ruler (Project > Marker Track, ShiftG). Put the playhead where a section starts and choose Project > Insert Marker, Insert: the marker goes at the nearest bar, named "Section 1", "Section 2"... Or right-click the sections lane > Add Section Marker Here (on the grid where you clicked). A section runs to the next marker; the last one to the end of the song. With the playhead where a section already starts, the status line says so and nothing is added. Double-click a section to rename it, or right-click > Name for Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Solo, Breakdown or Outro (Rename... for your own name, Add Comment Here... for a note at that point). Drag a marker's left edge to move it (it snaps to the grid). To jump from section to section, use Transport > Set Project Cursor Position > Locate Previous Marker / Next Section, ShiftB / ShiftN. Each kind of section has a colour of its own (the usual names all differ: a bridge never looks like the chorus), and every one of a kind looks the same ("Chorus 2", "Verse B" and a "Refrain" too).

Loop, duplicate or delete a whole section

Right-click a section in the sections lane:

  • Loop This Section sets the loop to it, turns looping on (and punch off) and puts the playhead at its start: for rehearsing or recording takes over just that part.
  • Duplicate Section repeats it right after itself on every track (a second chorus).
  • Delete Section (and Everything in It) removes it and closes the gap.
  • Remove Marker Only keeps the music and removes the marker.

The clips (cut at the section's edges), takes, automation on every track and the master, chords and lyrics go with the music: a duplicated section has its own copy of them, and after a deleted one they move up to close the gap. So do tempo and time-signature changes: a duplicated section starts at its own tempo and changes where it does, and after a deleted one the song goes on at the tempo that played after it. Each is one undo step.

Try the song in another order (arranger chains)

Project > Arranger Chains opens a window with the song's sections (from its markers) as buttons: click them in the order you want them played, a section as often as you like (a chorus twice) and leaving out the ones you don't (the bridge cut). The chain shows as a list, each section with its bars and where it starts in the chain: Move Up, Move Down, Remove and Clear change it. Play Chain plays the song in that order without changing it (the song's playhead jumps along to where each section is in the song; the button turns to Stop, and pressing Play on the song stops the chain). New, Duplicate, Rename... and Delete manage several chains to compare, and the drop-down switches between them; they're kept in the song, and each change is undoable. A section whose marker you delete drops out of the chains. Export as WAV... saves a chain's version as a file (in the song's exports, "<song> - <chain>.wav" to start with). Flatten into Song... (after asking) rewrites the song in the chain's order: every track's clips (cut at the sections' edges, with short fades there so the joins don't click), automation, markers, chords and lyrics. Sections the chain leaves out are gone; one undo takes it all back. Anything before the first marker is the section "Start". A song that changes tempo or time signature can't use chains yet.

Insert bars of silence

Press 2 for the Range Tool (or T and choose Marquee from the tool menu), drag a range as long as the gap you want, starting where the gap should go, and choose Edit > Functions > Insert Silence, CtrlShiftEShiftCmdE: everything from the range's start moves later by its length, on the tracks the range covers, their automation too (the gap holds the value it had there); a clip across the start is split there. With nothing selected, the status line says how. Over every track, it's the whole song that makes room: the section markers, tempo and time-signature changes, chords, lyrics and takes after it move too. Over some tracks, only those tracks move. Edit > Delete and Close Gap, ShiftBackspace, does the opposite: it takes the range out and pulls what's after it back (over every track, the whole song's). One undo step each.

Set the tempo and time signature

Double-click the tempo in the transport bar's display and type a new one (20 to 400 BPM); pick a time signature from the box next to it (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/8 or 12/8; the tempo lane has more). Both change the tempo or signature the playhead is in: the whole song, for a song with no changes, or that stretch of it (to add a change, use the tempo lane). MIDI stays on its beats; ordinary audio stays where it is in time, so a recording no longer lines up with the bars (audio clips set to follow the tempo are stretched with it). The display always shows the tempo and signature at the playhead. Each change is one undo step.

Tap the tempo

Transport > Tap Tempo, ShiftSpace: tap along at least twice and the tempo the playhead is in is set from your taps (the last eight, averaged). A pause of two seconds starts the count again. A run of taps is one undo step. The first tap only starts the count: the status line says to keep going. To set the tempo by typing, double-click the tempo in the transport bar. For a whole free-time take, see "Fit the tempo to a band take".

Add tempo changes and ramps (the tempo lane)

Project > Tempo Track, CtrlTCmdT, shows the lane under the ruler. Double-click (or right-click > Add Tempo Change Here) to add a tempo change; it starts at the tempo already playing there. Drag a point up or down for the tempo, in whole BPM (Shift: tenths), or sideways to move it (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid); a drag goes one way or the other, as it starts. A change can't be dragged past its neighbours, and the first one (the song's starting tempo) stays at the start. Double-click a point, or right-click > Set Tempo..., to type a tempo; AltOption-click it, or right-click > Remove This Change, to delete it. Right-click a point > Ramp to Next for a smooth speed-up or slow-down to the next change. Right-click or double-click the lane's top strip for a time signature from that bar (twelve to choose from), or to remove one.

Fit the tempo to a band take played without a click (Conduct Tempo)

  1. Choose Transport > Conduct Tempo (choose it again to stop without making anything).
  2. Play the take from the start and tap ShiftSpace on every beat (or every half bar, or bar), the first tap on a downbeat.
  3. Stop: say what each tap was ("Each tap was a beat", half a bar or a bar), then Make the Tempo (Keep Tapping to go on, Discard to throw the taps away). It needs two taps or more.

The bar lines now fall on the played downbeats, the tempo lane shows the speeding up and slowing down, and the audio hasn't moved, so the click and MIDI follow the band. The taps are corrected for the interface's delay, and the bars are in the song's starting time signature. One CtrlZCmdZ undoes it.

Find the tempo of a recording

Right-click an audio clip > Detect Tempo...: for a loop or a take played to a steady beat (six seconds or more), it suggests a tempo between 60 and 200 BPM, to a tenth of a beat per minute (or says none was found). It hears the beat from where the notes start, and the bar from how they repeat, so strummed eighths don't come out twice as fast; between two that fit equally well it picks the more usual tempo (a song at 70 could come out as 140). Choose Set Song Tempo to set the song's starting tempo to it, or Clip Follows Song to stretch the clip to the song's tempo instead. For a band take played without a click, see "Fit the tempo to a band take".

Make linked copies of a MIDI part or an audio clip

Select a clip (MIDI or audio) and choose Edit > Functions > Duplicate Linked, CtrlAltROptionCmdR, or right-click it > Duplicate Linked: a copy right after it, linked (shown by a link before its name).

  • MIDI: editing the notes of any copy changes them all.
  • Audio (the chorus guitar used three times): a fade, gain, pan, reverse, pitch, warp marker, envelope or clip effect on one is on all of them. Each keeps its own place, length, trim, name, colour and mute.

Right-click one > Unlink (it says how many others it's linked to) to vary it on its own. Plain Duplicate, CtrlDCmdD, makes an independent copy.
