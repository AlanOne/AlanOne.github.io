Editing audio

Select, move and copy clips

  • Click a clip to select it; Shift-click adds or removes clips. Drag across empty lane space to select clips on several tracks (Shift adds).
  • Drag a clip to move it, also onto another track: an outline shows where it lands. Several selected clips move together. The clip's start lands on the grid (a clip that was off the grid jumps onto it); hold CtrlCmd while dragging to ignore the grid. A clip can't go before the start of the song.
  • Edit > Copy / Cut / Paste, CtrlCCmdC / CtrlXCmdX / CtrlVCmdV: paste goes at the playhead, on the tracks the clips came from, and replaces what was there.
  • Edit > Select All, CtrlACmdA, selects every clip.
  • Edit > Delete, Backspace or Delete, removes them (selected tracks too).

A clip moved over another wins: the one underneath is trimmed back to a 10 ms crossfade (a MIDI clip cut exactly, with no crossfade), or removed if it's almost all covered.

Split a clip

  • Put the playhead where to cut, select the clip and Edit > Functions > Split at Cursor, AltXOptionX.
  • Or choose the Split Tool (3, or press T for the tool menu) and click the clip where to cut.

With Edit > Snap to Zero Crossing on (it is by default, and kept with the song), cuts and trims land where the waveform crosses zero (within 5 ms), so they don't click; each selected clip at its own crossing. Clips that are stretched, reversed, looped or pitch-shifted are cut exactly where asked. The two halves meet exactly, with no crossfade. With the Scissors, clicking a selected clip cuts all the selected clips there.

Trim the start or end of a clip

Drag a clip's left or right edge. Trimming follows the snap grid (hold CtrlCmd, to ignore it) and, with Edit > Snap to Zero Crossing on, lands on a zero crossing. Trimming only hides audio: drag the edge back out to get it back (as far as the recording goes; a clip can't be shorter than 10 ms). Trimmed over a neighbour, the trimmed clip wins and the neighbour is cut back to a 10 ms crossfade. The trim edges are the outer few pixels of a clip (a very narrow clip has none: zoom in). To slide the audio inside a clip without moving the clip, see "Nudge, repeat and slip clips".

Add fades and crossfades

Drag the fade handle at a clip's top-left or top-right corner (just under the name) to fade in or out. Right-click the clip > Fade In Shape / Fade Out Shape: Linear, Fast (convex), Slow (concave) or S-curve; choosing a shape on a clip without a fade gives it a beat-long one (at most a third of the clip). Fade drags don't snap, and a clip's fade in and fade out together can't be longer than the clip. Only audio clips have fades. Fade lengths can also be typed in the Inspector (I). Overlapping clips crossfade by themselves (10 ms; drawn as an X); drag the later clip's fade-in handle at the join to make the crossfade longer: it stays centred on the join, as long as there's audio either side, and the status line shows its length.

Change a clip's volume (clip gain) or normalise it

Right-click an audio clip > Gain: +6, +3, +1, -1, -3 or -6 dB, Normalise (to -0.1 dB) or Reset to 0 dB (the menu shows the gain now: "Gain (-3.0 dB)"). Normalise sets the gain from the loudest sample in the part the clip plays (fades and envelope aside), each selected clip on its own. Clip gain goes from -60 to +24 dB; type it, or drag it (-24 to +24), in the Inspector. The clip's gain shows in its name strip after the name. Settings in the clip menu apply to every selected audio clip (MIDI clips in the selection are left alone).

Draw a volume or pan envelope on a clip

Right-click an audio clip > Volume Envelope (or Pan Envelope): a line over the clip. Click the line to add a point, drag points to shape it (the status line shows the value; points don't snap and can't pass their neighbours), AltOption-click a point to remove it. Volume goes from silence up to +6 dB, on a scale like a fader's; pan has left at the top. The envelope moves and copies with the clip. The envelope commands act on the clip you right-click; Clear Envelopes removes both from every selected clip.

Nudge, repeat and slip clips

  • Edit > Functions > Nudge Left / Nudge Right, Ctrl←Cmd← / Ctrl→Cmd→: move the selected clips one grid step (10 ms with the grid off); never before the start. Their automation goes with them when Edit > Automation Follows Events is on.
  • Edit > Functions > Duplicate, CtrlDCmdD: a copy of the selection right after it (several clips keep their layout), selected, replacing what's there; press again for more.
  • Slip: AltShiftOptionShift-drag a clip to move the audio inside it while the clip stays put (not snapped, and only as far as the recording goes).
  • Loop: drag the right end of a clip's name strip (its top-right corner) to repeat the clip as a loop, on the grid; right-click > Unloop puts it back to one pass.

Use the snap grid, triplets and swing

The Snap box above the track headers ("Snap: Beat" to start with) sets the grid for moving, trimming and nudging: Off, Bar, Beat, 1/8, 1/16, or the triplets 1/4 T, 1/8 T, 1/16 T. Hold CtrlCmd while dragging to ignore it. The feel box next to it is Straight, Swing 54, 58, 62, 66 or 70% (on the Beat, 1/8 and 1/16 grids; bars and triplets don't swing), or a groove from a played part (right-click a MIDI clip, or an audio clip, > Use as Groove; it applies when its grid is the snap grid). Snapping and quantising follow the feel.

The lines in the tracks (and the ticks in the ruler) are the snap grid's own: a line at every place a drag can land, swing included, bars strongest, then beats, then the steps between them. Zoomed out, where they'd crowd together, only the beats are drawn, then only the bars. With the grid off, bars and beats show; on Bar, only bars.

Use the edit tools (pointer, marquee, scissors, glue, mute, zoom, draw, play)

Press T (Edit > Tools > Tool Menu) to choose what the mouse does in the tracks: Select Tool, 1 (select, move, trim and fade), Range Tool, 2 (select a range of time across tracks), Scissors Tool, 3 (click a clip to split it), Glue Tool, 4 (click the selected MIDI clips to join them), Mute Tool, 7 (click a clip to mute or unmute it; muted clips are dimmed), Zoom Tool, 6 (click to zoom in there, AltOption-click to zoom out, drag across what you want to fill the view), Draw Tool, 8 (drag across an empty part of an instrument track: a MIDI clip that long, on the grid) and Play Tool, 9 (hold the mouse down anywhere in the tracks: it plays from there until you let go, then goes back; not while recording). Fade work is an ordinary mouse gesture with the pointer. A tool acts on the clip you click, or on all the selected clips when that one is among them. The Glue Tool joins MIDI clips; for audio, use Audio > Bounce Selection.

Select a range across tracks and edit it

Choose the Range Tool (press T and pick it), then drag across one or more tracks; its edges snap to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, not to). Dragging a range deselects any selected clips, so the commands below act on the range alone. Its edges cut clips exactly (not at zero crossings). With a range:

  • Edit > Delete, Cut, Copy and Paste act on just that range.
  • Edit > Functions > Delete Time, ShiftBackspace: remove it and move what's after earlier (the tracks' automation too; over every track, the markers, tempo, chords and lyrics as well).
  • Edit > Functions > Insert Silence, CtrlShiftEShiftCmdE: push everything from the range's start later by its length (see "Insert bars of silence").
  • Transport > Locators to Selection, P: loop it.
  • Audio > Bounce Selection (CtrlBControlB): bounce just that range.
  • File > Export > Export Range as WAV...: what each track plays there, with its effects, one file per track.
  • Edit > Zoom > Zoom Full, ShiftF: fit it in view.
  • Drag inside it to move what's in it (clips cut at its edges, and its automation) to another place on the same tracks, snapped to the grid: what was where it lands is replaced, and the automation where it was holds the values either side. Hold AltOption to copy it instead (a razor edit).
  • Drag its right edge to stretch what's in it from its start: audio is time-stretched with its pitch kept (each clip's Stretch Mode; it stops following the tempo), MIDI notes spread out (or squeezed together), and the automation with them. Longer, it covers what came after on those tracks; shorter (down to 50 ms), it leaves a gap. Warped clips are left as they are.

Each is one undo step. Click an empty lane to clear the range.

Delete so the gap closes (ripple editing)

Turn on Edit > Ripple Edits: deleting (or cutting) clips moves the later clips on the track earlier to close the gap; their automation stays where it is. It's off whenever a song opens. Or use Edit > Functions > Delete Time, ShiftBackspace, once, on selected clips or a marquee range (a range takes its automation along).

Reverse, change channels or export a clip

Right-click an audio clip:

  • Reverse plays it backwards.
  • Channels: Stereo, Mono (Left + Right), Swap Left and Right, Left Only, Right Only.
  • Export as WAV... saves the clip as heard (gain, fades, envelope, reversed or warped, its clip effects with their tail), without the track's effects, as a 24-bit WAV in the song's Exports folder; with several clips selected, a file each in a folder.

Remove silence between phrases (Strip Silence)

Right-click an audio clip (a vocal, a tom track) > Strip Silence and choose Quiet recordings (-50 dB), Normal (-40 dB) or Loud or noisy (-30 dB): the quiet parts are cut out, leaving a clip for each phrase or hit. Gaps shorter than a quarter of a second are kept, and each piece keeps a moment before the sound (10 ms) and after it (80 ms), so nothing is clipped off; there are no fades. The status line says how many clips are left; one undo puts them all back. Clips that follow the tempo, play reversed or loop are skipped.

Make audio follow the song's tempo

Right-click an audio clip (a drum loop, say) > Follow Tempo: it stretches with the song's tempo and tempo changes, keeping its pitch, and stays locked to the beat. Its own tempo is taken to be the song's where it starts; for a loop recorded at another tempo, right-click it > Detect Tempo... > Clip Follows Song (see "Find the tempo of a recording"). Ordinary audio stays where it is in time when the tempo changes; MIDI always stays on its beats.

Right-click it > Stretch Mode for how it's stretched (following the tempo, warped or pitch shifted): Standard (all-round, the default), Percussive (the sharpest hits: drums, picked guitar) or Smooth (bass, vocals, pads: softer attacks). All three stay on the beat; stretched a long way (half speed, say) hits soften and smear in any of them.

Tighten the timing of a drum take (warp and audio quantise)

  • Select audio clips and press Q (Edit > Quantize), or right-click > Quantise Audio to Grid: their hits move all the way to the snap grid (1/16 with the grid off), with its swing or groove; hits less than half a millisecond off stay. Finding the hits can take a few seconds on a long clip. MIDI clips in the selection are left alone (quantise them in the MIDI editor). On clips in an edit group, Q opens Quantise Group instead (below).
  • For hand edits, right-click > Warp (it also turns on Follow Tempo): click in the clip's top strip to put a marker on the nearest hit, drag to pin the audio there and move it; drag a marker to move it (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid); AltOption-click a marker to remove it. Remove Warp Markers starts over.

Edit a multi-mic drum kit as one (edit groups)

Select the drum tracks (or their folder) and choose Project > Group > Group for Editing, CtrlShiftGShiftCmdG. Their headers show G1 (G2 for the next group). Now clicking a clip on one mic selects the clips at the same time on all of them, so a cut (AltXOptionX), move, nudge, delete or quantise does the same to the whole kit; trimming, fading or slipping one clip does the same to the others; a marquee range over one track covers the group; and comping a take on one mic (the takes lanes) comps the same take on the others. Edit > Edit Groups On turns grouping off for a moment (to fix one mic on its own; the badges go faint); Project > Group > Ungroup for Editing ends it.

Tighten a multi-mic drum take without phase problems (Quantise Group)

Quantising each drum mic on its own moves each by its own hits, so the overheads and rooms drift away from the close mics and the kit sounds smeared. With the kit grouped (above), select a clip on any mic and press Q (or Edit > Quantize Group...); to do only a section, drag a marquee over it first. In the window:

  • Tick the tracks to find the hits on: the kick and snare are ticked to start with (the inside kick and top snare mics).
  • Move them by: Cutting before each hit, crossfading the joins slices every mic 4 ms before each hit and moves the slices, with 6 ms crossfades: the hits stay exactly as played and the mics exactly in phase (the usual choice for drums). Warping (stretching between the hits) stretches the audio between the hits instead: no joins, the mics within a millisecond of each other.
  • Strength (100%: all the way to the grid) and Sensitivity (50% to start; higher finds ghost notes too), both remembered. Hits within 30 ms of each other on different mics count as one.

It uses the arrange view's grid and feel (swing, groove). One CtrlZCmdZ puts it all back.

Jump to the next drum hit (Opt+N)

Transport > Set Project Cursor Position > Locate Next Hitpoint, AltNOptionN, moves the playhead to the next hit (transient) on the selected tracks (or on the selected clips' tracks; with nothing selected, any track); AltBOptionB goes back to the one before. Handy for cutting at hits (AltXOptionX, splits there) and checking a quantise.

Slow down, speed up or transpose a clip

Right-click an audio clip:

  • Varispeed (tape: faster is higher): 50, 67, 75, 80, 90, 95, 100, 105, 110, 125, 133, 150 or 200%, like a tape machine; length and pitch change together. The clip's start stays and its end moves (check it doesn't now run over the next clip), and it stops following the tempo.
  • Pitch Shift (keeps the length): -12 to +12 semitones, in the clip's Stretch Mode.

Both apply to every selected clip.

Correct the pitch of a vocal

Double-click an audio clip (or right-click > Pitch Correction...) to open the Pitch editor under the tracks: sung notes as bars and the pitch as a line. Tick Correct: after a moment the corrected line sits on the notes of the key and the clip plays in tune, the voice still sounding like the singer. Choose which notes are in tune (Song key, Chromatic or Notes I choose), and set how far notes go (Correction, 100% to start), how quickly (Speed, 0 to 400 ms, 40 to start: 0 is the robotic effect, 100-200 ms keeps vibrato) and how much natural drift stays (Humanise, 30% to start). Recorded clips open it; imported ones too. Drag a note to send it to another note (hold AltOption, to move it in cents); double-click one to leave it as sung. Untick Correct to hear the original. The controls are down the left of the editor, beside the note names (across its top when it's too small). Scroll up and down through the pitches; AltOption-scroll shows more or fewer of them, the one under the pointer staying put.

Fix single notes in the Pitch editor (split, join, vibrato, timing)

Right-click a note in the Pitch editor:

  • Split the Note Here (a melisma or run found as one note, tuned to one pitch) or Join with the Next Note (a legato note found as two).
  • Octave Heard Wrong: the note's real pitch is an octave above or below what's shown.
  • Vibrato (none, half, as sung, more, or as Speed decides), Drift (straightened, half, as sung, or as Humanise decides) and Tilt (a rise or fall through the note, a half or a whole semitone): for that note only, whatever Speed and Humanise say.
  • Formants (bigger or smaller voice, -3 to +3) and Level (louder or quieter by 3 or 6 dB).
  • Leave This Note as Sung, Back to the Nearest Note in Tune, Reset This Note.

To move a note's timing, drag its start or end (the pointer shows arrows), or Shift-drag the note to move it whole: it snaps to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, not to). That's written as warp markers on the clip, the audio half a second either side pinned so only the note and the gaps beside it stretch.

Tune a screamed or gritty vocal

In the Pitch editor's More menu, Pitch Heard In says how readily pitch is heard: Screams Left Alone hears pitch only in clean singing, so a scream or growl isn't touched (no warbling); Clean Voice, Normal (the default), Gritty or Breathy Voice and Rough Voice hear it in rougher and rougher singing. To leave one stretch alone (a scream, a spoken line), drag across it in an empty part of the editor and right-click > Leave This Stretch Alone (Correct This Stretch Again undoes it). Leave Notes Already in Tune (Flex-Tune: Off, 10, 20, 30 or 50 cents) leaves notes that close to a note in tune where they are, so only the ones that are off move.

Formants, the chord lane, breaths and levels for a vocal

In the Pitch editor's More menu:

  • Formants (Whole Clip): the whole clip's voice bigger (-) or smaller (+), -4 to +4, without changing its pitch (a double that sounds like another singer).
  • Follow the Chord Lane (Chord Snap): each note goes to the nearest note of the chord playing there, not of the key (where the chord lane has chords).
  • Breaths and Esses: the breaths turned down 6, 10 or 15 dB (or breaths 10 dB and esses 4 dB), as dips in the clip's volume envelope (which you can then adjust).
  • Level the Notes: quiet notes up and loud ones down, Gently (half way) or Fully (all to one level), also in the envelope.
  • Correct the Track's Other Clips the Same Way: a comp's clips with the same settings (not the note-by-note changes).
  • Show Other Tracks Behind: the pitch of doubles and harmonies at the same time, faintly, to match them.

Write lyrics along the song (the lyrics lane)

Project > Lyrics Track shows a lane under the chords. Click a line to select it (it lights up); Shift-click another to select the lines between, CtrlCmd-click to add or take away one. Drag to move the selected lines together (on the grid; hold CtrlCmd, to ignore it). CtrlCCmdC, CtrlXCmdX and Delete copy, cut and delete the selected lines; CtrlVCmdV pastes them at the playhead, as far apart as they were (the words go on the system's clipboard too, to paste into a message). Right-click for Add a Line Here..., Change, Copy, Cut, Paste Here, Delete, Paste Lyrics from Here... (a whole song's words, a line each, a bar apart, to drag into place after), Clear All Lyrics, Lyrics Window... and Lyrics Prompter. Hyphens split a word into its syllables for the lead sheet ("beau-ti-ful").

Double-click the lane (or Project > Lyrics Lines...) for the Lyrics Lines window: every line with where it's sung (bar|beat and the time), the one being sung lit. Click a position or the words to type new ones: a position takes a bar ("17"), a bar and beat ("17.3") or a time ("1:23.5") and moves the line there; empty words take the line out. Add Line puts one where you double-clicked (or a bar after the selected line) with its words ready to type; Delete takes the selected lines out; Go To puts the playhead on the selected line; Paste Words... pastes a whole song's words from it. The rows selected there are the lane's selected lines too, so Copy and Paste work on them. While the song plays, the lane's header shows the first word of the line being sung. Project > Lyrics Prompter shows the words big, for the singer: the line being sung, the next under it, and a bar across for how far through the line the song is. It opens by itself when recording starts if the song has lyrics; put it on a screen the singer can see. On the lead sheet (Notation, and the printed and PDF parts) the lyrics go under the first part that isn't drums, a syllable a note. Lines transcribed from a vocal (see "Get the lyrics from a recorded vocal") also know when each word is sung: the lane draws each word where it's sung (when there's room), the prompter's bar goes word by word, and so does the lyrics video's lighting. Correcting a word keeps the words' times: a word put right or changed keeps its own, a word added gets one between its neighbours, and a line moved takes them with it. Lines copied and pasted, or typed, have none (their words are spread over the line).

Get the lyrics from a recorded vocal (Transcribe Lyrics)

Rather than type a song's words, have Take 5 hear them: right-click the vocal's audio clip > Transcribe Lyrics..., or select it and choose Audio > Transcribe Lyrics.... In the window:

  • Model: Standard (141 MB) is quick and good on a clear vocal; Better (465 MB) and Best (547 MB) get more of a sung vocal's words right (and put each word more exactly where it's sung), taking longer. Start with Standard; if too many words are wrong, undo and try Better.
  • Language: Find it from the vocal, or choose the language (a short or sung vocal can be taken for the wrong one). Nearly a hundred languages.
  • Separate the vocal first (the clip has the band in it): for a mixed song or a live take with one mic, the vocal is taken out of the mix first (as Separate Stems does, but nothing is added to the song), and only that is listened to. A vocal recorded on its own doesn't need it.
  • Replace the lyrics already there (on to start with): the lyrics lane's lines from the first word heard to the last are taken out first. Off, they stay and the new lines go between them (a line at the same beat is replaced).

Click Transcribe. The first time, the model is downloaded (from Hugging Face; with Separate the vocal first, the 4-stem separation model too, 84 MB) and kept in the Models folder (see "Where Take 5 keeps things"); after that it needs no internet. It runs on the computer and nothing is sent anywhere: a verse takes seconds, a whole song well under a minute with Standard on a recent computer, a few minutes with Best on an older one. Stop stops it, and nothing changes in the song. The words arrive in the lyrics lane (which opens if it was hidden) as one undo step: a line at each phrase (a new one after a pause, or after eight words or so), at the beat its first word is sung, each word at its own time. A line's last full stop or comma is left off; "[Music]", "(laughs)" and the like aren't words and are left out. Then check them: double-click the lyrics lane for the Lyrics Lines window and play the song, its line lit as it's sung, and click the words to put the wrong ones right (each keeps its time). With a sung vocal, expect some words wrong, more where it's quiet, sung low under the band or held long; the times are close to where each word starts (within a tenth of a second with Better and Best, a little further out with Standard). Add hyphens for the lead sheet's syllables ("beau-ti-ful") as you would for typed lyrics. The assistant can do it too ("write down the lyrics of this vocal").

Make a harmony from the lead vocal

Open the lead's clip in the Pitch editor and choose More > Make a Harmony Voice: a third, fifth or sixth above, or a third, fourth or octave below. The notes (as corrected) move that many notes of the key (or the chord, following the chord lane), keeping half the lead's own vibrato so it sounds like a second singer, onto a new track under the lead ("Lead Harmony (3rd above)"). Undo takes it away.

Tighten double-tracked guitars or stacked vocals (align to a reference take)

Pick the take the others should follow (usually the tightest). Select all the takes (Shift-click them), then right-click the reference > Align Selected to This Clip > Tight (100%), Natural (70%) (a little of each player's looseness kept) or Loose (40%). Recorded or imported audio clips only. The hits (palm mutes, picked notes, consonants) of each other take are paired with the reference's nearest, within 80 ms, and moved onto them with warp markers; between hits the audio is stretched, with the Percussive stretch mode so the attacks stay sharp. A hit with no partner nearby (a note only one take plays) is left where it is. Move Whole Clips (no stretching) only slides each clip by their typical difference, for takes recorded together that are just a little apart. It takes a few seconds per take; one undo takes it all back, and the warp markers can be moved by hand afterwards (see warping above). The clip's name strip says "warp" once it's aligned.

Match a double's pitch to the lead vocal

Select the doubles (or gang vocals) and the lead, right-click the lead > Match Pitch of Selected to This Clip > Tight, Natural or Loose. Each double is pulled toward the lead's pitch moment by moment, wherever they're singing the same note (within two semitones, so a harmony is left alone), keeping its own formants, so it still sounds like a second voice. The double plays a new file in the song's Audio folder ("matched" on its name strip); right-click it > Remove Pitch Match puts its own audio back. Do it after aligning the timing.

Take out a squeak, a click, hum or mouth clicks (Repair)

Right-click a recorded clip > Repair:

  • Remove Mouth Clicks: lip smacks and tongue clicks on a close-miked vocal, found and smoothed over (the voice around them left alone).
  • Remove Hum (50 Hz: Europe) / (60 Hz: the Americas): mains hum and its harmonics, in narrow notches.
  • Spectral Repair...: the clip's spectrogram (time across, pitch up, louder brighter). Drag a box over the sound to take out (a chair squeak, a pick click, a cough stands out brighter), then Remove Selected: each frequency in the box comes down to what it is just before and after it, so what stood out goes and what was under it stays.

Each makes a new file the clip plays (undoable); Take Off Repairs goes back to the original. The spectral window is titled "Spectral Repair" with the clip's name.

Split a song into vocals, drums, bass and the rest (Separate Stems)

For a mixed recording (a song you're learning or covering, a band's live take with one mic, a demo bounced to one file): right-click the audio clip > Separate Stems..., or select it and choose Audio > Separate Stems.... In the window:

  • Model: 4 stems: vocals, drums, bass and the rest (the cleanest), or 6 stems: guitar and piano apart too (the guitar comes out well; the piano is rougher, and the other stems a little less clean than with four).
  • Make tracks of: tick the stems you want (all to start with). Other is everything the others aren't: keys, strings, synths, effects, and with the 4-stem model the guitars too.
  • Mute the original (on to start with): the clip stays where it is, muted, so turning it back on compares.

Click Separate. The first time, the model is downloaded (84 MB for 4 stems, 55 MB for 6, from Hugging Face) and kept for next time in the Models folder (see "Where Take 5 keeps things"); after that it needs no internet. It runs on the computer, on as many cores as its memory allows (it can use up to half the memory while it runs): about a minute per minute of song on a recent computer, a few on an older one or one with 8 GB. The window shows how far it's got and the time left; Stop stops it, and nothing changes in the song. Each stem arrives on a new audio track under the clip's, named "<clip> Vocals", "<clip> Drums"... and coloured for what it is, with a clip exactly where the original is and as long: they line up sample for sample, so together they play the song again. The stems are the clip as it plays (its gain, fades, pitch or warp included), saved as WAV files in "<clip> Stems" in the song's Audio folder. One undo takes the tracks away and unmutes the original. The stems are good, not perfect: a little of the drums can be heard in the vocal and the other way round, more in a dense mix. The assistant can do it too ("take the vocal out of this").

Play along with a song with the guitar (or the vocal) taken out

  1. Bring the song in (drag the file onto the tracks) and set the song's tempo to it (right-click the clip > Detect Tempo... > Set Song Tempo), so the bars and click line up.
  2. Right-click it > Separate Stems..., choose 6 stems for a guitar, and Separate.
  3. Mute the stem you'll play (its track's M), or solo the others: the band plays on without it. For a vocal (karaoke, or singing it yourself), mute the Vocals track.
  4. To practise a hard part, loop it (drag in the ruler, or right-click its section > Loop This Section). To slow it down without changing the pitch, select every stem's clip, right-click > Follow Tempo, then lower the song's tempo (double-click it in the transport bar): the stems stretch with it (see "Make audio follow the song's tempo").

Play along through your amp sim on a track of your own and record yourself over it if you like. Just to learn it, File > Practice... does all this in one window, with the bars and chords shown (see "Learn a song from a recording").

Pull a riff, a bass line or a drum part out of a recording

Separate the recording (see "Split a song into vocals, drums, bass and the rest"), then work on the stem alone:

  • A bass line or a guitar or vocal melody as notes: right-click its stem > Melody to MIDI (see "Turn a sung or hummed melody into MIDI"); the Notation view shows it as tab on a bass or guitar part. It works best on a stem with one note at a time.
  • A drum part onto pads: right-click the Drums stem > Slice at Hits (see "Slice a drum loop onto pads"), or right-click its track's header > Drum Replacement... to play your own samples from its hits.
  • A remix: the stems are ordinary audio clips: cut, rearrange, put effects on them or take one out.

Move the playhead

  • Click anywhere in the lanes: on empty space, a clip, a folder, a section, a chord, a lyric or the tempo. The playhead goes there, snapped to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, for exactly where you clicked), and the click does what it does there too (a click on a clip still selects it). A click in the ruler puts it exactly where you clicked. Clicks (in the ruler too) don't move it while recording.
  • Or only by dragging it: turn off Edit > Preferences... > Editing > Locate When Clicked in Empty Space. Then a click in the tracks selects and edits as usual and leaves the playhead where it is; to move it, drag the playhead's line in the tracks (the pointer becomes a two-way arrow over it), and it stays wherever you let go (on the grid; hold CtrlCmd, for exactly there). A click in the ruler still moves it. Kept between launches.
  • From the keys (in Transport > Set Project Cursor Position, and To Project Start in Transport): - Num - / Num +: back or on a bar. Back from inside a bar goes to its start first. - Shift← / Shift→: back or on a grid step (a beat with the grid off). - Page Up / Page Down: back or on by a screen (the width of the arrange view). - ShiftB / ShiftN: the start of the section before or the next one. - Home or Num .: the start. End: the end of the song (where its last clip ends). - AltNOptionN / AltBOptionB: the next or previous drum hit (see "Jump to the next drum hit (AltNOptionN)").
  • When the playhead moves out of view (from a key, To Project Start, or the assistant), the arrange view scrolls to show it. While playing, it pages along as the playhead reaches the edge.
  • Another key set (Edit > Key Commands...) puts some of these on that set's keys: Classic, Take 5's keys before 1.0, moves the playhead a bar with the arrow keys and goes to the start with Return.

Follow the playhead while playing

Window > Autoscroll, F, on to start with: while the song plays, the tracks page along when the playhead reaches the right edge. Editing while it plays (moving or trimming a clip, dragging a marquee) or scrolling along the song yourself pauses it, so the view stays where you're working. It follows again on the next Play or Stop, or when you move the playhead (a click, a key). Zooming doesn't pause it. Turned off, the tracks stay where they are while playing.

Hear the audio as I drag the playhead (scrub)

Grab the playhead in the ruler (the pointer turns to a two-way arrow over it), or AltOption-drag anywhere in the ruler, and drag: the audio under it plays in tiny loops as it moves, to find a hit, a word or a click by ear. Let go and the playhead stays there (the song plays on if it was playing).

Put a clip at an exact place (spot)

Right-click a clip > Spot... and type where it starts: a bar (17), a bar and beat (17.3), with ticks (17.3.480, 960 a beat), or a time (1:23.5, 83.5s, 0:01:23.5). With several clips selected, the earliest goes there and the others keep their places relative to it (MIDI clips too). One undo step. Unlike a drag, it doesn't trim the clips it lands on: check for overlaps.

Zoom and scroll

  • Zoom: Edit > Zoom > Zoom In / Zoom Out, H / G, CtrlCmd-scroll, the scroll wheel over the ruler, or pinch on a trackpad (a Windows touchpad's pinch works too). It zooms around the pointer, or around the playhead when the pointer is near it, so the playhead stays put.
  • Drag up or down in the ruler to zoom (down zooms in), moving sideways in the same drag to scroll. A drag sideways sets the loop range as before. Double-click the ruler to zoom to fit, as ShiftF does (the range, the selected clips or the whole song; again to go back).
  • Scroll: the wheel scrolls through the tracks, Shift-scroll scrolls sideways along the song, and a trackpad's two fingers scroll both ways at once. Drag with the middle mouse button anywhere in the lanes to pan both ways, and in the piano roll, drum editor and Pitch editor too (along the song, and up or down the notes or pitches).
  • Taller or shorter tracks: AltOption-scroll or CtrlShiftCmdShift-scroll, as well as Edit > Zoom > Zoom In Tracks / Zoom Out Tracks. In the piano roll and the drum editor, the same makes the note rows taller or shorter, the row under the pointer staying where it is.
  • Edit > Zoom > Zoom Full, ShiftF: fit the marquee range, the selected clips or the whole song; press ShiftF again to go back.
  • Edit > Zoom > Undo Zoom, ShiftZ, goes back to how it was zoomed before, and again further back (each zoom is kept once it has settled, however many wheel or pinch moves made it); Edit > Zoom > Redo Zoom, AltShiftZOptionShiftZ, goes on again.
  • Each song opens zoomed and scrolled as it was when it was last closed (kept in Take 5's settings, not the song, so zooming never asks to save). The main window comes back at the size and place it was when Take 5 was quit, full screen if it was.

Bring in audio files

  • Drag audio (or MIDI) files from Finder or Explorer onto a track: a green ghost shows where it lands. A new track is named, coloured and given an icon from the file's name: "KICK_IN_01.wav" arrives as "Kick In", red, with a drum (drums red, bass purple, guitars orange, keys green, vocals blue...); stems from another studio arrive looking like a session (a name that says nothing about an instrument stays as it is). AAF and other DAWs' sessions get the colours and icons, keeping their track names. Project > Colorize by Instrument does it to the song's tracks now (keeping their names). Several files: the first on that track, the rest on new tracks below; hold AltOption to put them all on that track one after another.
  • Drop files between tracks, below the last one or into an empty song for new tracks there.
  • File > Import Audio or MIDI..., CtrlShiftIShiftCmdI: asks whether to place them at the playhead or at bar 1, on new tracks (one each) or one after another on the selected track, and for MIDI files whether to keep the song's tempo or use the file's.
  • Window > Panels > Media Browser, F5: browse folders, click a file to hear it, drag files in. Right-click a file > Tags... to tag it (kick, punchy, room: kept for every song) and the places list then has Tagged: kick...; right-click > Find Similar Sounds lists the sounds in the folder (and the folders in it) most like it, by how long, bright, sharp and noisy they are: a kick finds kicks, a hat finds hats, for drum replacement's samples. Click one to hear it.

Files are copied into the song's Audio folder.

Use my sample libraries (loops in the song's tempo and key)

Folders of loops and one-shots (bought sample packs, a sample service's downloads folder, a sample drive) can be the browser's libraries. In Window > Panels > Media Browser, open the places list and choose Add Sample Library..., then the folder. It's in the places list from then on as Library: <its name> (the download folders of the common sample services are found by themselves). The box under the list searches every library: type words from the samples' or packs' names, a tempo and a key, "funky bass 120 Am", and press Return: the samples with every word in their names, within 3 BPM of the tempo, and in the key or its relative (A minor finds C major too). Click one to hear it; selecting a file shows the tempo and key its name gives.

A loop dragged into the song from a library fits it: it follows the song's tempo (from the tempo in its name, "120bpm") and is transposed by the fewest semitones to the song's key (from the key in its name, "Am", when the song has a key: MIDI > Song Key); the status line says what it did ("in time from 95 BPM, up 2 semitones"). One-shots without a tempo in their name stay as they are (so does a file shorter than two beats at the tempo its name gives: "Kick 101" is a hit, not a loop at 101 BPM), and so do files from anywhere else. Right-click a file in a library > Remove "<name>" from the Libraries to take a library off the list (the files stay).
