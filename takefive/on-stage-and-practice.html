On stage and at practice

Play a gig with a setlist (stage mode)

File > Stage Mode... plays your songs one after another at a gig: backing tracks and samples to the PA, the click and cues only to the in-ears, one key (or a foot switch) for the next song. It opens on the setlist: Add Songs... (their .tracktionedit files) in the order you play them, Move Up and Move Down to change it, Remove to take one out. For the selected song:

  • Note: shown big on stage while it plays ("capo 2", "drop D", "Mike counts in").
  • Then: wait for the next-song key (the band talks to the crowd, tunes...) or start the next song after a gap in seconds (songs played straight through).
  • In-ears: this song's tracks that only the band should hear, by name with commas (a guide vocal).

Save... keeps the setlist as a .t5setlist file (the songs by where they are next to it, so a folder with the setlist and its songs can be copied to another computer); Open... opens one, and the last one opens by itself next time. A song that's been moved or deleted shows in red.

Start Show opens the first song (and the next one, so it starts at once) and switches to the stage view: the song's name big, its note, the section playing and the next one, the bar and tempo, and what's next. Space plays or stops; Return (or Right or Page Down) starts the song when it's waiting, and otherwise goes to the next song at once; Esc stops everything. Double-click a song in the setlist to start the show from it. Setlist goes back. The song open in the main window stops while the show runs, and comes back when it ends. Songs play as they were saved, from the start to the end (a loop saved on in a song is left off): the show changes nothing in them, and nothing is saved. Only the song up runs: the next one is opened but quiet until it starts, so it takes no processing and an armed track in it doesn't play your guitar a second time.

Send the click and cues to the in-ears, the rest to the PA

In File > Stage Mode..., choose In-ear output: the interface's output your in-ear (or headphone) mix is plugged into (outputs are pairs, as enabled in Settings > Audio Settings...). In the show the click, the spoken cues, every track named like a cue (Click, Cue, Guide, Count In) and each song's own In-ears tracks go there; everything else plays through the main outputs as usual, to the PA. With None, everything goes to the main outputs (a rehearsal with headphones, say), and there are no spoken cues. If the chosen output isn't on the interface that's plugged in (a setlist made with another one), the show says so as it starts: then there are no spoken cues either, and the click and in-ear tracks play through the main outputs, so check before the audience is in. The click plays as each song has it (on or off, its sound and count-in): set those in the song.

Hear each section's name before it comes (spoken cues)

With Say each section a bar before it on (in File > Stage Mode...), each section marker's name ("Chorus", "Bridge") is spoken a bar before the section starts, in the in-ears only (never through the PA: with no in-ear output there are none), so nobody loses their place. The computer's own voice says it the first time and it's kept in the Stage Cues folder in Take 5's settings folder (see "Where Take 5 keeps things"). To use your own voice instead, record a short file named after the section (Chorus.wav, Bridge.wav) into that folder: it's used in place of the computer's. Sections are the song's markers (Alt'Option' [Alt+'] adds one).

Change songs with a foot switch

A MIDI foot switch or any controller button can run the show. In File > Stage Mode..., click Learn Foot Switch and press the switch: it shows as "CC 64, channel 1" (or a note). In the show it plays a song that's waiting and starts the next song between songs. While a song plays, one press does nothing (so a stray step doesn't cut a song off): press twice within a second to skip to the next one.

Change my guitar rig's patch at each section (rig changes)

A guitar modeller, amp or pedal switcher that takes MIDI program changes can change patch by itself at the chorus, while you record and on stage. MIDI > Rig Changes..., Add Rig, then set:

  • Name: what you call it ("Floor unit", "Bass rig").
  • MIDI output: the output the unit is on (over USB it shows by the unit's name; else your interface's MIDI out with a cable to the unit's MIDI in) and the Channel the unit listens on (set on the unit).
  • Unit: how patches are named in the lists, to match what your unit shows: numbers (PC 1-128), banks of four (01A-32D), performances of five slots (P1 S1) or banks of eight (1A-16H).
  • Scenes: the controller your unit changes snapshots or scenes on, if you use them (CC 69 or CC 43, as its manual says).

The list has the song's start and each section marker. Select one and choose its Patch (and a Bank, for a unit with more than 128 patches, and a Scene); "-" leaves that as it is. Or click Learn and pick the patch on the unit itself: the program change it sends is set. Send Now sends the selected one to try it. While the song plays each change is sent as the playhead reaches its section, and whenever playing starts (from anywhere, before a count-in too) the patch in force there is sent first, so the unit is always right. A second guitarist's or the bass player's unit is another rig (Add Rig). Rig changes are kept with the song, each change an undo step. Stage mode sends a song's first patches as it gets ready to play.

Try ideas as loops in the clip launcher

View > Panels > Clip Launcher opens a grid: a column for each track, a row for each scene. Each slot holds a clip that loops. To fill one, select a clip on the timeline, right-click a slot and choose Copy Selected Clip Here (the clip stays on the timeline too); Clear Slot empties one. Click a clip to launch it: it starts on the next bar (Launch on sets that: 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars, a half, a quarter, an eighth, or At once), in time with whatever's playing, and the song starts playing if it wasn't. A clip about to start blinks; one playing is lit with a play sign. Click it again to stop it, or click an empty slot to stop that track. Each track plays one launcher clip at a time: launching another on it changes over at the next bar. A scene's button (at the left) launches its whole row at once; an empty slot in a scene stops its track. Right-click a scene's button to rename it. Add Scene adds a row, Stop All stops everything on the next bar.

While a track plays launcher clips it doesn't play its timeline (its name is lit); Back to Arrangement sends every track back to the song as it's arranged.

Make scenes from the song's sections

In View > Panels > Clip Launcher, Scenes from Sections makes a scene for each section of the song (its markers, Alt'Option' [Alt+'] adds one), named after it, each track's part of the section in the scene's slot: a MIDI track's notes in the section in one clip, an audio track's clip cut to the section. Launch Verse, then Chorus, then Verse again to try the song in another order, live. (Track > Arranger Chains does the same thing on the timeline, to keep.)

Record a jam into the song

In View > Panels > Clip Launcher, turn on Record Jam and launch clips and scenes as you like. Turn it off and what you played is written into the timeline: each clip where it played, looped for as long as it played, so the jam becomes an arrangement you can edit (one undo step). Every track goes back to the arrangement then.

Launch scenes with a foot switch

In View > Panels > Clip Launcher, click Learn Foot Switch and press a MIDI foot switch (or any controller button). Each press launches the next scene that has anything in it, from the one after the last scene launched, round to the first again.

Control Take 5 from my phone (phone remote)

When you're at the drum kit or the amp, across the room from the computer, a phone can be the remote. Transport > Phone Remote..., Start the Remote: the window shows an address (http://192.168.1.20: 8765/?code=k7m2xq, say) and a code. Type the address into the phone's browser (the phone on the same Wi-Fi as the computer). The page has big buttons: back to the start, Play (Stop while playing), Rec, the previous and next section, + Marker (a section marker at the playhead), and the song's tracks to arm and mute, with where the song is, its tempo and section. Everything the buttons do is as if you'd done it in Take 5 (with the count-in, if it's on), and each change can be undone there. Nothing can reach Take 5 until you start the remote, and only from this computer or its local network, with the code (a new one each time Take 5 opens; five wrong ones and that phone waits a minute), and from the remote's own page (another web page open in a browser can't send it commands). Stop the Remote turns it off; it keeps running with the window closed. On Windows, the first time you start it, Windows may ask whether Take 5 may use the network: allow it on private networks.

Print a chord chart of the song for the band

File > Export Other Formats > Chord Chart... makes a page to play from, with nothing to type: the song's title, key, tempo and time signature; its sections in order (from the section markers), each with how many bars it has; the chord lane's chords in those bars, four bars to a line, the chord on the beat it starts and a slash for each beat it carries on; under each section its lyrics (from the lyrics lane), each chord above the word it comes on; and the rig changes at each section (see "Change my guitar rig's patch at each section"). The page is drawn on the right as it will print: the type is as big as fits on one page, and a long song goes onto a second. Print... prints it (or saves it as a PDF from the print window); Save PDF... saves it to send round or read on a tablet. To change the chart, change the song (a marker's name, a chord in the chord lane) and open it again.

A chart for guitar, bass or drums

In File > Export Other Formats > Chord Chart..., For chooses who the chart's for:

  • The band: the chords and lyrics.
  • Guitar: a chord diagram for each chord in the song along the top (where to put the fingers, in the tuning of the song's first guitar part, e.g. Drop D; open strings as rings, strings not played as x, a barre where it needs one), then the chart.
  • Bass: the roots of the chords in the bars (A, F, C, G), the lyrics too.
  • Drums: the sections and their bars as slashes, an accent (>) where the chord changes off the first beat (where the band hits together), and the chords small for reference; no lyrics.

Save Every Part... saves all four as PDFs in a folder ("Song - Chart.pdf", "Song - Guitar Chart.pdf"...). Nashville numbers writes the chords as numbers in the key (1, 4, 5, 6m; b7 for a chord from outside it), so the chart works in any key. Lyrics with their chords leaves the lyrics out when it's off.

Play the song in another key, or with a capo

In File > Export Other Formats > Chord Chart..., Key transposes the chart: the chords for playing the song in another key (a singer's), up or down six semitones; the key on the page is the new one. With For set to Guitar, Capo puts a capo on a fret: the chart shows the chords as the shapes you play with the capo there (with Key a tone up and Capo on 2, the shapes stay those of the song's own key), and the diagrams are those shapes. The song itself doesn't change.

Print the setlist for a gig

In File > Export Other Formats > Chord Chart..., click Setlist... and choose a setlist saved by stage mode (a .t5setlist, see "Play a gig with a setlist"): a page in big print, a song to a line, numbered, with each song's key, tempo and guitar tuning (read from the songs) and its note ("capo 2"). It's saved as a PDF beside the setlist ("Friday - Setlist.pdf") and shown in Finder (Explorer on Windows), to print or put on a tablet. A song that can't be found is listed with just its name.

Record a whole rehearsal and find the songs in it (the rehearsal recorder)

File > Rehearsal Recorder... records a band practice without thinking about it, no song needed. Under Record, tick the interface's inputs to record: a stereo pair from a room mic (or the desk's two-track out), or every channel the band's plugged into (the ticks are remembered). Click Record Rehearsal: the time counts up big, a meter for each input, and how many hours of disk space are left. It records for as long as you play (hours is fine), to a folder of its own named by the date and time, written to disk as it goes in pieces of five minutes, so if the computer crashes only the last seconds are lost. Click Stop when you're done. Then it's cut into its songs by itself (a minute or so for a two-hour rehearsal): where the band plays is found by how loud it is against the room's own quiet (learnt from the recording), so the talk, tuning and noodling between songs are left out; a song has to last half a minute, and a break of up to six seconds in one keeps it one song. Each song becomes a take, its own WAV file, with its tempo and key found. They're listed under the rehearsal: stars, the song's name, its length, tempo, key and a note. Show talk between songs lists the stretches in between too. Closing the window while it records asks first: closing stops the recording (what's recorded is kept).

Name a rehearsal's takes from the setlist

In File > Rehearsal Recorder..., Name songs from > Setlist... chooses a setlist (a .t5setlist, as stage mode saves): the takes are named from its songs in order, the first song played after the setlist's first song and so on; takes past its end are "Take 4", "Take 5". None names them all Take 1, Take 2... To fix a name (a song played twice, one left out), select the take and click Rename... (or double-click its name): its file is renamed too. Cut Again... cuts the chosen rehearsal into songs again with the setlist chosen now; stars and notes stay with the songs of the same name.

Find how a song went at every rehearsal

In File > Rehearsal Recorder..., the rehearsals are listed newest first, each with its length and how many songs. Type a song's name in Find a song: its takes from every rehearsal are listed together, with the date of each, to hear how it's come on ("how did the bridge sound on Tuesday?"). Select a take and click Play (or press Space) to hear it; click it again to stop. Click a star to rate a take (the same star again: none), or press 1 to 5 (0 for none); Note... (or double-click the note) writes one ("the bridge finally worked"). Show in Finder (Explorer on Windows) opens the rehearsal's folder.

Turn a rehearsal take into a song, or send it round

Select a take in File > Rehearsal Recorder...:

  • Open as New Song starts a new song (after asking to save the open one) with the take on a track, at the take's tempo, so the bars line up: record the parts properly over it, or use it as a guide.
  • Add to This Song puts it on a new track in the open song at the playhead.
  • Share as MP3 saves it as an MP3 ("2026-10-31 Ballad.mp3") in the band's shared folder (see "Work on a song through a shared band folder"), for everyone to hear; with none it asks where, once.

Learn a song from a recording (the practice player)

File > Practice... opens the practice player, a window of its own for learning a song you have as a recording (a band's demo, a song you're covering, a lesson's backing track). Open... a recording (WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3 or OGG), or drop one on the window. It doesn't need a song open in Take 5 and changes none: what's playing in the main window stops while you practise. As it opens it finds the beats, the bars and the chords (a few seconds): the bars are numbered along the top of the waveform (bar 1 is the first full bar; anything before it is the pickup), and the chords are named under them. While it plays, the chord playing shows big, with the next one and how soon it comes, and the bar and time at the right, so "from bar 33" means the same to you and your teacher. The chords are the plain major and minor ones the music sounds like (a 7th or a sus shows as its major or minor), and a song without a steady beat (rubato, a free intro) gets no bars or chords.

  • Click the waveform to play from there; Space plays and stops; Home (or To Loop Start) goes back.
  • Speed: 50% to 125%, the pitch kept (double-click the slider for 100%).
  • Transpose: semitones (-12 to +12), and cents (-50 to +50) for a recording that isn't tuned to A 440. The speed is kept.

Everything you set for a recording (the speed, transpose and saved loops) is kept for it, and comes back the next time you open that file.

Loop the hard part and slow it down

In File > Practice..., drag across the waveform over the part (it snaps to the beats; hold CtrlCmd, not to), or double-click a bar's number to loop that bar. Loop turns it on and off (off, the loop stays drawn faintly, to turn on again). Turn Speed down to where you can play it, and press Space: it plays round and round. Count-in gives a bar of clicks before it starts, on the recording's own beats, the recording silent until the loop begins. Save Loop... names it ("Solo", "Bridge riff") and keeps it with the recording; the Loops list brings it back (with its bars), Delete forgets one.

Speed a loop up a little each time round

To get a solo up to speed: loop it, set Speed where you can play it cleanly (70%, say), and tick Speed up each pass, choosing how much (+1% to +5% a pass). Each time the loop comes round it plays that much faster, until 100%; the readout says which pass it is and at what speed. Save the loop to keep the setting with it.

Mute the guitar (or the vocal) of a recording and play along

In File > Practice..., click Separate Stems...: the recording is split into vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and the rest (as Separate Stems does on a clip; see "Split a song into vocals, drums, bass and the rest"). It takes a few minutes the first time (and downloads the 55 MB model if it hasn't been), and you can keep practising meanwhile; the stems are kept, so the next time the recording opens they're there. Then each part has a box under Parts: untick the guitar to mute it and play it yourself, and S solos a part (hear only the bass, to learn its line). The speed, transpose, loops and count-in work on the parts as on the recording.

Play along through the Amp, tune up first

In File > Practice..., Play along chooses the input your guitar, bass or mic is on: you hear yourself over the recording. Through the Amp puts Take 5's Amp on it (for a guitar or bass plugged straight into the interface); Amp... opens its settings. Tuner opens the tuner on that input (see "Tune my guitar or bass"). For a recording tuned down a half step, set Transpose to +1 to play it in standard tuning, or tune down and leave it.

Play in time with other apps and devices over Wi-Fi (Link)

Transport > Link keeps Take 5's tempo and beat in time with the other apps and devices on the same network that have Link (drum machine and looper apps on a phone or tablet, other music software, some hardware), with no cables or setup. Turn it on in each; Take 5 then plays in time with the others, its bars lined up with theirs, and nudges itself to stay there. Transport > Share Start and Stop with Link also starts and stops them when you play and stop (and Take 5 when they do).

The tempo goes both ways. A song with one tempo takes the tempo the others are at (once there are others: turning Link on with no one else leaves the song's tempo as it is), and when you change it they follow; each tempo taken from them is an undo step of its own. A song with tempo changes leads: as it plays through a change, the others follow it. The setting is for every song, and stage mode's songs follow it too. The status line says how many others are in time with it when you turn it on.

Let the song follow the drummer's tempo

When the band plays live with backing tracks or a click from Take 5, it can follow the drummer instead of the drummer following it. Transport > Follow the Drummer... lists the tracks that record from an input: tick the drum tracks to listen to (kick and snare mics work best; tracks named as drums are ticked to start with) and choose Up to, how far from the song's tempo the band may take it (6 BPM either way to start with). Press Follow and play: every half second Take 5 finds the tempo the drums are playing in the last few seconds and plays the song at that tempo (smoothly, never further than Up to from the song's own), so backing tracks and the click speed up and slow down with the band. The window shows the band's tempo and the song's. The song's own tempo isn't changed while following. To keep what the band played, Write the Band's Tempo writes it into the song's tempo, a change at each bar where it moved by more than half a BPM (one undo step). It finds the tempo, not the exact beat, so start together with the count-in. It can't follow while Link has another app in time with Take 5 (both would set the song's speed): switch Link off first.

What stage mode does to be safe on stage

In a show Take 5 doesn't autosave or show dialogs, and keys only do what the stage view says (Space, Return, Esc), not the main window's commands. With Low-Latency Mode on (the default) each song plays in Transport > Low-Latency Mode, for playing live through its plugins. A song that won't open (moved, deleted, damaged) is skipped and named in red on the stage view, and the show goes on with the next one.
