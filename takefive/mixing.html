Mixing

Open the mixer

View > Mixer, X, shows or hides the mixer; so does the × at the right of its title bar. Where it shows is up to you: View > Windows > Mixer in Place of Tracks, Mixer Below Tracks (drag the edge between them to resize) or Mixer in Own Window. With the mixer in place of the tracks, the window's close button closes the mixer first (the status bar says so); click it again to quit.

The strips are laid out as on a console: the track's name at the top, then its Inserts (plugin slots) and Sends, then pan and stereo width, the fader with its dB scale and meter, Ø (polarity), the level, arm, mute and solo, and the name again at the bottom. The fader stays a sensible height, so a taller mixer shows more slots rather than a longer fader; a short one (below the tracks) drops the headings and the fader shrinks to fit the slots. The master strip is on the right with Talk, Listen, Mono, Dim, Master Off and Mix / Ref.

See a track's settings in the Inspector

View > Inspector, I, shows a column at the left of the tracks (and of the mixer, when it's in their place) for the selected track. Click a track or a clip and it follows; with nothing selected it keeps the last track. Click a section's heading to fold it away (Take 5 remembers which); drag its right edge to make it wider (double-click the edge for the default width); the x in its title bar, or I again, closes it.

  • Clip (when a clip is selected): its name, Start, End and Length; for audio clips its Gain, Fade in and Fade out; Muted. Click a value to type a new one: Start takes a bar ("17"), a bar and beat ("17.3") or a time ("1:23.5") and moves the clip there; End trims the clip's end; fades take milliseconds ("250") or seconds ("1.5 s"). Several clips selected: the first is shown.
  • Track: its name, colour (click for the palette), Input, Output (the master or an output of the interface) and Notes.
  • Channel: the track's mixer strip, with its inserts, sends, pan, fader and buttons, and beside it the strip of where it goes (its folder, when the folder is a submix, or the master).

Every change is one undo step.

Set levels, pan and width

Drag a fader or pan knob; double-click it, or AltOption-click it, to go back to 0 dB or centre. Shift-drag moves it in fine steps, a tenth as far (pressing Shift partway through a drag works too). CtrlCmd-click any fader or knob to type an exact value ("-6", "L30", "120%"). These work on every knob and fader in Take 5. The width knob next to pan: fully left is mono, the middle (100%) leaves it as it is, fully right is wider. Undo puts fader, pan and send moves back, one step per drag. The mouse wheel over a track header's fader or pan scrolls the tracks rather than moving them, so scrolling never changes a level by accident.

Solo and mute tracks

  • M mutes, S solos. A soloed track keeps its reverb and delay buses.
  • AltOption-click an S: solo just this one.
  • Shift-click an S: dry solo, without the buses it sends to (the S shows D).
  • Right-click an S: Solo-Safe (stays audible whatever else is soloed; for a reverb bus), Unsolo All, Unmute All. Mix > Unsolo All and Unmute All do the same.

Add a reverb or delay on a bus (sends)

Track > Other New Tracks > New Bus adds a bus; put a Reverb or Delay on it. Then right-click a track > Send To and pick the bus (or New Bus...), or use the + send button on its mixer strip. Each send has its own level knob. Right-click a send > Pre-Fader (the fader doesn't change it) to make it independent of the track's fader, or Remove Send. Turn the plugin on the bus fully wet.

Real-sounding rooms, halls and plates (Convolution Reverb)

Add Built-in > Convolution Reverb (on a bus, as above; it starts fully wet). Space picks the room: Small Room, Studio Room, Drum Room, Chamber, Plate, Short Plate, Concert Hall or Cathedral. Each is made from a room's size and shape (its early reflections) and how long it rings in the lows, mids and highs, and they're all levelled alike, so switching spaces changes the sound, not the volume. Pre-delay holds the reverb back after the sound (clearer vocals), Length shortens it with a smooth fade, Low Cut and High Cut keep it out of the bass and take the fizz off, Width narrows or widens it, Mix sets wet against dry (lower it when it's on a track itself). Load IR... uses an impulse response of your own (WAV or AIFF): a recording of a real room, hall, plate or spring (free ones are on OpenAIR and similar sites; check each one's licence). It adds no latency, so it can be on a track you're recording through.

Make a knob move by itself (modulators: LFO, envelope follower, steps...)

Right-click any knob (a plugin setting, a fader's pan, a send in the mixer) > Modulate With > New LFO on <track> (or Envelope Follower, Steps, Random, Shape), or tick one the song already has. View > Panels > Modulators (or the track header's menu > Modulators...) shows the selected track's: a card for each, its settings, and what it moves, each with an amount (-100 to 100 %: how far at its full swing; negative turns it down). + Target... adds something more for it to move, on any track.

  • LFO: a wave (sine, triangle, saw, square, steps, random). Sync type Transport and Rate Type a note value (1/8, 1/4 T...) keep it in time with the song; Free uses Rate in Hz.
  • Envelope Follower: follows how loud its own track is. On the vocal, with the guitars' reverb send as a target at a negative amount, the reverb ducks while the vocal sings and comes back between lines. Gain sets how readily it moves; Attack and Release how quickly.
  • Steps: a pattern of steps (click or drag the bars), in time with the song.
  • Random: a new value each step, smoothed or not. Shape: a shape over each cycle.

A modulated knob shows a dot where the modulators have it now. Modulators are kept in the song, and their own settings can be automated like any other (their lanes are "LFO 1: Rate"...). x on a card takes one away; what it moved stays where it was.

Repeat a bar of automation (automation that loops)

Draw the pattern in an automation lane over a bar (or any range), select that range (marquee), then right-click the lane > Loop the Range to the End of the Song: the pattern repeats from the range's end to the song's end, replacing what was there. One undo takes it back. (For a pattern that stays editable in one place, use a Steps or Shape modulator instead.)

A few big knobs that move several settings at once (Smart Controls)

Select a track and press B (View > Panels > Smart Controls, or the track header's menu > Smart Controls...). Set Up Knobs adds ones to start with for what's on the track: Drive and Tone for the Amp (Drive turns the gain up and pulls the treble and level back, so it gets dirtier, not just louder), Squash and Brightness for the channel strip (Squash compresses harder and makes up for it, so it gets denser, not quieter), Space for a send. It measures what the track plays to work out how much Drive and Squash make up, so turned all the way up the track stays about as loud (Squash too with the compressor's Mix below 100 %, where its make-up is only partly heard): a few seconds for a long track. Or + Knob for your own (up to eight; double-click its name to rename it). Click a knob to see what it moves: each setting with where the knob at 0 and at 100 puts it (drag or type them; at 100 lower than at 0 turns it down as the knob goes up). Add Setting... gives it something else to move: any of the track's plugin settings, its fader, pan or sends; the x takes one off. A knob is automated like any setting (its lane is "Smart Control: Drive"), and right-clicking it maps it to a controller knob (MIDI Learn). They're kept in the song and in track templates. The settings' own knobs still work: the knob's range moves with them.

Move several faders together (VCA)

Select tracks and choose Track > Group > Group into VCA (or right-click > Group Selected Tracks into VCA). The VCA's fader turns all of them up or down by the same dB while their own faders stay where they are, and its mute mutes them all. Each track keeps its own output. Drag a track out of the VCA to free it; right-click the VCA > Ungroup VCA removes it.

Move selected tracks' faders, pans, mutes and sends together (linked channels)

Select several tracks (CtrlCmd-click or Shift-click their headers or mixer strips), then move one's fader: the others move by the same dB, keeping their balance. Their pans move by the same amount, a mute mutes them all, and a send moves the same bus's send on the others (on tracks that have one). It works while they're selected: no VCA to make. Mix > Link Selected Channels turns it off (and on again).

Compare versions of a mix (mixer snapshots)

Mix > Save Mix Snapshot... keeps the mix as it is under a name ("Vocal up", "Less verb"): every track's fader, pan, mute, solo, sends and plugin settings, bypasses included. Mix > Mix Snapshots lists them: choosing one puts that mix back, on the tracks and plugins still in the song (it says which aren't). The mix you had before is kept as "Before recalling", so you can go straight back. The song's clips and arrangement don't change (for the whole song, use File > Project History snapshots). Delete a Snapshot takes one away. They're kept in the song.

Give the mix an analog desk's sound (Console Mode)

Mix > Console Mode puts a Console on every track, folder and bus and first on the master: the gentle saturation an analog desk adds to each channel and the mix, rounding off peaks and adding a little warmth, louder signals more, quiet ones left as they are. Each Console's Drive sets how much (30% at first). Mix > Console Mode again takes them all off.

Add plugins to a track

Click the + in a track's plugin slots (in the header or the mixer strip) and choose from Built-in or Plugins (your scanned plugins). Your Favourites and the plugins you added lately (Recent, the last eight) are at the top of that menu: right-click a slot > Add to Favourites to put one there (Remove from Favourites takes it off). Click a plugin's slot to open its window. The light at the left of a slot says whether the plugin is on: green on, red off (bypassed); click the light to switch it (one undo step), in the track headers, the mixer and a Split's paths alike. Drag a slot to another place to change the order, or onto another track's slots (in the headers or the mixer) to move it there; hold AltOption as you let go to copy it, its settings with it. One undo step each. An effect dropped above a track's instrument goes just after it (before it, it'd get no sound). (The master's and a Split's plugins stay where they are.) Right-click a slot for Open, Add to Favourites, Bypass / Turn On, Load Preset, Compare: Switch to B (A/B), Sidechain From, Save Preset..., Run in Its Own Process and Remove. In a short track, Ctrl↑Cmd↑ makes room. In the mixer, a strip with more plugins than fit shows "N more".

Add a channel strip (gate, EQ, compressor)

Select a track and choose Mix > Add Channel Strip, or the + slot > Built-in > Channel Strip (Gate, EQ, Compressor): a Gate, EQ and Compressor go in before the fader, each with its own window. Click a slot to open its window: set the Gate's Threshold to shut out noise between notes, shape the tone with the EQ's handles, and even out the level with the Compressor's Threshold, Ratio and Makeup.

Know the built-in effects

The + slot > Built-in menu, grouped:

  • Guitar and bass: Amp (Clean, Crunch, High Gain and Bass voicings, gain, bass, mid, treble, presence, level, four cabinets or your own impulse response with Load IR...), Capture Player (NAM captures of real amps, pedals and rigs: see "Play a real amp or pedal (NAM captures and TONE3000)"), Overdrive (a tight boost: put it before the Amp to tighten a high-gain palm mute; Tight cuts the lows before it clips), Fuzz (Fuzz, Tone from dark to bright, Level), Octaver (one or two octaves below a single note, as an OC-2 does, mixed with the dry sound), Pitch Shifter (up or down by semitones: an octave down for a bass line), Hardware Insert.
  • Tone: EQ (drag the numbered handles on its curve), Dynamic EQ (bands that cut or boost only while their part of the spectrum is loud), Saturator, Low Pass.
  • Dynamics: Compressor (with a gain-reduction meter, sidechain, and Mix for parallel compression), Multiband Compressor (2-4 bands, each compressed on its own), Gate, Transient Shaper, Limiter (true-peak, for the master).
  • Vocals: De-esser, Level Rider (evens out a vocal before the compressor), Doubler, Live Tune (pitch correction as it plays: Key and Scale, Speed (0 is the robotic Auto-Tune sound) and Amount; put it on the armed vocal track to hear yourself in tune while recording).
  • Space and movement: Reverb, Convolution Reverb (rooms, halls, plates, or your own impulse response), Delay (tempo-synced note values, dotted and triplet, ping-pong), Chorus, Phaser, Flanger, Tremolo, Auto-Pan and Auto-Filter (a filter swept by an LFO, or by how hard you play: the auto-wah). Tremolo, Auto-Pan, Flanger and Auto-Filter follow the song when Sync is a note value (1/4, 1/8, 1/8 T...), pulsing on the beat from wherever it plays; Free uses Rate in Hz.

Plus Channel Strip (Gate, EQ, Compressor) and Split (paths side by side in one slot, each with plugins of its own).

Play a real amp or pedal (NAM captures and TONE3000)

Add Built-in > Capture Player to a guitar or bass track (where the Amp would go). It plays NAM captures (.nam files, A1 and A2 models): real amps, pedals and whole rigs captured with Neural Amp Modeler. Load Capture... lists the ones already on this computer, the last used first, with a search box (type part of a name or a maker; Return or a click loads it): every one you've loaded or downloaded from TONE3000, the ones in any song's Captures folder, and the ones in folders you add with Add Folder... (Folders shows them; click one to stop listing it). Other File... loads one from anywhere, and it's listed from then on. A capture loaded from elsewhere is copied into the song's Captures folder, so it goes wherever the song goes. TONE3000 reaches TONE3000 (tone3000.com), the free library of thousands of them:

  1. The first time, choose Set the TONE3000 Key...: sign in on tone3000.com, make a key in Settings > API Keys (it starts t3k_pub_), and paste it in. It's kept in Take 5's settings.
  2. TONE3000 > Pick a Tone on TONE3000... opens TONE3000's catalog in your browser (signing you in the first time): pick a tone and it comes back into Take 5, downloaded into the song's Captures folder. A tone with several models (gain settings, mics) offers the others in a menu.
  3. It plays at once. An amp head captured without its speaker gets a cabinet (Cabinet: one of the Amp's, or your own IR with Load IR..., listed and copied into the song the same way); an IR tone from TONE3000 becomes the cabinet.

Input drives it harder, Gate shuts out hum between notes, Tone tilts it darker or brighter, Output sets its level (captures are brought to about the same loudness first). The song opens with its captures without the internet. A song package (Share) carries your own .nam files, but a TONE3000 capture only as its link (its licence lets it be used, not passed on): on a bandmate's computer TONE3000 > Get It Again fetches it. Set Up Knobs gives it Drive and Tone. Ask the assistant for a tone from your TONE3000 favourites, downloads or the trending ones, and it can load it; TONE3000's free API doesn't let it search the whole library, so for anything else pick it in TONE3000's own catalog.

Use built-in plugin windows

In a built-in plugin's window, drag a knob up or down to turn it (Shift-drag: in fine steps), double-click or AltOption-click it to go back to its default, and type a value in the box under it (or CtrlCmd-click). The power button bypasses the plugin (grey when off). Some controls (Model, Cabinet, Note value) are menus. In the EQ, drag a numbered handle for that band's frequency and gain (Shift: frequency only), scroll over it for its width (Q), and double-click it to put its gain back to 0 dB. Plugins with bands (the Multiband Compressor, the Dynamic EQ) show a row per band; bands not in use are faint.

Squash only the part of the sound that's too loud (Multiband Compressor, Dynamic EQ)

For palm mutes that boom, a bass whose low end jumps around, or cymbals that get harsh, use the + slot > Built-in:

  • Multiband Compressor: the sound is split into 2-4 bands (Bands, with Split 1-3 where they meet) and each band has its own Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release and Gain; each row shows how much its band is being turned down. Listen plays one band alone while you set it. With nothing over the thresholds, it leaves the sound as it was.
  • Dynamic EQ: four bands (Low Shelf, Bell or High Shelf, or Off), each with a Frequency and Q, that move only while that part of the spectrum is over its Threshold, by the Ratio, up to the Range (below 0 cuts, above 0 boosts). Two start on: 250 Hz for low-mid boom and 3.5 kHz for harshness. Listens To: Sidechain makes a band follow another track instead (right-click the slot > Sidechain From): a vocal carving its own room in the guitars. The curve shows how far each band can go, and where they all are as it plays.

Process parts of the sound separately (Split)

The + slot > Built-in > Split puts paths side by side in one slot, each with plugins of its own, then mixes them back together. In its window, Split by:

  • Nothing: every path gets the whole sound (parallel processing): a clean and a distorted bass, or a squashed copy of the drums under the drums. 2-4 paths; two paths are 6 dB louder than one, so turn one down.
  • Frequency: each path gets a band (2-4, with Split 1-3 where they meet), and the bands add back up to the sound as it was: distort the bass's highs and keep its lows clean.
  • Mid and side: the mid (what's the same on both sides) and the side (what's different) each get a path, then are put back: widen only the sides, or EQ the middle on its own.
  • Left and right: one path each.

Each path has a Level knob (automatable), M (mute) and S (solo, to hear one band), and its plugins: + adds one, click opens it, right-click for the same menu as a track's slots. Plugins in a path are automated, MIDI-learned and mapped to smart controls like any other, and plugins with latency stay lined up with the other paths. Save the whole Split (paths and plugins) with Save Preset...; it's kept in track templates, and a copied track gets a Split of its own.

Compare two settings of a plugin (A/B)

Every plugin has two sets of settings to switch between while you set it up: in a built-in plugin's window, A | b switches to B (the first time, B starts as a copy of A, so change it from there) and back; A to B copies the set you're on to the other. For an installed plugin, right-click its slot > Compare: Switch to B and Compare: Copy A to B. Each switch is an undo step. The other set is kept while the song is open, not in the song.

Duck the bass with the kick (sidechain)

Put a Compressor (or another plugin with a sidechain input) on the bass, right-click its slot > Sidechain From and choose the kick track. The kick now drives the compressor, ducking the bass on every hit. None turns it off.

Automate volume, pan or a plugin

Click a track's A button to show its automation lanes (Volume, Pan, sends). To find what's already automated, press A (View > Automation > Next Track with Automation, or the A button under the track headers): it scrolls to the next track that has any automation and opens its lanes, the status line naming what's automated; again goes on to the one after, ShiftA back. While a track's automation is showing, touch any of its knobs or faders (in a plugin window, the mixer or the header; a third-party plugin's own knobs while its window is in front) and the lane shows that one, ready to draw; View > Automate Last Touched Control, AltAOptionA, opens the lane for the last one you touched even when the lanes are hidden. Click in a lane to add a point, drag points (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid), right-click or AltOption-click a point to delete it. Pick another parameter, including any plugin knob or send level, from the lane's menu. The mode box sets Read (play back), Touch (record while you hold a control, then return to what was there), Latch (record from the first move until stop) or Write. Ride a fader or plugin knob while playing to record it.

Draw automation shapes over a range

Select a marquee range (or clips), show the automation (A), right-click the lane > Draw Shape: Fade In, Fade Out, Swell, Duck or Sine, just in that range. Reverse and Invert work on the range too, and Copy Lane to Track puts the same automation on another track's matching parameter. With Edit > Edit Options > Automation Moves with Clips on, moving a clip takes the automation under it along.

Send a track to another output

Right-click a track > Output > an output pair (or Master, the main mix). Turn on more outputs in Settings > Audio Settings... first. Useful for outboard summing, a separate headphone feed or a second set of speakers.

Compare my mix with a reference track

Mix > Reference Track > Choose Reference Track... and pick a finished song: the mix's loudness is measured. Then Mix > Reference Track > Listen to Reference (A/B), AltROptionR, or Mix / Ref on the master strip, switches between your mix and the reference at the same place in the song. With Match Reference Loudness on, they play at the same loudness so louder doesn't sound better; Measure Mix Loudness Again after changing the mix. The reference also shows as a lane above the tracks (its waveform from the start of the song, its name in the header): click the lane to switch between it and the mix (View > Lanes > Reference Lane, AltShiftGShiftOptionG, hides it). Remove Reference Track stops using it. The reference never goes into an export. A reference file inside the song's folder is kept by where it is in the folder, so it's found when the song moves to another computer.

Check the mix in mono, at low volume, and on the analyser

  • Mix > Mono (or Mono on the master strip): hear the mix summed to mono.
  • Mix > Dim (or Dim): 20 dB quieter.
  • Mix > Master Off (Matched Loudness) (or Master Off): see below.
  • Mix > Analyser: the stereo correlation under one of four views, chosen at its top right: Spectrum (bass on the left), Spectrogram (the spectrum over the last seconds, louder brighter, the newest at the right), Vectorscope (where the stereo sits: mono is a line up the middle, wide stereo a round cloud, something out of phase a line across) and Level History (the level over the last minute, to see a chorus that doesn't lift).
  • The loudness readout in the transport bar shows LUFS SHORT (the last 3 seconds), LUFS INT (integrated) and the TRUE PEAK (red at -1 dBTP or above); click it to reset.

None of these change an export.

Correct my room or headphones (the listen bus)

Mix > Listen Bus... (or Listen on the mixer's master strip) holds plugins that only change what you hear from the speakers or headphones, such as room or headphone correction (Sonarworks SoundID Reference, for example). + Plugin adds one from your scanned plugins, On switches it off to compare, Edit opens its window and x takes it off. They come after everything else (Mono, Dim, the reference) and are never in an export, a bounce or a freeze; the meters show the mix without them. They're kept for this computer, not the song, with their settings. Listen on the master strip is lit while one is on.

Hear what the master's plugins do (Master Off)

Mix > Master Off (Matched Loudness), AltMOptionM, or Master Off on the master strip plays the mix without the plugins on the master (EQ, compressor, Limiter, whatever's there), turned up or down to the loudness it has through them. So you hear what the master does to the sound, not just that it's louder. The first time, and after any change to the song, it renders the mix twice to measure the loudness (over the loop if looping, else the whole song), which takes a few seconds; the status line says how much it turned the mix by. Press it again for the master back. It changes only what you hear (the click too): exports, the assistant's analysis and the saved song keep the master, and there's nothing to undo. A plugin added to the master while it's on isn't bypassed: switch it off and on again.

Bounce a track to audio (Bounce in Place)

Select tracks and choose Track > Bounce in Place (CtrlBControlB), or right-click a track > Bounce in Place: what they play, effects and all, becomes audio on new tracks and the originals are muted. With a marquee range on the track, Bounce Range in Place does just those bars. One undo step.

Plugins

Find my installed plugins (scan)

Settings > Plugins... scans for installed plugins: VST3, Audio Units and CLAP; VST3 and CLAP on Windows (from C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 and the usual CLAP folders). The scan runs in helper processes, so a plugin that crashes while being scanned doesn't take Take 5 with it. After scanning, effects appear under Plugins in the + slot menu and instruments under Track > New Instrument Track... > Plugins. Scan for Plugins... is also at the bottom of the + slot menu.

Save and load plugin presets

Right-click a plugin's slot > Save Preset... and give it a name; Load Preset lists the saved ones for that plugin. Works for built-in and third-party plugins, on top of the plugin's own presets. Presets are kept in Take 5's settings folder, so they're there in every song. To save a whole track's plugins and settings at once, use right-click the track > Save as Track Template....

A plugin crashed (amber light with a !)

Every third-party plugin runs in a helper process of its own ("Take 5 Plugin Host"), so when one crashes or hangs, the song keeps playing: the plugin's light turns amber with a "!", audio passes straight through it, and a message says what happened. Click the slot to reload it with its last settings, kept up to date in the background. Settings > Run Plugins in Their Own Processes turns this off for all plugins (they then load inside Take 5); right-click a slot > Run in Its Own Process sets it for one.

Use Intel-only plugins on an Apple silicon Mac

Intel-only plugins run under Rosetta in their own helper process: they're found by the scan and work like any other. Their slot menu says "Runs under Rosetta (an Intel-only plugin)". Nothing to set up: Rosetta must be installed (macOS offers it the first time an Intel app runs).

Put an effect on one clip only (clip effects)

For a reverb or echo throw on one word, de-essing one line, or another amp on one part, without automating the whole track: right-click the audio clip > Clip Effects > Add an Effect... and pick it, as for a track. It plays on that clip's audio only, before the track's own effects, and goes with the clip when it's moved, copied or split. Clip Effects lists the clip's effects: each has Open, Bypass and Remove. A clip with effects says "fx 1" (how many) on its name strip. The clip's fades are on its audio, before its effects, so a reverb or delay on it rings on past the clip's end.

Render a clip's effects into it, and take the render back

Right-click a clip with effects > Clip Effects > Render Into the Clip: the clip as it plays, its effects included (a reverb's tail too: the clip gets longer by as much as it rings on, up to the next clip on the track), becomes a new file in the song's Audio folder and a plain clip in its place ("rendered" on its name strip). It saves CPU, and frees the effect to edit the audio further. Clip Effects > Take Back the Render puts the clip back as it was, its effects playing live again, at any time, even after saving and reopening.

Use outboard gear on a track (Hardware Insert)

Add Built-in > Hardware Insert to a track. Choose the interface output your pedal or outboard unit is connected to and the input it comes back on, and press Measure so it stays in time with the other tracks. The track then plays through the gear; bypassing the insert plays it dry.
