Mixing

Open the mixer

View > Mixer, X, shows or hides the mixer; so does the × at the right of its title bar. Where it shows is up to you: View > Windows > Mixer in Place of Tracks (to start with), Mixer Below Tracks (drag the edge between them to resize) or Mixer in Own Window; it's remembered with the layout. With the mixer in place of the tracks, the window's close button closes the mixer first (the status bar says so); click it again to quit.

The strips are laid out as on a console: the track's name at the top, then its Inserts (plugin slots) and Sends, then pan and stereo width, the fader with its dB scale and meter, Ø (polarity), the level, arm, mute and solo, and the name again at the bottom. The fader stays a sensible height, so a taller mixer shows more slots rather than a longer fader; a short one (below the tracks) drops the headings and the fader shrinks to fit the slots; sends that don't fit are in the track's menu, the Inspector and the headers. Click a strip's name to select its track, drag it to move the track (into or out of a folder too). A VCA's strip has just its fader, name, mute and solo; a bus has no arm button. The master strip is on the right with Talk, Listen, Mono, Dim (20 dB down), Master Off and Mix / Ref; Mono and Dim change only what you hear, never an export. In the Console layout the strips also have Routing and Pre (see "Work in the Console layout").

See a track's settings in the Inspector

View > Inspector, I, shows a column at the left of the tracks (and of the mixer, when it's in their place) for the selected track. Click a track or a clip and it follows; with nothing selected it keeps the last track. Click a section's heading to fold it away (Take 5 remembers which); drag its right edge to make it wider (double-click the edge for the default width); the x in its title bar, or I again, closes it.

  • Clip (when a clip is selected): its Name, Start, End and Length; for audio clips its Gain (-24 to +24 dB; double-click for 0), Fade in and Fade out; Muted. Click a value to type a new one: Start takes a bar ("17"), a bar and beat ("17.3") or a time ("1:23.5") and moves the clip there; End trims the clip's end; fades take milliseconds ("250") or seconds ("1.5 s"). Several clips selected: the first is shown.
  • Track: its name, colour (click for the palette), Input (audio inputs, or MIDI inputs for an instrument track, or No input), Output (the master or an output of the interface; a bus has neither) and Notes.
  • Channel: the track's mixer strip, with its inserts, sends, pan, fader and buttons, and beside it the strip of where it goes (its folder, when the folder is a submix, or the master; none when it goes straight to an interface output).

Every change is one undo step (but notes, which are kept as you type).

Set levels, pan and width

Drag a fader or pan knob; double-click it, or AltOption-click it, to go back to 0 dB or centre. Shift-drag moves it in fine steps, a tenth as far (pressing Shift partway through a drag works too). CtrlCmd-click any fader or knob to type an exact value ("-6", "-inf", "L30", "C", "120%"). These work on every knob and fader in Take 5. The width knob next to pan (0 to 200%): fully left (0%) is mono, the middle (100%) leaves it as it is, fully right is twice as wide. Undo puts fader, pan and send moves back, one step per drag. The mouse wheel over a track header's fader or pan scrolls the tracks rather than moving them, so scrolling never changes a level by accident.

Solo and mute tracks

  • M mutes, S solos. A soloed track keeps its reverb and delay buses.
  • AltOption-click an S: solo just this one.
  • Shift-click an S: dry solo, without the buses it sends to (the S shows D).
  • Right-click an S for those (Solo, Solo Just This One, Dry Solo, Without Its Buses), Solo-Safe (Never Silenced by Another Solo) (for a reverb bus; its S stays half lit), Unsolo All and Unmute All. Mix > Unsolo All and Unmute All do the same.

Soloing a folder keeps the buses its tracks send to. Solos are kept in the song but aren't undo steps (press the S again).

Add a reverb or delay on a bus (sends)

Track > Other New Tracks > New Bus adds a bus ("Bus 1"...); put a Reverb or Delay on it. Then right-click a track > Send To and pick the bus (or New Bus...; a bus already sent to is ticked, and picking it again removes the send), or use the + Send button on its mixer strip. A new send starts at -10 dB, with a level knob of its own. Right-click a send's name > Pre-Fader (the fader doesn't change it) to make it independent of the track's fader (it shows "(pre)"), or Remove Send. Each is one undo step. On the bus the effect should be all effect, no dry sound (the track's own dry sound is already in the mix): a built-in Reverb or Delay added to a bus starts that way (Reverb's Dry Level all the way down, Delay's Mix at 100%), and Convolution Reverb starts fully wet anywhere. A third-party reverb on a bus: turn its mix fully wet.

Real-sounding rooms, halls and plates (Convolution Reverb)

Add Built-in > Convolution Reverb (on a bus, as above; it starts fully wet). Space picks the room: Small Room, Studio Room (to start with), Drum Room, Chamber, Plate, Short Plate, Concert Hall or Cathedral (or your own: Own IR). Each is made from a room's size and shape (its early reflections) and how long it rings in the lows, mids and highs, and they're all levelled alike, so switching spaces changes the sound, not the volume. Pre-delay (0 to 250 ms, 10 to start) holds the reverb back after the sound (clearer vocals), Length (10 to 100%) shortens it with a smooth fade, Low Cut (20 Hz to 1 kHz, 80 to start) and High Cut (1 to 20 kHz, 12 to start) keep it out of the bass and take the fizz off, Width (0 to 150%) narrows or widens it, Mix sets wet against dry (lower it when it's on a track itself). Load IR... uses an impulse response of your own (WAV or AIFF): a recording of a real room, hall, plate or spring (free ones are online; check each one's licence). It adds no latency, so it can be on a track you're recording through.

Make a knob move by itself (modulators: LFO, envelope follower, steps...)

Right-click any knob (a plugin setting, a fader's pan, a send in the mixer) > Modulate With > New LFO on <track> (or Envelope Follower, Steps, Random, Shape), or tick one the song already has. View > Panels > Modulators (or the track header's menu > Modulators...) opens a window with the selected track's: a card for each (+ LFO, + Envelope, + Steps, + Random, + Shape add more), its settings, and what it moves, each with an amount (-100 to 100 %: how far at its full swing; negative turns it down; +30% to start, -50% for an envelope follower). + Target... adds something more for it to move, on any track. Adding, ticking and removing are undo steps.

  • LFO: a wave (Sine, Triangle, Saw Up, Saw Down, Square, 4 or 8 Steps Up or Down, Random, Noise). Sync type Transport and Rate Type a note value (1/8 to start, 1/4 T...) keep it in time with the song; Free uses Rate in Hz.
  • Envelope Follower: follows how loud its own track is. On the vocal, with the guitars' reverb send as a target at a negative amount, the reverb ducks while the vocal sings and comes back between lines. Gain (12 dB to start) sets how readily it moves; Attack and Release (5 and 150 ms)

how quickly; Hold, Depth, Offset and its filters shape it further.

  • Steps: a pattern of 2 to 64 steps (8 sixteenths, rising, to start: click or drag the bars), in time with the song.
  • Random: a new value each step (1/8 to start), smoothed or not. Shape: a shape over each cycle (a bar to start).

A modulated knob shows a dot where the modulators have it now. Modulators are kept in the song, and their own settings can be automated like any other (their lanes are "LFO 1: Rate"...). x on a card takes one away; what it moved stays where it was.

Repeat a bar of automation (automation that loops)

Draw the pattern in an automation lane over a bar (or any range), select that range (marquee), then right-click the lane > Loop the Range to the End of the Song: the pattern repeats from the range's end to the song's end (where its last clip ends), replacing what was there; the status line says how many times. It's greyed without a range, or when the range reaches the end already. One undo takes it back. (For a pattern that stays editable in one place, use a Steps or Shape modulator instead.)

A few big knobs that move several settings at once (Smart Controls)

Select a track and press B (View > Panels > Smart Controls, or the track header's menu > Smart Controls...; a window of its own). Set Up Knobs adds ones to start with for what's on the track (audio and instrument tracks): Drive and Tone for the Amp or a Capture Player (Drive turns the gain up and pulls the treble and level back, so it gets dirtier, not just louder), Boost for an Overdrive, Fuzz for a Fuzz, Movement for a Tremolo, Auto-Pan, Flanger, Auto-Filter, Chorus or Phaser, Tune for Live Tune, Squash for the Compressor (it compresses harder and makes up for it, so it gets denser, not quieter), Brightness for an EQ (its high shelf), Space for the first send. Drive, Squash and Space start at 0, Tone and Brightness in the middle. One undo step. It measures what the track plays to work out how much Drive and Squash make up, so turned all the way up the track stays about as loud (Squash too with the compressor's Mix below 100 %, where its make-up is only partly heard): a few seconds for a long track. Or + Knob for your own (up to eight; double-click its name to rename it). Click a knob to see what it moves: each setting with where the knob at 0 and at 100 puts it (drag or type them; at 100 lower than at 0 turns it down as the knob goes up). Add Setting... gives it something else to move: any of the track's plugin settings, its fader, pan or sends; the x takes one off. A knob is automated like any setting (its lane is "Smart Control: Drive"), and right-clicking it maps it to a controller knob (MIDI Learn). They're kept in the song and in track templates. The settings' own knobs still work: the knob's range moves with them.

Move several faders together (VCA)

Select tracks and choose Track > Group > Group into VCA (or right-click > Group Selected Tracks into VCA). The VCA's fader turns all of them up or down by the same dB while their own faders stay where they are, and its mute mutes them all (all the way down silences them; VCAs inside VCAs add up). Post-fader sends follow it, pre-fader ones don't. Each track keeps its own output. Drag a track out of the VCA to free it; right-click the VCA > Ungroup VCA removes it and leaves the tracks where they are. One undo step each.

Move selected tracks' faders, pans, mutes and sends together (linked channels)

Select several tracks (CtrlCmd-click or Shift-click their headers or mixer strips), then move one's fader: the others move by the same dB, keeping their balance. Their pans move by the same amount, a mute mutes them all, and a send moves the same bus's send on the others (on tracks that have one). It works while they're selected (on any of them: move one that isn't selected and only it moves); solos aren't linked. No VCA to make. Mix > Link Selected Channels turns it off (and on again); on to start with, kept with the song.

Compare versions of a mix (mixer snapshots)

Mix > Save Mix Snapshot... keeps the mix as it is under a name ("Vocal up", "Less verb"; "Mix 1" to start; a name you've used replaces that one): every track's and the master's fader, pan, mute, solo, sends and plugin settings, bypasses included. Mix > Mix Snapshots lists them with when each was saved: choosing one puts that mix back (one undo step), on the tracks and plugins still in the song (it says which aren't). A plugin removed since isn't put back, and one added since is left as it is; a third-party plugin's settings may not undo. The mix you had before is kept as "Before recalling", so you can go straight back. The song's clips and arrangement don't change (for the whole song, use File > Project History snapshots). Delete a Snapshot takes one away. They're kept in the song.

Give the mix an analog desk's sound (Console Mode)

Mix > Console Mode puts a Console on every track, folder and bus and first on the master: the gentle saturation an analog desk adds to each channel and the mix, rounding off peaks and adding a little warmth, louder signals more, quiet ones left as they are. Each Console sits last before the fader, adds no delay, and its Drive (0 to 100%, 30 at first; 0 leaves the sound alone) sets how much. Mix > Console Mode again takes them all off, with the Drives you set. Tracks added afterwards don't get one: switch it off and on (every Drive goes back to 30%). One undo step.

Add plugins to a track

Click the + in a track's plugin slots (in the header or the mixer strip) and choose from Built-in or Plugins (your scanned effect plugins, by maker; Scan for Plugins... at the bottom). Your Favourites and the plugins you added lately (Recent, the last eight) are at the top of that menu: right-click a slot > Add to Favourites to put one there (Remove from Favourites takes it off). Click a plugin's slot to open its window. The light at the left of a slot says whether the plugin is on: green on, red off (bypassed); click the light to switch it (one undo step), in the track headers, the mixer and a Split's paths alike. Drag a slot to another place to change the order, or onto another track's slots (in the headers or the mixer) to move it there; hold AltOption as you let go to copy it, its settings with it. One undo step each. An effect dropped above a track's instrument goes just after it (before it, it'd get no sound). (The master's and a Split's plugins stay where they are.) Right-click a slot for Open, Add to Favourites, Bypass / Turn On, Load Preset, Compare: Switch to B and Copy A to B (A/B), Sidechain From (plugins with a sidechain input), Save Preset..., Run in Its Own Process (third-party plugins), Reload (after a crash) and Remove. Favourites and Recent are kept with Take 5, for every song. In a short track, Ctrl↑Cmd↑ makes room. In the mixer, a strip with more plugins than fit shows "N more".

Add a channel strip (gate, EQ, compressor)

Select a track and choose Mix > Add Channel Strip, or the + slot > Built-in > Channel Strip (Gate, EQ, Compressor): a Gate (its threshold low, at -60 dB, so nothing's cut until you raise it), EQ and Compressor go in before the fader, each with its own window (one undo step). Click a slot to open its window: set the Gate's Threshold to shut out noise between notes, shape the tone with the EQ's handles, and even out the level with the Compressor's Threshold, Ratio and Makeup.

Know the built-in effects

The + slot > Built-in menu, grouped:

  • Guitar and bass: Amp (Clean, Crunch, High Gain and Bass voicings, gain, bass, mid, treble and presence 0-10, level, a cabinet (4x12 British, 2x12 American, 1x12 Open Back, 4x10 Bass, off) or your own impulse response with Load IR...), Capture Player (NAM captures of real amps, pedals and rigs: see "Play a real amp or pedal (NAM captures and TONE3000)"), Overdrive (a tight boost: put it before the Amp to tighten a high-gain palm mute; Tight cuts the lows before it clips), Fuzz (Fuzz, Tone from dark to bright, Level), Octaver (one or two octaves below a single note, as an analogue octave pedal does, mixed with the dry sound), Pitch Shifter (up or down by up to 24 semitones: an octave down for a bass line), Hardware Insert.
  • Tone: EQ (drag the numbered handles on its curve), Dynamic EQ (bands that cut or boost only while their part of the spectrum is loud), Saturator, Low Pass.
  • Dynamics: Compressor (Threshold, Ratio, Knee, Attack, Release, Makeup, with a gain-reduction meter, sidechain, and Mix for parallel compression), Multiband Compressor (2-4 bands, each compressed on its own), Gate (Threshold, Range, Attack, Hold, Release), Transient Shaper (Attack and Sustain up or down), Limiter (Gain, Ceiling -1 dB to start, Release, true or sample peaks: for the master).
  • Vocals: De-esser (Frequency, Threshold, Range; split-band or wideband; Listen to the esses alone), Level Rider (evens out a vocal before the compressor: Target, Range, Speed and a Floor below which it leaves breaths alone), Doubler (Mix, Width, Detune, Delay), Live Tune (pitch correction as it plays: Key and Scale, Speed (0 is the robotic, hard-tuned sound) and Amount; put it on the armed vocal track to hear yourself in tune while recording).
  • Space and movement: Reverb, Convolution Reverb (rooms, halls, plates, or your own impulse response), Delay (tempo-synced note values, dotted and triplet, or milliseconds; Feedback, Mix 30% to start, 100% on a bus; High cut, ping-pong), Chorus (Rate, Depth, Width, Mix), Phaser (Rate, Depth in octaves, Feedback), Flanger, Tremolo, Auto-Pan and Auto-Filter (a filter swept by an LFO, or by how hard you play: the auto-wah). Tremolo, Auto-Pan, Flanger and Auto-Filter follow the song when Sync is a note value (1/4, 1/8, 1/8 T...), pulsing on the beat from wherever it plays; Free uses Rate in Hz.

Plus Channel Strip (Gate, EQ, Compressor) and Split (paths side by side: parallel, by frequency, mid/side). (The Console from Console Mode isn't in the menu.)

Play a real amp or pedal (NAM captures and TONE3000)

Add Built-in > Capture Player to a guitar or bass track (where the Amp would go). It plays NAM captures (.nam files, A1 and A2 models): real amps, pedals and whole rigs captured with Neural Amp Modeler. Load Capture... lists the ones already on this computer, the last used first, with a search box (type part of a name or a maker; Return or a click loads it): every one you've loaded or downloaded from TONE3000, the ones in any song's Captures folder, and the ones in folders you add with Add Folder... (Folders shows them; click one to stop listing it). Other File... loads one from anywhere, and it's listed from then on. A capture loaded from elsewhere is copied into the song's Captures folder, so it goes wherever the song goes. TONE3000 reaches TONE3000 (tone3000.com), the free library of thousands of them:

  1. The first time, choose Set the TONE3000 Key...: sign in on tone3000.com, make a key in Settings > API Keys (it starts t3k_pub_), and paste it in. It's kept in Take 5's settings.
  2. TONE3000 > Pick a Tone on TONE3000... opens TONE3000's catalog in your browser (signing you in the first time): pick a tone and it comes back into Take 5, downloaded into the song's Captures folder. A tone with several models (gain settings, mics) offers the others in a menu.
  3. It plays at once. An amp head captured without its speaker gets a cabinet (Cabinet: one of the Amp's, or your own IR with Load IR..., listed and copied into the song the same way); an IR tone from TONE3000 becomes the cabinet.

Input (±24 dB) drives it harder, Gate (off to start) shuts out hum between notes, Tone tilts it darker or brighter, Output sets its level (captures are brought to about the same loudness first). Only mono captures load; one made at another sample rate is converted (adding a little delay). Pick a Tone and copying into Captures need the song saved first. Each load is one undo step. The song opens with its captures without the internet. A song package (Share) carries your own .nam files, but a TONE3000 capture only as its link (its licence lets it be used, not passed on): on a bandmate's computer TONE3000 > Get It Again fetches it. Set Up Knobs gives it Drive and Tone. Ask the assistant for a tone from your TONE3000 favourites, downloads or the trending ones, and it can load it; TONE3000's free API doesn't let it search the whole library, so for anything else pick it in TONE3000's own catalog.

Use built-in plugin windows

In a built-in plugin's window, drag a knob up or down (or sideways) to turn it (Shift-drag: in fine steps), double-click or AltOption-click it to go back to its default, and type a value in the box under it (or CtrlCmd-click). The power button bypasses the plugin (grey when off), and A | b next to it compares two settings (see "Compare two settings of a plugin"). Right-click a knob for MIDI Learn... and Modulate With. Some controls (Model, Cabinet, Note value) are menus. Each move is one undo step. In the EQ, drag a numbered handle for that band's frequency and gain (Shift: frequency only), scroll over it for its width (Q), and double-click it to put its gain back to 0 dB. Plugins with bands (the Multiband Compressor, the Dynamic EQ) show a row per band; bands not in use are faint.

Squash only the part of the sound that's too loud (Multiband Compressor, Dynamic EQ)

For palm mutes that boom, a bass whose low end jumps around, or cymbals that get harsh, use the + slot > Built-in:

  • Multiband Compressor: the sound is split into 2-4 bands (Bands, with Split 1-3 where they meet) and each band has its own Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release and Gain; each row shows how much its band is being turned down. It starts with 3 bands split at 150 Hz and 2.5 kHz, each at -24 dB, 3:1. Listen plays one band alone while you set it. With nothing over the thresholds, it leaves the sound as it was. No added delay.
  • Dynamic EQ: four bands (Low Shelf, Bell or High Shelf, or Off), each with a Frequency and Q, that move only while that part of the spectrum is over its Threshold, by the Ratio, up to the Range (below 0 cuts, above 0 boosts). Two start on: 250 Hz for low-mid boom and 3.5 kHz for harshness. Listens To: Sidechain makes a band follow another track instead (right-click the slot > Sidechain From): a vocal carving its own room in the guitars. The curve shows how far each band can go, and where they all are as it plays.

Process parts of the sound separately (Split)

The + slot > Built-in > Split puts paths side by side in one slot, each with plugins of its own (two to start), then mixes them back together. A Split can't go inside a Split. In its window, Split by:

  • Nothing (the whole sound to each path): every path gets the whole sound (parallel processing): a clean and a distorted bass, or a squashed copy of the drums under the drums. 2-4 paths; two paths are 6 dB louder than one, so turn one down.
  • Frequency: each path gets a band (2-4, with Split 1-3 where they meet), and the bands add back up to the sound as it was: distort the bass's highs and keep its lows clean.
  • Mid and side: the mid (what's the same on both sides) and the side (what's different) each get a path, then are put back: widen only the sides, or EQ the middle on its own.
  • Left and right: one path each.

Fewer paths (or bands) removes the last ones with their plugins. Each path has a Level knob (-60 to +12 dB, automatable), M (mute) and S (solo, to hear one band), and its plugins: + adds one, click opens it, right-click for the same menu as a track's slots. Plugins in a path are automated, MIDI-learned and mapped to smart controls like any other, and plugins with latency stay lined up with the other paths. Save the whole Split (paths and plugins) with Save Preset...; it's kept in track templates, and a copied track gets a Split of its own.

Compare two settings of a plugin (A/B)

Every plugin has two sets of settings to switch between while you set it up: in a built-in plugin's window, A | b switches to B (the first time, B starts as a copy of A, so change it from there) and back (it then reads a | B); A to B (or B to A) copies the set you're on to the other. For an installed plugin, right-click its slot > Compare: Switch to B and Compare: Copy A to B. Each switch is an undo step (undoing one puts the settings back, though the button stays where it is); copying isn't. The other set is kept while the song is open, not in the song.

Duck the bass with the kick (sidechain)

Put a Compressor (or another plugin with a sidechain input) on the bass, right-click its slot > Sidechain From and choose the kick track. The kick now drives the compressor, ducking the bass on every hit. The list has every audio and instrument track but its own; the choice is kept with the song (one undo step). None turns it off. Plugins inside a Split's paths can't be sidechained.

Automate volume, pan or a plugin

Click a track's A button to show its automation lanes: one for each setting already automated, and one more whose drop-down picks any setting (Volume to start). To find what's already automated, press A (View > Automation > Next Track with Automation, or the A button under the track headers): it scrolls to the next track that has any automation and opens its lanes, the status line naming what's automated; again goes on to the one after, ShiftA back. While a track's automation is showing, touch any of its knobs or faders (in a plugin window, the mixer or the header; a third-party plugin's own knobs while its window is in front) and the lane shows that one, ready to draw; View > Automation > Automate Last Touched Control, AltAOptionA, opens the lane for the last one you touched even when the lanes are hidden. Click in a lane to add a point (on the grid), drag points (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid), right-click or AltOption-click a point to delete it. Pick another parameter, including any plugin knob or send level, from the bottom lane's drop-down; Clear in a lane's header deletes all of that setting's automation (one undo step). The mode box (the track's, Read to start with) sets Read (play back), Touch (record while you hold a control, then return to what was there), Latch (record from the first move until stop) or Write (from Play, every automated setting is written as it is, touched or not; then the track goes to Latch). Ride a fader or plugin knob while playing to record it. The header's R and W, and the Console layout's toolbar R and W (every track), set Read and Touch.

Draw automation shapes over a range

Select a marquee range (or clips), show the automation (A), right-click the lane > Draw Shape (greyed without a range): Fade In, Fade Out, Swell, Duck or Sine (once a beat), just in that range. Reverse and Invert work on the range (or the whole lane with none), Loop the Range to the End of the Song repeats it, and Copy Lane to Track puts the same automation on another track's matching parameter. With Edit > Edit Options > Automation Moves with Clips on (off to start, kept with the song), moving a clip takes the automation under it along.

Send a track to another output

Right-click a track > Output > an output pair (or Master, the main mix), or choose it in the Inspector's Output. Turn on more outputs in Settings > Audio Settings... first (the menu says so while there's only one pair). Kept with the song; one undo step. Folders have no output of their own. Useful for outboard summing, a separate headphone feed or a second set of speakers.

Compare my mix with a reference track

Mix > Reference Track > Choose Reference Track... and pick a finished song: its loudness and the whole mix's are measured. Then Mix > Reference Track > Listen to Reference (A/B), AltROptionR, or Mix / Ref on the master strip, switches between your mix and the reference at the same place in the song (the button then reads Reference, and the loudness readout and analyser measure the reference). With Match Reference Loudness on (it is to start with), the reference is turned down or up (by up to 12 dB) to the mix's loudness, so louder doesn't sound better; the mix itself is never changed; Measure Mix Loudness Again after changing the mix. The reference also shows as a lane above the tracks (its waveform from the start of the song, its name in the header): click the lane to switch between it and the mix (View > Lanes > Reference Lane, AltShiftGShiftOptionG, hides it). Remove Reference Track stops using it. The reference never goes into an export. A reference file inside the song's folder is kept by where it is in the folder, so it's found when the song moves to another computer; one elsewhere isn't copied into the song (put it in the song's folder to take it along).

Check the mix in mono, at low volume, and on the analyser

  • Mix > Mono (or Mono on the master strip): hear the mix summed to mono.
  • Mix > Dim (or Dim): 20 dB quieter.
  • Mix > Master Off (Matched Loudness) (or Master Off): see below.
  • Mix > Analyser: the mix before Mono, Dim and the listen bus, with the stereo correlation (-1 to +1, red below 0: parts cancelling in mono) under one of four views, chosen at its top right: Spectrum (bass on the left), Spectrogram (the spectrum over the last seconds, louder brighter, the newest at the right), Vectorscope (where the stereo sits: mono is a line up the middle, wide stereo a round cloud, something out of phase a line across) and Level History (the level over the last minute, to see a chorus that doesn't lift). It needs the song playing.
  • The loudness readout in the transport bar shows LUFS SHORT (the last 3 seconds), LUFS INT (integrated) and the TRUE PEAK (red at -1 dBTP or above), with the click when it's on; click it to reset.

None of these change an export. Mono and Dim are for this session only (not saved).

Correct my room or headphones (the listen bus)

Mix > Listen Bus... (or Listen on the mixer's master strip) holds plugins that only change what you hear from the speakers or headphones, such as room or headphone correction software. + Plugin adds one from your scanned third-party plugins (each in its own process), On switches it off to compare, Edit opens its window and x takes it off. They come after everything else (Mono, Dim, the reference) and are never in an export, a bounce or a freeze; the meters show the mix without them. They're kept for this computer, not the song, with their settings. Listen on the master strip is lit while one is on; the toolbar's L opens it too. It's on the main outputs only: a headphone mix or a track sent to another output doesn't go through it.

Hear what the master's plugins do (Master Off)

Mix > Master Off (Matched Loudness), AltMOptionM, or Master Off on the master strip plays the mix without the plugins on the master (EQ, compressor, Limiter, whatever's there), turned up or down to the loudness it has through them. So you hear what the master does to the sound, not just that it's louder. The first time, and after any change to the song, it renders the mix twice to measure the loudness (over the loop if looping, else the whole song), which takes a few seconds; the status line says how much it turned the mix by. Press it again for the master back. It changes only what you hear (the click too): exports, the assistant's analysis and the saved song keep the master, and there's nothing to undo. A plugin added to the master while it's on isn't bypassed: switch it off and on again. With no plugins on the master it has nothing to do (and says so); it matches by up to 24 dB.

Bounce a track to audio (Bounce in Place)

Select tracks and choose Track > Bounce in Place (CtrlBControlB), or right-click a track > Bounce in Place: what they play, effects and all (their tails too), becomes a 24-bit WAV in the song's Audio folder on new tracks, with the originals' fader, pan and sends, and the originals are muted. With a marquee range, Track > Bounce in Place (or Bounce Range in Place) does just those bars: the original's clips are cut at the range's edges and muted only inside it. One undo step.

Plugins

Find my installed plugins (scan)

Settings > Plugins... opens the Plugins window: its Options... button scans for installed plugins: VST3, Audio Units and CLAP (the CLAP folders in Library/Audio/Plug-Ins); VST3 and CLAP on Windows (from C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 and the Common Files\CLAP folders). The scan runs in helper processes, so a plugin that crashes while being scanned doesn't take Take 5 with it. After scanning, effects appear under Plugins in the + slot menu and instruments under Track > New Instrument Track... > Plugins. Scan for Plugins... is also at the bottom of the + slot menu.

Save and load plugin presets

Right-click a plugin's slot > Save Preset... and give it a name; Load Preset lists the saved ones for that plugin. Works for built-in and third-party plugins, on top of the plugin's own presets. Presets are kept in Take 5's settings folder, so they're there in every song (a plugin's VST3 and AU copies have their own lists). Load Preset is greyed while there are none. A Split's preset keeps its paths and their plugins. To save a whole track's plugins and settings at once, use right-click the track > Save as Track Template....

A plugin crashed (amber light with a !)

Every third-party plugin runs in a helper process of its own ("Take 5 Plugin Host"), so when one crashes or hangs, the song keeps playing: the plugin's light turns amber with a "!", audio passes straight through it, and a message says what happened. Click the slot to reload it with its last settings, kept up to date in the background (or right-click > Reload (it crashed)). Settings > Run Plugins in Their Own Processes (on to start with) turns this off for all plugins (they then load inside Take 5, and the song's plugins reload); right-click a third-party plugin's slot > Run in Its Own Process sets it for that plugin, wherever it's used (kept with Take 5).

Use Intel-only plugins on an Apple silicon Mac

Intel-only plugins run under Rosetta in their own helper process: they're found by the scan and work like any other. Their slot menu says "Runs under Rosetta (an Intel-only plugin)", and they always run in their own process (they need the Take 5 Plugin Host that comes with Take 5). Nothing to set up: Rosetta must be installed (macOS offers it the first time an Intel app runs).

Put an effect on one clip only (clip effects)

For a reverb or echo throw on one word, de-essing one line, or another amp on one part, without automating the whole track: right-click the audio clip > Clip Effects > Add an Effect... and pick it, as for a track. It plays on that clip's audio only, before the track's own effects, and goes with the clip when it's moved, copied or split. Clip Effects lists the clip's effects: each has Open, Bypass and Remove (the menu reads "Clip Effects (2)" with two). A clip with effects says "fx 1" (how many) on its name strip. The clip's fades are on its audio, before its effects, so a reverb or delay on it rings on past the clip's end.

Render a clip's effects into it, and take the render back

Right-click a clip with effects > Clip Effects > Render Into the Clip: the clip as it plays, its effects included (a reverb's tail too: the clip gets longer by as much as it rings on, up to 4 seconds and the next clip on the track), becomes a new file in the song's Audio folder and a plain clip in its place ("rendered" on its name strip). It saves CPU, and frees the effect to edit the audio further. Clip Effects > Take Back the Render puts the clip back as it was, its effects playing live again, at any time, even after saving and reopening. One undo step each.

Use outboard gear on a track (Hardware Insert)

Add Built-in > Guitar and bass > Hardware Insert to a track. Choose the interface output your pedal or outboard unit is connected to and the input it comes back on (it starts on the second output and the last input; with one output it waits for you to choose, so nothing feeds back), and press Measure so it stays in time with the other tracks: it plays clicks out and listens for them, so turn the gear's output down or measure with a cable first. The result (in ms and samples) is kept with the song. The track then plays through the gear; bypassing the insert plays it dry.
