Recording

Record a guitar or vocal

  1. Choose your interface in Settings > Audio Settings....
  2. Add an audio track (+ Track). It takes the first input no other track uses; pick another from the input menu in its header. An input feeds one track at a time: choosing it on a track takes it off the one that had it.
  3. Arm it with its R button. Its meter now shows the input level: play as loud as you will and check it stays clear of the top. Too hot clips the recording for good, so set the level on the interface's gain knob, not the track's fader (the fader changes what you hear, not what's recorded).
  4. Turn the click on (K, or the metronome button) and choose a Count-in in the transport bar. A new song starts with both on: the click, and a count-in of one bar.
  5. Press R (or the record button) to record, and Space (or R again) to stop.

The waveform draws as you record. The take is a 24-bit WAV file at the interface's sample rate, named after the track ("Vocal_Take_3.wav"), in the song's Audio folder, and the song is saved after each take: the status line says "Take recorded and saved" (or what went wrong). Recording over an old take replaces it where they overlap, but the old one is kept as a take (see "Comp the best parts of several takes"); MIDI on an instrument track works the same way. Recording over a clip that wasn't recorded (an imported file, or notes you drew) cuts it where they overlap, and only Undo brings that part back. If the song has lyrics, the Lyrics Prompter opens when recording starts. In Cubase's layout the status line shows the record format and how long you can record on the song's disk.

Hear myself while recording (input monitoring)

The headphones button in a track's header sets input monitoring. Click it to go through Auto (you hear the input while the track is armed, playing or stopped), On (always) and Off (never, for when the interface's own direct monitoring is on; the button is drawn faint). With monitoring on, the input goes through the track's plugins, so you can play through an amp sim. If what you hear is delayed, lower the buffer size or turn on Transport > Low-Latency Mode. Monitoring belongs to the input, not the track: it goes with the input to another track and to other songs, and is kept between launches. The button is greyed out while the track has no input. Hearing yourself twice, with a slight echo? Direct monitoring on the interface is on too: turn one of them off.

Record several inputs at once (a band)

Tick all the inputs in Settings > Audio Settings..., then Track > Other New Tracks > New Tracks for Unused Inputs to get a track for each, or set each track's input by hand. Arm them all: Shift-click an R arms the selected tracks too (and disarms them, when it disarms), CtrlCmd-click arms every track, and Track > Arm All Tracks / Disarm All Tracks do it from the menu (tracks without an input, and buses, are left out). Press R: each input records onto its own track, all starting at the same moment, so they stay in time with each other.

Record a stereo source on one track (stereo inputs)

Inputs are mono, one per track, which suits guitars and mics. For something stereo (an amp modeller's two outputs, keys, a pair of overheads), open an audio track's input menu and choose Make Input 1 + Input 2 One Stereo Input (or 3 + 4, and so on: always an odd one and the next, never 2 + 3): the pair becomes one input, "Input 1 + 2", that track records from it, and its takes are stereo files. Make Input 1 + 2 Two Mono Inputs in the same menu splits it again. The pairs are a setting of this computer, as the inputs are the interface's: a pair made stereo is stereo in every song, and another track that was on Input 1 or 2 has no input afterwards (the status line says which).

Use the metronome and count-in

  • Transport > Metronome, K, or the metronome button (in the transport, beside loop and punch) turns the click on or off. The Click slider at the left of the bar is its volume (it goes quiet but never silent: turn the click off for that).
  • Count-in (beside the Click slider): No count-in, 1 beat, 2 beats, 1 bar or 2 bars of clicks before recording starts (a bar as the song's first time signature has it). It plays only when you record from stopped: pressing R while playing starts recording at once.
  • Transport > Click Sound: Classic (the default), Woodblock, Beep, Cowbell, Hi-hat, Claves, Side Stick, Tambourine or Shaker; the first beat of the bar is higher or louder. The sound is the same in every song.
  • Transport > Click Only When Recording: no click on playback.
  • Transport > Click Output: send the click to another output (for example only the drummer's headphones) instead of the Main outputs.

The click's volume, output and Click Only When Recording are kept with the song. The click is never in an export.

Punch in to fix a part

  1. Drag in the ruler over the part to fix: that sets the punch (and loop) range, drawn in the ruler. The drag doesn't snap to the grid.
  2. Turn on the punch button (red) in the transport bar. Loop and punch can't be on together, so turning one on turns the other off.
  3. Arm the track, start playing before the range and press R (or start recording early): only the punched part is replaced; the rest of the old take stays, and the replaced part is kept as a take.

Transport > Pre-roll for Punch-in (Off, 1 bar, 2 bars) starts recording that many bars before the punch-in, wherever the playhead is, so you can play yourself in (a count-in plays before that). Without pre-roll, recording from past the range starts a second before the punch-in. The pre-roll is kept with the song.

Record several takes in a loop

Drag in the ruler to set a range (longer than 2 seconds: a shorter one doesn't record), turn on looping with Transport > Loop, C, or the loop button, arm the track and record. Recording always starts from the start of the loop. Each pass becomes a take of its own, on audio and instrument tracks alike; stop when you have enough. The track plays the last complete pass (a pass you stopped part way through is kept as a take too), and its Takes button shows them all for comping. Transport > Set Loop to Selection, CtrlUCmdU, loops over the selected clips or a marquee range instead (and turns punch off).

Keep something I played without recording (Capture Recording)

Take 5 keeps what you play even when not recording: a MIDI keyboard's notes, and the last minute of every input a track in the song uses. Select the track and choose Transport > Capture Recording, ShiftR: it appears as a clip named "Captured". On an instrument track you get the notes (not the sustain pedal or other controllers); on an audio track, what came in on its input: the last performance, from after its last pause of 4 seconds or more, saved in the song's Audio folder (it isn't a take). Played along with the song, it lands where you played it (for a loop, the last pass); played while stopped, it goes at the playhead with your timing. It replaces whatever is under it on the track. With no track selected, it goes on the armed track (an instrument track first), or the first instrument track for notes. Undo takes it away.

Play through plugins with less delay (low-latency mode)

Some plugins delay the sound to do their work (look-ahead limiters, linear-phase EQs and the like), and Take 5 delays everything else to match, so playing through them feels late. Transport > Low-Latency Mode switches off (bypasses) every plugin on the tracks and the master that delays the sound by more than 3 ms, so what you hear while playing is immediate; the status line says how many. Turn it off again to bring exactly those plugins back. It's kept with the song, so turn it off before mixing: while it's on, those plugins stay off for playback. An export always has every plugin on, Low-Latency Mode or not (its message says so). Plugins you add while it's on aren't switched off.

Tune my guitar or bass

Transport > Tuner, AltTOptionT, or right-click an audio track > Tuner... to tune on that track's input. Choose the input (its meter shows it's hearing you), the instrument (Guitar, 7-string guitar, 8-string guitar, Bass, 5-string bass, 6-string bass, or Chromatic for anything) and a tuning (standard, half or whole step down, drop D, C#, C or B, DADGAD, open D, E, G or C, and the 7- and 8-string and bass ones: drop A, drop E, bass drop D, BEAD, high C...). Play a string: the string is found by itself (or click one to tune only it; Auto goes back to finding it), its note and cents show, and the pill says Tune up, Tune down or In tune (within a cent); a string gets a tick once it's stayed in tune for half a second. Needle or Strobe switch the view: the strobe turns one way when the string is sharp and the other when it's flat, faster the further out, and stands still when it's in tune, which shows the last cent better than the needle. Capo (0 to 12 frets) and a reference pitch other than A = 440 Hz (415 to 466) are at the bottom; New Tuning... in the tuning list takes your own as notes ("D A D G B E"), and Delete This Tuning removes one. Mute speakers silences the song while tuning, until the tuner closes. The input, view and your tunings are kept between launches.

Reamp a DI through a real amp

Record a clean DI first, then play it out to a real amp later and record the amp:

  1. Right-click the DI track > Reamp.... Choose the output your reamp box is on (it starts on the DI track's output) and the input the amp's mic comes back in on.
  2. Press Measure once (with the amp turned down, or a cable from the output straight to the input): it measures how late the sound comes back, as Settings > Recording Latency... does, and keeps that for the input on this computer.
  3. Set Up Reamp: the DI track's output goes to the reamp output (so it leaves the main mix), and a "<track> Reamp" track appears under it, armed on the mic's input. Record it: the reamped take lines up with the DI to the sample.

For pedals or outboard gear on a track as it plays, use the Hardware Insert built-in effect instead.

Make a headphone mix for each player (cue mixes)

Turn on more outputs in Settings > Audio Settings... (for example outputs 3 and 4 for a headphone amp). Track > Other New Tracks > New Cue Mix... makes a mix for one player on an output of its own: give it a name ("Cue: Drums" to start with) and an output. It starts like the main mix (each track's send at its fader level) and is fed by pre-fader sends (marked "pre" in the mixer), so its levels change without touching the main mix, and the main mix's faders don't change it. Turn up what a player wants more of ("more me") on that cue mix's sends. Only the tracks there when it's made get a send: give a track added later one with its + Send. To send the click to only that output, use Transport > Click Output.

Talk to the band in their headphones (talkback)

Set up a mic at the computer once: Transport > Talkback Settings... has the Mic it's on (its meter shows it's the right one) and its Level in the headphones (-40 to +12 dB; double-click for 0 dB). Then Transport > Talkback, Y, Talk in that window or on the mixer's master strip turns it on until you press it again: the mic goes into every cue mix (headphone mix), never the speakers, the song or an export. While recording it's turned down 20 dB, so a word during a take doesn't drown the players (the button says so). It needs cue mixes on outputs of their own (see "Make a headphone mix for each player"), not shared with the main outputs; the window says which ones it reaches. The mic and level are kept for this computer; talkback itself starts off.

Takes and comping

See all the takes on a track

Once a track has a recording, its header gets a Takes button with the number of takes ("Takes 3"). Click it to show or hide the take lanes under the track: each take ("Take 1", "Take 2"...) on its own lane, wherever on the track it was recorded, the parts used in the comp shown bright. MIDI recordings get take lanes too, shown as notes. Whether a track's lanes show is kept with the song.

Comp the best parts of several takes

Show the take lanes with the track's Takes button. Drag across a take lane over the part you like: that part of that take goes into the track (the comp). The drag snaps to the grid; hold CtrlCmd while dragging to ignore it. Click a take lane to use the whole take. Do this for each phrase until the comp is right; each choice is undoable. Comped pieces are ordinary clips with short crossfades (10 ms; none between MIDI pieces), so you can trim or fade them like any other clip. Moving all of a comp's clips, even to another track, moves the takes with them. On tracks in an edit group (a drum kit's mics), choosing a take on one chooses it on all of them, so the kit stays in phase.

Delete a take I don't want

Hover over a take's name in the take lanes and click its x, or right-click the take > Delete Take (and the comp's pieces of it). Right-click > Use Whole Take puts all of it in the comp. Deleting a take also removes the pieces of it that were in the comp, so check the comp afterwards. CtrlZCmdZ brings it back. The take's file stays in the song's Audio folder (File > Clean Up Unused Audio... removes files nothing uses).

Turn a comp into one clip (Flatten Comp)

Right-click the track > Flatten Comp (greyed until the track has two or more clips): everything on the track, from its first clip's start to its last clip's end, becomes one clip named "Comp" that sounds the same, its audio a new file ("<track> Comp.wav") in the song's Audio folder. The track's plugins and fader stay on the track. The takes are still there to comp again, and CtrlZCmdZ puts the comp back. For MIDI, the comped clips are merged into one.

Put each take on its own track (Takes to Tracks)

Right-click a track with two or more takes > Takes to Tracks: a copy of the track for each take appears under it ("<track> - Take 2"), with the same plugins and fader but no input, each playing its take. The original keeps its comp. Useful for doubling a part, or comparing takes in the mixer.

Tidy MIDI takes automatically after recording

MIDI > Align MIDI Takes After Recording (off at first) gives each MIDI take a gentle quantise as soon as it's recorded, keeping the feel: notes close to the grid move 60% of the way to it (the snap grid, with its swing or groove; 1/64 with snapping off), the rest stay, and lengths don't change. Each pass is tidied before it's kept as a take, so choosing a take again from its lane gives the tidied notes too. The setting is saved with the song. Turn it off to keep takes exactly as played. To tidy a part by hand later, open it in the MIDI editor and use MIDI > Align Notes or the Quantise button.
