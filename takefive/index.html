Take 5 v0.17.2 · cheat sheet

Record / Edit / Mix / Master

Take 5 is an open-source music studio (a DAW) for Mac and Windows, made for bands and songwriters: record a whole band or yourself take by take, comp the best takes, edit audio and MIDI, write with built-in instruments, drum kits and a session drummer, mix with built-in effects, guitar amps and NAM captures of real gear, and master to streaming loudness against a reference track. Its layouts can work like Logic, Pro Tools, Cubase and others, and an assistant makes changes when you ask in plain words.

This sheet has the keys and steps to take a song from the first idea to a finished file. Keys are Take 5's default set (the Logic-style one). Single-letter keys work whenever you're not typing in a box, and almost everything can be undone with CtrlZCmdZ.

Before you start

Set up the sound once, learn a dozen keys, and know where everything is on screen.

Set up your audio interface

  1. Plug in the interface (and install its driver) before opening Take 5.
  2. Settings > Audio Settings… Ctrl,Cmd,: pick the driver type (ASIO on Windows), then the interface for input and output.
  3. Tick the inputs you'll record from and the outputs you'll listen on. Press Test to hear a tone.
  4. Buffer size: 128 samples for recording, 512–1024 for mixing a big song. Hear crackles? Go up one step.

Find any command

  • CtrlShiftPShiftCmdP Command Palette: type part of a name and press Return. Typos are forgiven; with no command, it finds the guide's tasks.
  • Ctrl/Cmd/ Shortcuts panel: every key and mouse gesture, beside the tracks.
  • Help > Tutorial: a guided tour on the open song.
  • Take 5 says when an update is available as it opens: Install Now gets it (on Windows it installs and opens again). Help > Check for Updates… (the Take 5 menu on the Mac) asks any time. About Take 5 says which version you have.
  • Hover any button for a tooltip; the status line at the bottom gives hints.
  • Prefer Pro Tools, Cubase, Reaper, Ableton or FL Studio keys? Settings > Key Commands… Cubase's whole window (toolbar, status and info lines, its track headers and MixConsole)? View > Cubase Layout.
  • A song from another computer, or a moved folder? Take 5 lists any files it can't find; File > Find Missing Files… finds them in a folder by name.

Get around the song

  • Click anywhere in the lanes (a clip, a section, a chord…) to move the playhead there, on the grid; the ruler puts it exactly where you click.
  • ← → a bar, Shift← Shift→ a grid step, Page Up Page Down a screen, Alt,Option, Alt.Option. a section, End the song's end. The view follows it.
  • Pinch or CtrlCmd-scroll to zoom (around the pointer, or the playhead when it's near); drag up or down in the ruler to zoom, double-click it to fit the song.
  • Two fingers scroll both ways; Shift-scroll goes along the song; middle-drag pans; AltOption-scroll makes tracks (or piano roll rows) taller or shorter.
  • While playing, the tracks follow the playhead (CtrlShiftFShiftCmdF turns it off); an edit or a scroll pauses it until the next Play.

Recording

Takes are saved in the song's Audio folder, and the song saves itself after every take.

Record a guitar or a vocal

  1. Add an audio track: + Track or CtrlAltNOptionCmdN.
  2. Pick its input in the track header's input menu.
  3. Click the track's R to arm it. Play: the meter should move without hitting the top.
  4. Press K for the click and choose a Count-in (1 bar is a good start).
  5. Press R to record, Space to stop.
  6. Recorded over an old take? It's kept as a take, not lost.

Hear yourself while recording

Click the headphones button in the track header to cycle:

  • Auto: you hear the input while the track is armed.
  • On: always. Off: never (use when your interface's direct monitoring is on).

You hear the input through the track's plugins, so you can play through the Amp. Hear a delay? Lower the buffer, or turn on Transport > Low-Latency Mode.

Record a whole band at once

  1. Tick every input in Audio Settings.
  2. Track > New Tracks for Unused Inputs makes a track for each.
  3. Arm them: Shift-click an R arms the selected tracks; CtrlCmd-click an R arms every track.
  4. Press R: each input records onto its own track.

Headphone mixes per player: Track > New Cue Mix…. Talk to them with Y (the mic is set in Transport > Talkback Settings…; it's turned down while recording).

A stereo source (amp modeller, keys, overheads) on one track: its input menu > Make Input 1 + Input 2 One Stereo Input.

Fix one part (punch in)

  1. Drag in the ruler over the bars to fix.
  2. Turn on the punch button (it goes red).
  3. Arm the track, start before the bars, press R. Only the punched part is replaced.

Transport > Pre-roll for Punch-in plays 1 or 2 bars first so you can play yourself in.

Lyrics for the singer

  1. View > Lyrics Lane, right-click it > Paste Lyrics from Here..., then drag each line to where it's sung.
  2. Click a line to select it, CtrlCCmdC and CtrlVCmdV to copy it to the playhead (a chorus's lines, say). Double-click the lane for every line and where it's sung, to type new words and positions.
  3. Record: the Lyrics window opens by itself, the line big, the next under it (View > Lyrics Prompter any time).

Loop-record takes, then comp the best

  1. Drag in the ruler to set the range, press C to loop.
  2. Arm and press R. Every pass becomes a take. Stop when you have enough.
  3. Click the track's Takes button to show the take lanes.
  4. Drag across the best part of each take: it goes into the comp. Click a lane to use a whole take.
  5. Right-click the track > Flatten Comp to make it one clip.

Guitar and bass tone

  • Record the DI and play through + > Built-in > Amp: change the tone later without playing again.
  • A real amp or pedal: Built-in > Capture Player, Load Capture... (the NAM captures on this computer, with a search box; Other File... for a .nam anywhere, copied into the song) or TONE3000 to pick one from tone3000.com.
  • Pedals before it: Overdrive (tightens high gain), Fuzz, Octaver, Pitch Shifter.
  • Through your real amp later: right-click the DI track > Reamp..., Measure, Set Up Reamp, record: it lines up to the sample.

Sing in tune while you record: Live Tune on the armed vocal track.

Click, count-in and tuning

  • K click on/off; the slider beside it is the click volume.
  • Transport > Click Sound: woodblock, cowbell, hi-hat, beep…
  • Transport > Click Only When Recording.
  • Transport > Click Output: send the click only to the drummer's headphones.
  • AltTOptionT Tuner: guitar, bass or chromatic, many tunings.

The click is never in an export.

Editing

Tighten, tune and clean up what was recorded. Every edit is one undo step, and the recordings themselves are never changed.

Tighten a multi-mic drum take

  1. Select the drum tracks (or their folder), CtrlShiftGShiftCmdG Group for Editing: a clip on one mic now selects the whole kit (cuts, moves, fades and comps go to every mic).
  2. Drag a marquee over a section (or select a clip), press Q: Quantise Group finds the hits on the kick and snare and cuts every mic at the same times, so the overheads and rooms stay in phase.
  3. Tab / ShiftTab jump the playhead from hit to hit to check it.
  4. Drums that follow the tempo or are warped: right-click > Stretch Mode > Percussive keeps the hits sharpest.

Tighten doubled guitars or vocals

  1. Select all the takes, right-click the tightest > Align Selected to This Clip > Tight or Natural: their hits move onto its hits.
  2. A double vocal: right-click the lead > Match Pitch of Selected to This Clip.

Tune a vocal

  1. Double-click the vocal clip: the Pitch editor opens under the tracks. Tick Correct.
  2. Drag a note to move it (AltOption-drag: in cents); drag its start or end to move its timing.
  3. Right-click a note: split, join, vibrato, drift, tilt, formants, level.
  4. More: screams left alone, breaths down, levelling, follow the chord lane, a harmony voice.

Move, copy or stretch a range

  1. T > Marquee Tool, drag across the bars and tracks.
  2. Drag inside it to move what's there (clips cut at its edges, automation too); AltOption-drag copies.
  3. Drag its right edge to stretch it: audio keeps its pitch, MIDI notes spread out.
  4. ShiftBackspace deletes it and closes the gap; Edit > Insert Silence opens one.

Find the exact spot

  • Drag the playhead in the ruler (or AltOption-drag anywhere in it) to scrub: the audio plays as it moves.
  • Tab / ShiftTab: the next or previous hit in the selected clip.
  • Right-click a clip > Spot...: type where it starts (17.3, 1:23.5).
  • Alt←Option← Alt→Option→ nudge it a grid step.

Fix a noise in a recording

  1. Right-click the clip > Repair: Remove Mouth Clicks, Remove Hum (50 or 60 Hz).
  2. Repair > Spectral Repair...: drag a box over a squeak or click in the spectrogram, Remove Selected.

Reuse a part

  • CtrlRCmdR Repeat: a copy right after it.
  • CtrlAltROptionCmdR Repeat Linked: copies that change together (notes, or an audio clip's fades, gain and effects). Right-click > Unlink to vary one.
  • Right-click a section marker: loop, duplicate or delete the whole section.

A band take played without a click

  1. Transport > Conduct Tempo, play the take and tap ShiftT on every beat.
  2. Stop, Make the Tempo: the bar lines fall on the played downbeats, the audio stays put.
  3. The click and MIDI parts now follow the band.

Writing & arranging

Sketch the song before (or after) the band plays it: instruments, a drummer, chords, lyrics, and the order of the sections.

Add an instrument

  1. CtrlAltSOptionCmdS New Instrument Track: a built-in Synth, Drum Machine, Keys, Bass, Guitar, Strings or Sampler, or your plugins.
  2. Double-click in its lane for a clip, double-click the clip for the piano roll; double-click to add notes.
  3. No keyboard? CtrlKCmdK plays notes from the computer keys. Played something good without recording? ShiftR keeps it.

A drum part in a minute

  1. Add section markers (Alt'Option') and name them: intro, verse, chorus.
  2. Track > New Drummer Track, press Play.
  3. Track > Drummer...: a style (Rock, Punk, Metal...), how busy and loud each section is, its fills, and the kick following the bass.
  4. Turn into MIDI to edit every hit in the drum editor.

Chords and ideas

  • View > Chord Lane, double-click to add a chord (Am7, F#dim).
  • Right-click the lane > Chords > Chord Ideas or Melody Idea, then Make a Clip from the Chords to hear them.
  • Hum the tune into a mic, right-click the clip > Melody to MIDI: the notes on a new instrument track.

Write parts faster

In the piano roll, MIDI > Transform Notes on the selected notes:

  • Strum Chords down, up or alternating, like a guitar.
  • Add Flams, Grace Notes, Ratchets, Chop into Repeats.
  • Chance: a hi-hat note that plays some of the time; Euclidean Rhythm... for a pattern.

Try the song in another order

  1. Track > Arranger Chains: click the sections in the order to try (a chorus twice, no bridge).
  2. Play Chain to hear it; make more chains to compare.
  3. Flatten into Song... when one wins: every track rewritten in that order, one undo.

Words and parts on paper

  • View > Lyrics Lane: paste the words, drag each line to where it's sung.
  • Right-click a MIDI track > Notation: sheet music, or tab for a guitar or bass part.
  • File > Sheet Music... prints it or saves a PDF or MusicXML.

Mixing

Press X for the mixer (or click its ×, top right, to close it). View > Mixer Below Tracks or Mixer in Own Window to place it.

Balance first

  1. Set every level with the faders before adding any plugin.
  2. Then pan: guitars left and right, kick, snare, bass and vocal in the middle.
  3. Keep the master meter out of the red; turn tracks down, not the master up.

Solo and mute

  • M mutes, S solos (a soloed track keeps its reverb).
  • AltOption-click S: solo only this track.
  • Shift-click S: dry solo, without its reverb and delay (shows D).
  • Right-click S > Solo-Safe: a reverb bus that stays on.
  • Mix > Unsolo All / Unmute All.

Clean up and shape each track

Select a track, Mix > Add Channel Strip: a Gate, EQ and Compressor before the fader.

  • Gate: raise Threshold to silence noise between notes.
  • EQ: cut what's muddy or harsh before boosting anything.
  • Compressor: Threshold and Ratio even out the level, Makeup brings it back up.

Reverb and delay on a bus

  1. Track > New Bus, then add Reverb, Convolution Reverb or Delay on it, fully wet.
  2. On each track: right-click > Send To > the bus (or + Send on the strip).
  3. Turn each send's knob for how much of that track goes in.
  4. A throw on one word: cut it into its own clip, right-click > Clip Effects > Add an Effect...; it moves with the clip. Render Into the Clip bakes it in (and can be taken back).

A big session

  1. The button under the track headers > Show Only > Drums (or Vocals, a folder, a set of your own): only those tracks, in the tracks and the mixer.
  2. Select tracks and move one's fader: the others follow (pans, mutes and sends too).
  3. Mix > Save Mix Snapshot... to compare versions of the mix; Mix > Mix Snapshots recalls one.
  4. Crackles? Click the CPU reading at the bottom right: the busiest track and plugin are at the top.

Group tracks

  • CtrlShiftDShiftCmdD Group into Folder: a folder is a bus, so one fader and plugins for all the drums.
  • Track > Group into VCA: one fader moves all their faders together.

Automate (moves during the song)

  1. Click the track's A to show its Volume, Pan and send lanes; pick any plugin knob from the lane's menu.
  2. Click in a lane to add a point, drag to shape, AltOption-click a point to delete it.
  3. Or set the mode to Touch or Latch and ride the fader while it plays.
  4. Right-click a lane > Draw Shape: fade, swell, duck over a selected range.
  5. A goes to the next track that has automation, its lanes open (ShiftA back): to find what's automated.
  6. Touch a knob or fader: its track's lane shows it; AltAOptionA opens the lane for the last one touched.

An analog desk's sound

Mix > Console Mode: a Console on every channel and the mix, rounding off peaks with a little warmth. Its Drive sets how much; the same command takes them off.

Stems from another studio? Track > Colour Tracks by Instrument colours them and gives each an icon.

Duck the bass under the kick

Put a Compressor on the bass, right-click its slot > Sidechain From > the kick track.

Heavy guitars and bass: parts of the sound on their own

  • + > Built-in > Split: paths side by side in one slot, each with its own plugins. Split by Frequency to distort a bass's top and keep its lows clean; Nothing for parallel paths; Mid and side to widen only the sides.
  • Multiband Compressor: tighten the boom of palm mutes without touching the top. Dynamic EQ: a cut that only happens on the loud chugs or harsh hits.
  • Parallel compression without a bus: the Compressor's Mix knob.

Fewer knobs, less CPU

  • B Smart Controls: a few big knobs (Drive, Tone, Squash, Space) that each move several settings.
  • Right-click any knob > Modulate With: an LFO in time with the song, or an envelope follower on another track to duck it (View > Modulators).
  • CtrlBControlB Bounce in Place: the track becomes audio, effects and all.
  • Right-click a track > Freeze to free CPU and keep it playing.

Mastering & export

Make the finished mix loud enough, check it against a song you like, and export it.

Build a mastering chain

  1. On the master strip, click + > Built-in and add, in order: EQ, Compressor, Limiter.
  2. EQ: small, broad moves (1–2 dB).
  3. Compressor: gentle, a low ratio, 1–3 dB of gain reduction.
  4. Limiter last: raise its gain while watching I and TP on the loudness meter. Keep TP under −1 dB.

Compare with a reference song

  1. Mix > Choose Reference Track…: pick a finished song you like.
  2. AltROptionR (or Mix / Ref) switches between your mix and it, at the same place in the song.
  3. Leave Match Reference Loudness on so louder doesn't fool you. After changes: Measure Mix Loudness Again.

Check the master

  • AltMOptionM Master Off: the mix without the master's plugins, at the same loudness. Does the master actually help?
  • Mix > Mono: does the bass or vocal disappear?
  • Mix > Dim: does it still work quietly?
  • Mix > Analyser: the spectrum, a spectrogram, a vectorscope (is it wide? out of phase?) or the level over the last minute, with the stereo correlation.
  • Mix > Listen Bus…: room or headphone correction on what you hear only, never in the export (Listen on the master strip).

Or let the assistant master it

  1. CtrlAltAOptionCmdA opens the Assistant (needs Claude Code installed and signed in).
  2. Ask: “master this for streaming, punchy” or “master it like the reference, −14 LUFS”.
  3. It measures, sets the EQ, compressor and Limiter, and checks again. One CtrlZCmdZ undoes it all.

Export the song

  1. CtrlECmdE File > Export…
  2. Tick formats: WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3 (one file each). 24-bit for masters, 16-bit for CDs.
  3. Whole song, or Loop range for part of it.
  4. Loudness: As mixed, or a target from the table below.
  5. Fill in Title, Artist, Album, Year. The status line then shows loudness and true peak.

An album or EP

  1. File > Album..., Add Songs... in order, a gap or crossfade after each.
  2. Match to -14 LUFS, Render Album...: a WAV per song as loud as the others, one long WAV with track marks, and a cue sheet.

Other exports

  • Stems: one file per track, folder and bus, for another studio.
  • Each section: one file per section marker (add markers with Alt'Option').
  • File > Export Range as WAV…: each track in a marquee range.
  • File > Export AAF… for Pro Tools, Resolve, Media Composer.
  • File > Export DAWproject... for Bitwig, Studio One: MIDI, sends, automation, VST3 settings.
  • File > Share with Bandmates to send the song itself.

  • Nothing soloed or muted by mistake: the export plays what you hear.

If something goes wrong

No sound: Audio Settings > Test; check Dim is off and nothing is soloed (Mix > Unsolo All).

Crackles: raise the buffer size; freeze heavy tracks; on Windows use ASIO.

Stuck notes: Transport > Panic, or double-click the play button.

Space or R does nothing: you're typing in a box. Click an empty part of the tracks.

Lost work: File > Song History… lists earlier saves to listen to and restore. Take 5 also keeps a recovery copy every 2 minutes.

Getting started11
Open Take 5 for the first time
  1. On a Mac, drag Take 5 into Applications. The first time, macOS asks you to confirm an app that isn't from the App Store: on macOS 11 to 14, CtrlControl-click Take 5 and choose Open; on macOS 15 or later, double-click it, click Done, then go to System Settings > Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  2. On Windows, run the installer (it installs just for you, no administrator password). If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway. To update later, use Help > Check for Updates... (see "Update Take 5"), or run a newer installer: it installs over the old version, copying only what changed, and if Take 5 is open it asks to close it first (Take 5 asks about unsaved songs).
  3. When Take 5 asks to use the microphone (Mac), click Allow: it needs that to record.
  4. Take 5 opens an empty "Untitled Song" (or the song you had open last time), and a short guided tour starts by itself the first time.
  5. Choose your audio interface or headphones in Settings > Audio Settings..., Ctrl,Cmd,. See "Set up an audio interface".
Update Take 5 (Check for Updates)

Each time Take 5 opens, it first asks the internet whether there's a newer Take 5 (in the background). If there is, a window says An update is available, with what's new in it (its release notes: what's new, changed and fixed; scroll for more) and two buttons: Install Now and Close (which leaves it for now). Help > Check for Updates... (on the Mac, Take 5 > Check for Updates...) asks any time. Install Now downloads it with a progress bar (Cancel stops it) and checks it's exactly the file that was published, then:

  • On Windows, Take 5 closes (asking about unsaved changes first), the installer runs by itself and Take 5 opens again, in a minute or so. Your songs and settings stay as they were.
  • On the Mac, the disk image opens in Finder: quit Take 5, drag the new Take 5 onto Applications (replacing the old one) and open it.

The check when Take 5 opens says nothing unless there's a newer version; Check for Updates at Startup, next to it, turns it off. Which version you have is in Help > About Take 5 (Take 5 > About Take 5), with the build it is ("Take 5 v0.17.1"). The online cheat sheet links to the latest under its title (Windows installer, Mac disk image, All releases).

Take the guided tour (tutorial)

Help > Tutorial starts a tour of the basics on the song that's open. It highlights the real controls one at a time: playing and recording, adding a track, a track's controls, plugins, instrument tracks, writing notes, the mixer, recording takes, saving and exporting, and making Take 5 your own. Do what a step says (it moves on by itself once it's done), or press Next. Back goes to the previous step and Skip ends the tour. The tour runs on its own the first time Take 5 starts, and you can open it again any time from the Help menu. The keys it mentions follow your chosen Key Commands set.

Find where my songs are saved

Each song is a folder of its own in your Music folder, under Take 5 (Music/Take 5, Music\Take 5 on Windows). Inside it are the song file (ending in .tracktionedit), an Audio folder with every recording and imported file, a Backups folder with earlier saves, and Snapshots if you've saved any. Recordings always go into the song's Audio folder, so the folder can be copied or moved as a whole. File > Show Song Folder opens it in Finder (Explorer on Windows).

Start a new song

File > New Song..., CtrlNCmdN, asks for a name and creates the song's folder in Music/Take 5. If you chose a template in Settings > Template for New Songs, the new song starts from it instead (its tracks, plugins, buses and settings). File > New Song from Template picks any saved song template. Without a template a new song has one audio track.

Open a song

File > Open Song..., CtrlOCmdO, opens a song's .tracktionedit file, or a song package (.take5song) a bandmate sent you. File > Open Recent lists the songs you've opened (Clear Menu empties it). Take 5 reopens the last song by itself when it starts. If the song has unsaved changes, Take 5 asks whether to save them first.

Open the demo song

The demo song "Take 5 Demo" plays on Take 5's own sounds (the Amp, the Drum Machine, the built-in synth), so it needs nothing else installed. On the Mac, copy the "Take 5 Demo" folder from the download window into your Music folder; on Windows the installer puts it in Music\Take 5\Take 5 Demo. Then File > Open Song... and open "Take 5 Demo.tracktionedit" inside it.

Know the parts of the window
  • The transport bar (top): go to start, play, record, loop, punch and click buttons; the display with bar, beat and tick, time, tempo and time signature; the click volume and Count-in on the left; the output loudness meter on the right. In a narrow window the bar goes compact (no captions under the display, narrower controls) so everything still fits.
  • Track headers (left): name, input, R (arm), M (mute), S (solo), the headphones (monitoring), A (automation), the fader, pan, meter and plugin slots.
  • The ruler and the lanes above the tracks: tempo, sections, chords and the reference track. Each lane's header (in the track headers' column) shows what it says at the playhead: the time signature and tempo, the section, the chord. Hover a header and click its x to hide the lane; View > Tempo Lane (G), Sections Lane (ShiftG), Chord Lane (AltGOptionG) and Reference Lane (AltShiftGShiftOptionG) show and hide them.
  • The tracks with their clips; below or instead of them, the mixer and the MIDI editor or Pitch editor.
  • Side panels on the right: the cheat sheet, the audio file browser, notes, the assistant.
  • The Inspector on the left (I): the selected track's and clip's settings (see "See a track's settings in the Inspector").
  • The status line at the bottom says what just happened and gives hints.
Find any command or shortcut
  • View > Command Palette..., CtrlShiftPShiftCmdP: type part of a command's name, the first letters of its words ("nit" finds New Instrument Track) or words from what it does; Up/Down and Return run one. Recent commands come first; ones that can't run right now are dimmed. A slip in the typing still finds it ("mxier", "metronme"), and so do its letters in order ("mxr"). When no command matches, it suggests the nearest ("Did you mean...") and the guide's tasks about what you typed ("tone3000" finds the NAM captures task), which open on the online cheat sheet.
  • View > Shortcuts, Ctrl/Cmd/: the cheat sheet beside the tracks, with every key and every mouse gesture, grouped by area. It follows your Key Commands set.
  • Hover over any button for a tooltip.
Bring a song over from another DAW

File > Import Project from Another DAW... opens a Reaper project (.rpp), an Ableton Live set (.als), a DAWproject file (Bitwig, Studio One, Cubase 14 and later), an AAF session (from Logic, Pro Tools, Cubase, Media Composer, DaVinci Resolve) or a folder of stems and MIDI files, as a new song. Tracks, folders, names, volume, pan, mute, colours, audio clips (copied into the song) and MIDI come over, plus the starting tempo and time signature. Plugins, automation, sends and tempo changes don't: an import report lists what didn't come in. Logic, Pro Tools, Cubase and FL Studio's own project files can't be read: export an AAF (or stems and MIDI) from them, and Take 5 explains how when you choose one. OMF isn't supported: export AAF instead.

Import an AAF from Pro Tools, Logic or Media Composer

In the other app, export the session as AAF (Logic: File > Export > Project as AAF File; Pro Tools: Export > Selected Tracks as New AAF/OMF, choosing AAF; Resolve: File > Export > Timeline as AAF). In Take 5, File > Import Project from Another DAW... and pick the .aaf. Clips land where they were, with their fades, crossfades, clip gain and clip volume envelopes, track volume and pan (automation too, in the track's lanes), mute, solo and markers. If the session started at a timecode (01:00:00:00, say), the song keeps it: an AAF exported from the song starts there too, so it lines up again in the editor. If the audio isn't next to the AAF, Take 5 asks for the folder it's in; for Media Composer, choose the drive's "Avid MediaFiles" folder (its MXF audio is converted to WAV files in the song's Audio folder). The import report lists anything missing.

Audio and MIDI setup8
Set up an audio interface
  1. Connect the interface (and install its driver, if it has one) before opening Take 5.
  2. Open Settings > Audio Settings..., Ctrl,Cmd,.
  3. Choose the type of driver (on Windows, ASIO for most interfaces; see "Use ASIO on Windows"), then the interface as the output and the input.
  4. Tick the input channels you'll record from and the output channels you'll listen on.
  5. Pick the sample rate and buffer size (see "Choose a buffer size").
  6. Use the Test button to hear a tone on the outputs.

The interface's name shows at the right end of the status line. Each ticked input appears in the tracks' input menus; inputs are mono, one per track, unless you make a pair one stereo input (see "Record a stereo source on one track"). Settings are remembered between launches.

Choose a sample rate

The sample rate is set in Settings > Audio Settings.... 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz is right for most music; higher rates use more CPU. Exports default to the interface's current rate and can be changed in the export dialog. Change it before recording a song, so every take is at the same rate.

Choose a buffer size (latency vs dropouts)

The buffer size in Settings > Audio Settings... decides how long the sound takes to get through the computer. Smaller is quicker (better for playing through an amp sim or monitoring a vocal) but works the computer harder; too small gives crackles and dropouts. 128 samples is a good start for recording, 64 or 32 on a fast computer with a good interface; raise it to 512 or 1024 for mixing a big song with lots of plugins. If you hear crackles, raise it one step. While recording, Transport > Low-Latency Mode helps too (see "Play through plugins with less delay").

Use ASIO on Windows

For the lowest latency on Windows, choose ASIO as the driver type in Settings > Audio Settings... and your interface's ASIO driver (for example "Focusrite USB ASIO"). The interface's own control panel (buffer size and so on) opens from the Control Panel button there. ASIO is built into Take 5: nothing extra to install but the interface's own driver. Without ASIO, "Windows Audio" works out of the box but runs at about 10 ms; its Exclusive Mode can go lower. On a Focusrite Scarlett, ASIO at 64 or 128 samples plays without dropouts.

Measure recording latency so takes line up

Take 5 moves every recording earlier by the delay the interface reports, so takes line up with what you played along to. Some interfaces report it wrongly. To correct it exactly:

  1. Connect a cable from an output of the interface straight to an input.
  2. Settings > Recording Latency...: choose that output and input.
  3. Press Measure: a short burst of clicks plays and Take 5 listens for it. It shows the round trip and the correction it now applies to that input.

If nothing comes back, check the cable and that the channels are ticked in Audio Settings.

Connect a MIDI keyboard
  1. Plug in the keyboard (USB, or through the interface's MIDI in) before opening Take 5, or open Settings > Audio Settings... after plugging it in.
  2. In Audio Settings, make sure it's ticked under the MIDI inputs.
  3. Add an instrument track (Track > New Instrument Track..., CtrlAltSOptionCmdS). Instrument tracks listen to "All MIDI Ins", so any connected keyboard plays them.
  4. Arm the track (its R button) to play it live and record it.

No keyboard? Use MIDI > Musical Typing (CtrlKCmdK) to play from the computer keyboard.

Send MIDI clock to a drum machine or another app

Settings > Send MIDI Clock To lists your MIDI outputs: tick one and it follows Take 5's tempo and start/stop. Tick it again to stop sending. The option is greyed out when no MIDI output is connected: plug in the device (or start the other app's virtual MIDI port) and open the menu again.

Use Bluetooth headphones (Mac)

Bluetooth headphones are fine for listening but lag too much for recording. Opening AirPods' (or another headset's) microphone switches them to a low-quality call mode, so when the output is a Bluetooth headset, Take 5 records from the built-in microphone instead and the headphones stay in music mode. For recording, use wired headphones on an interface.

Recording19

Recording13
Record a guitar or vocal
  1. Choose your interface in Settings > Audio Settings....
  2. Add an audio track (+ Track) and pick its input from the input menu in its header.
  3. Arm it with its R button. Its meter now shows the input level: play and check it moves without hitting the top.
  4. Turn the click on (K, or the click button) and choose a Count-in in the transport bar.
  5. Press R (or the record button) to record, and Space to stop.

The waveform draws as you record. The take is saved in the song's Audio folder and the song is saved after each take. Recording over an old take replaces it where they overlap, but the old one is kept as a take (see "Comp the best parts of several takes").

Hear myself while recording (input monitoring)

The headphones button in a track's header sets input monitoring. Click it to go through Auto (you hear the input while the track is armed), On (always) and Off (never, for when the interface's own direct monitoring is on). With monitoring on, the input goes through the track's plugins, so you can play through an amp sim. If what you hear is delayed, lower the buffer size or turn on Transport > Low-Latency Mode.

Record several inputs at once (a band)

Tick all the inputs in Settings > Audio Settings..., then Track > New Tracks for Unused Inputs to get a track for each, or set each track's input by hand. Arm them all: Shift-click an R arms the selected tracks too, CtrlCmd-click arms every track, and Track > Arm All Tracks / Disarm All Tracks do it from the menu. Press R: each input records onto its own track.

Record a stereo source on one track (stereo inputs)

Inputs are mono, one per track, which suits guitars and mics. For something stereo (an amp modeller's two outputs, keys, a pair of overheads), open a track's input menu and choose Make Input 1 + Input 2 One Stereo Input (or 3 + 4, and so on): the pair becomes one input, "Input 1 + 2", that track records from it, and its takes are stereo files. Make Input 1 + 2 Two Mono Inputs in the same menu splits it again. The pairs are a setting of this computer, as the inputs are the interface's: a pair made stereo is stereo in every song, and another track that was on Input 1 or 2 has no input afterwards (the status line says which).

Use the metronome and count-in
  • Transport > Metronome, K, or the click button in the transport bar turns the click on or off; the slider next to it is its volume.
  • Count-in (left of the transport bar): No count-in, 1 beat, 2 beats, 1 bar or 2 bars before recording starts.
  • Transport > Click Sound: Woodblock, Claves, Cowbell, Hi-hat, Side Stick, Tambourine, Shaker, Beep or Classic; the first beat of the bar is higher or louder.
  • Transport > Click Only When Recording: no click on playback.
  • Transport > Click Output: send the click to another output (for example only the drummer's headphones) instead of the Main outputs.

The click is never in an export.

Punch in to fix a part
  1. Drag in the ruler over the part to fix: that sets the punch (and loop) range.
  2. Turn on the punch button (red) in the transport bar. Loop and punch can't be on together, so turning one on turns the other off.
  3. Arm the track, start playing before the range and press R (or start recording early): only the punched part is replaced; the rest of the old take stays.

Transport > Pre-roll for Punch-in (Off, 1 bar, 2 bars) starts the playback that much before the punch-in when you record, so you can play yourself in.

Record several takes in a loop

Drag in the ruler to set a range (longer than 2 seconds), turn on looping with Transport > Loop, C, or the loop button, arm the track and record. Each pass becomes a take of its own; stop when you have enough. The track's Takes button then shows them for comping. Transport > Set Loop to Selection, CtrlUCmdU, loops over the selected clips or a marquee range instead.

Keep something I played without recording (Capture Recording)

Take 5 keeps what you play even when not recording: a MIDI keyboard's notes, and the last minute of every input a track in the song uses. Select the track and choose Transport > Capture Recording, ShiftR: it appears as a clip. On an instrument track you get the notes; on an audio track, what came in on its input: the last performance, from after its last pause of 4 seconds or more, saved in the song's Audio folder like a take. Played along with the song, it lands where you played it (for a loop, the last pass); played while stopped, it goes at the playhead with your timing. With no track selected, it goes on the armed track. Undo takes it away.

Play through plugins with less delay (low-latency mode)

Transport > Low-Latency Mode bypasses plugins that delay the sound (look-ahead limiters, linear-phase EQs and the like) while you record, so what you hear while playing is immediate. Turn it off again to bring exactly those plugins back.

Tune my guitar or bass

Transport > Tuner, AltTOptionT, or right-click a track > Tuner... to tune on that track's input. Choose the input, the instrument (6, 7 or 8-string guitar; 4, 5 or 6-string bass, or chromatic) and a tuning (standard, half or whole step down, drop D, C#, C or B, DADGAD, open D, E, G or C, bass drop D and BEAD...). Play a string: the string is found by itself (or click one to tune only it), its note and cents show, and the pill says Tune up, Tune down or In tune (within a cent); a string's peg gets a tick once it's stayed in tune. Needle or Strobe switch the view. A capo, a reference pitch other than A = 440 Hz, and New Tuning... for your own are there too. Mute speakers silences the song while tuning.

Reamp a DI through a real amp

Right-click the DI track > Reamp.... Choose the output your reamp box is on and the input the amp's mic comes back in on, press Measure once (with the amp turned down, or a cable from the output straight to the input), then Set Up Reamp: a "<track> Reamp" track appears under the DI, armed. Record it: the reamped take lines up with the DI to the sample. For pedals or outboard gear on a track as it plays, use the Hardware Insert built-in effect instead.

Make a headphone mix for each player (cue mixes)

Turn on more outputs in Settings > Audio Settings... (for example outputs 3 and 4 for a headphone amp). Track > New Cue Mix... makes a mix for one player on an output of its own. It starts like the main mix and is fed by pre-fader sends (marked "pre" in the mixer), so its levels change without touching the main mix. To send the click to only that output, use Transport > Click Output.

Talk to the band in their headphones (talkback)

Set up a mic at the computer once: Transport > Talkback Settings... has the input it's on (its meter shows it's the right one) and its level in the headphones. Then Transport > Talkback, Y, or Talk on the mixer's master strip turns it on until you press it again: the mic goes into every cue mix (headphone mix), never the speakers, the song or an export. While recording it's turned down 20 dB, so a word during a take doesn't drown the players. It needs cue mixes on outputs of their own (see "Make a headphone mix for each player"); the settings window says which ones it reaches.

Takes and comping6
See all the takes on a track

After recording over the same bars more than once (or loop recording), the track header gets a Takes button with the number of takes. Click it to show or hide the take lanes under the track: each take on its own lane, the parts used in the comp shown bright. MIDI recordings get take lanes too, shown as notes.

Comp the best parts of several takes

Show the take lanes with the track's Takes button. Drag across a take lane over the part you like: that part of that take goes into the track (the comp). Click a take lane to use the whole take. Do this for each phrase until the comp is right; each choice is undoable. Comped pieces are ordinary clips with short crossfades, so you can trim or fade them like any other clip. Moving all of a comp's clips moves the takes with them.

Delete a take I don't want

Hover over a take's name in the take lanes and click its x, or right-click the take > Delete Take (and the comp's pieces of it). Right-click > Use Whole Take puts all of it in the comp. Deleting a take also removes the pieces of it that were in the comp, so check the comp afterwards. CtrlZCmdZ brings it back.

Turn a comp into one clip (Flatten Comp)

Right-click the track > Flatten Comp: the comp becomes one clip that sounds the same (the track's plugins and fader stay on the track). The takes are still there to comp again, and CtrlZCmdZ puts the comp back. For MIDI, the comped clips are merged into one.

Put each take on its own track (Takes to Tracks)

Right-click a track with takes > Takes to Tracks: a copy of the track for each take appears under it, with the same plugins and fader, each playing its take. Useful for doubling a part or comparing takes in the mixer.

Tidy MIDI takes automatically after recording

MIDI > Align MIDI Takes After Recording applies a gentle quantise to each MIDI take as soon as it's recorded, keeping the feel. The setting is saved with the song. Turn it off to keep takes exactly as played. To tidy a part by hand later, open it in the MIDI editor and use MIDI > Align Notes or the Quantise button.

Select, move and copy clips

  • Transport > Set Loop to Selection, CtrlUCmdU: loop it.
  • Track > Bounce in Place (CtrlBControlB): bounce just that range.
  • File > Export Range as WAV...: what each track plays there, one file per track.
  • View > Zoom to Fit, Z: fit it in view.
  • Drag inside it to move what's in it (clips cut at its edges, and its automation) to another place on the same tracks, snapped to the grid: what was where it lands is replaced, and the automation where it was holds the values either side. Hold AltOption to copy it instead (REAPER's razor edits).
  • Drag its right edge to stretch what's in it from its start: audio is time-stretched with its pitch kept, MIDI notes spread out (or squeezed together), and the automation with them. Longer, it covers what came after on those tracks; shorter, it leaves a gap. Warped clips are left as they are.

Each is one undo step. Click an empty lane to clear the range.

Delete so the gap closes (ripple editing)

Turn on Edit > Ripple Edits: deleting clips moves the later clips on the track earlier to close the gap. Or use Edit > Delete and Close Gap, ShiftBackspace, once, on selected clips or a marquee range.

Reverse, change channels or export a clip

Right-click an audio clip:

  • Reverse plays it backwards.
  • Channels: Stereo, Mono, Swap Left and Right, Left Only, Right Only.
  • Export as WAV... saves the clip as heard (gain, fades, envelope, reversed or warped), without the track's effects; with several clips selected, a file each in a folder.
Remove silence between phrases (Strip Silence)

Right-click an audio clip (a vocal, a tom track) > Strip Silence and choose Quiet recordings (-50 dB), Normal (-40 dB) or Loud or noisy (-30 dB): the quiet parts are cut out, leaving a clip for each phrase or hit. The status line says how many clips are left. Clips that follow the tempo, play reversed or loop are skipped.

Make audio follow the song's tempo

Right-click an audio clip (a drum loop, say) > Follow Tempo: it stretches with the song's tempo and tempo changes, keeping its pitch, and stays locked to the beat. Ordinary audio stays where it is in time when the tempo changes; MIDI always stays on its beats.

Right-click it > Stretch Mode for how it's stretched (following the tempo, warped or pitch shifted): Standard (all-round, the default), Percussive (the sharpest hits: drums, picked guitar) or Smooth (bass, vocals, pads: softer attacks). All three stay on the beat; stretched a long way (half speed, say) hits soften and smear in any of them.

Tighten the timing of a drum take (warp and audio quantise)
  • Select audio clips and press Q (Edit > Quantise), or right-click > Quantise Audio to Grid: their hits move to the snap grid.
  • For hand edits, right-click > Warp: drag in the clip's top strip to pin the audio there and move it; drag a marker to move it (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid); AltOption-click a marker to remove it. Remove Warp Markers starts over.
Edit a multi-mic drum kit as one (edit groups)

Select the drum tracks (or their folder) and choose Track > Group for Editing, CtrlShiftGShiftCmdG. Their headers show G1 (G2 for the next group). Now clicking a clip on one mic selects the clips at the same time on all of them, so a cut (CtrlTCmdT), move, nudge, delete or quantise does the same to the whole kit; trimming, fading or slipping one clip does the same to the others; a marquee range over one track covers the group; and comping a take on one mic (the takes lanes) comps the same take on the others. Edit > Edit Groups On turns grouping off for a moment (to fix one mic on its own; the badges go faint); Track > Ungroup for Editing ends it.

Tighten a multi-mic drum take without phase problems (Quantise Group)

Quantising each drum mic on its own moves each by its own hits, so the overheads and rooms drift away from the close mics and the kit sounds smeared. With the kit grouped (above), select a clip on any mic and press Q (or Edit > Quantise Group...); to do only a section, drag a marquee over it first. In the window:

  • Tick the tracks to find the hits on: the kick and snare are ticked to start with (the inside kick and top snare mics).
  • Move them by: Cutting slices every mic a moment before each hit and moves the slices, crossfading the joins: the hits stay exactly as played and the mics exactly in phase (the usual choice for drums). Warping stretches the audio between the hits instead: no joins, the mics within a millisecond of each other.
  • Strength (100%: all the way to the grid) and Sensitivity (higher finds ghost notes too).

It uses the arrange view's grid and feel (swing, groove). One CtrlZCmdZ puts it all back.

Jump to the next drum hit (Tab)

Transport > Go to Next Hit, Tab, moves the playhead to the next hit (transient) on the selected tracks (or on the selected clips' tracks; with nothing selected, any track); ShiftTab goes back to the one before. Handy for cutting at hits (CtrlTCmdT splits there) and checking a quantise.

Slow down, speed up or transpose a clip

Right-click an audio clip:

  • Varispeed (tape: faster is higher): 50% to 200%, like a tape machine; length and pitch change together.
  • Pitch Shift (keeps the length): -12 to +12 semitones.

Both stay in time with the other tracks.

Correct the pitch of a vocal

Double-click an audio clip (or right-click > Pitch Correction...) to open the Pitch editor under the tracks: sung notes as bars and the pitch as a line. Tick Correct: after a moment the corrected line sits on the notes of the key and the clip plays in tune, the voice still sounding like the singer. Choose which notes are in tune (Song key, Chromatic or Notes I choose), and set how far notes go (Correction), how quickly (Speed: 0 is the robotic effect, 100-200 ms keeps vibrato) and how much natural drift stays (Humanise). Drag a note to send it to another note (hold AltOption, to move it in cents); double-click one to leave it as sung. Untick Correct to hear the original.

Fix single notes in the Pitch editor (split, join, vibrato, timing)

Right-click a note in the Pitch editor:

  • Split the Note Here (a melisma or run found as one note, tuned to one pitch) or Join with the Next Note (a legato note found as two).
  • Octave Heard Wrong: the note's real pitch is an octave above or below what's shown.
  • Vibrato (none, half, as sung, more), Drift (straightened, half, as sung) and Tilt (a rise or fall through the note): for that note only, whatever Speed and Humanise say.
  • Formants (bigger or smaller voice) and Level (louder or quieter by 3 or 6 dB).
  • Leave This Note as Sung, Back to the Nearest Note in Tune, Reset This Note.

To move a note's timing, drag its start or end (the pointer shows arrows), or Shift-drag the note to move it whole: it snaps to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, not to). That's written as warp markers on the clip, the audio half a second either side pinned so only the note and the gaps beside it stretch.

Tune a screamed or gritty vocal

In the Pitch editor's More menu, Pitch Heard In says how readily pitch is heard: Screams Left Alone hears pitch only in clean singing, so a scream or growl isn't touched (no warbling); Gritty or Breathy Voice and Rough Voice hear it in rougher singing. To leave one stretch alone (a scream, a spoken line), drag across it in an empty part of the editor and right-click > Leave This Stretch Alone. Leave Notes Already in Tune (Flex-Tune) leaves notes within 10 to 50 cents of a note in tune where they are, so only the ones that are off move.

Formants, the chord lane, breaths and levels for a vocal

In the Pitch editor's More menu:

  • Formants: the whole clip's voice bigger (-) or smaller (+), without changing its pitch (a double that sounds like another singer).
  • Follow the Chord Lane (Chord Snap): each note goes to the nearest note of the chord playing there, not of the key (where the chord lane has chords).
  • Breaths and Esses: the breaths turned down 6, 10 or 15 dB, the esses 4 dB, as dips in the clip's volume envelope (which you can then adjust).
  • Level the Notes: quiet notes up and loud ones down, gently or fully, also in the envelope.
  • Correct the Track's Other Clips the Same Way: a comp's clips with the same settings (not the note-by-note changes).
  • Show Other Tracks Behind: the pitch of doubles and harmonies at the same time, faintly, to match them.
Write lyrics along the song (the lyrics lane)

View > Lyrics Lane shows a lane under the chords. Click a line to select it (it lights up); Shift-click another to select the lines between, CtrlCmd-click to add or take away one. Drag to move the selected lines together (on the grid; hold CtrlCmd, to ignore it). CtrlCCmdC, CtrlXCmdX and Delete copy, cut and delete the selected lines; CtrlVCmdV pastes them at the playhead, as far apart as they were (the words go on the system's clipboard too, to paste into a message). Right-click for Add a Line Here..., Change, Copy, Cut, Paste Here, Delete, Paste Lyrics from Here... (a whole song's words, a line each, a bar apart, to drag into place after) and Clear All Lyrics. Hyphens split a word into its syllables for the lead sheet ("beau-ti-ful").

Double-click the lane (or View > Lyrics Lines...) for the Lyrics Lines window: every line with where it's sung (bar|beat and the time), the one being sung lit. Click a position or the words to type new ones: a position takes a bar ("17"), a bar and beat ("17.3") or a time ("1:23.5") and moves the line there; empty words take the line out. Add Line puts one where you double-clicked (or a bar after the selected line) with its words ready to type; Delete takes the selected lines out; Go To puts the playhead on the selected line; Paste Words... pastes a whole song's words from it. The rows selected there are the lane's selected lines too, so Copy and Paste work on them. While the song plays, the lane's header shows the first word of the line being sung. View > Lyrics Prompter shows the words big, for the singer: the line being sung, the next under it, and a bar across for how far through the line the song is. It opens by itself when recording starts if the song has lyrics; put it on a screen the singer can see. On the lead sheet (Notation, and the printed and PDF parts) the lyrics go under the first part that isn't drums, a syllable a note.

Make a harmony from the lead vocal

Open the lead's clip in the Pitch editor and choose More > Make a Harmony Voice: a third, fifth or sixth above, or a third, fourth or octave below. The notes (as corrected) move that many notes of the key (or the chord, following the chord lane), keeping half the lead's own vibrato so it sounds like a second singer, onto a new track under the lead ("Lead Harmony (3rd above)"). Undo takes it away.

Tighten double-tracked guitars or stacked vocals (align to a reference take)

Pick the take the others should follow (usually the tightest). Select all the takes (Shift-click them), then right-click the reference > Align Selected to This Clip > Tight (100%), Natural (70%) (a little of each player's looseness kept) or Loose (40%). The hits (palm mutes, picked notes, consonants) of each other take are paired with the reference's nearest, within 80 ms, and moved onto them with warp markers; between hits the audio is stretched, with the Percussive stretch mode so the attacks stay sharp. A hit with no partner nearby (a note only one take plays) is left where it is. Move Whole Clips (no stretching) only slides each clip by their typical difference, for takes recorded together that are just a little apart. It takes a few seconds per take; one undo takes it all back, and the warp markers can be moved by hand afterwards (see warping above). The clip's name strip says "warp" once it's aligned.

Match a double's pitch to the lead vocal

Select the doubles (or gang vocals) and the lead, right-click the lead > Match Pitch of Selected to This Clip > Tight, Natural or Loose. Each double is pulled toward the lead's pitch moment by moment, wherever they're singing the same note (within two semitones, so a harmony is left alone), keeping its own formants, so it still sounds like a second voice. The double plays a new file in the song's Audio folder ("matched" on its name strip); right-click it > Remove Pitch Match puts its own audio back. Do it after aligning the timing.

Take out a squeak, a click, hum or mouth clicks (Repair)

Right-click a recorded clip > Repair:

  • Remove Mouth Clicks: lip smacks and tongue clicks on a close-miked vocal, found and smoothed over (the voice around them left alone).
  • Remove Hum (50 Hz: Europe) / (60 Hz: the Americas): mains hum and its harmonics, in narrow notches.
  • Spectral Repair...: the clip's spectrogram (time across, pitch up, louder brighter). Drag a box over the sound to take out (a chair squeak, a pick click, a cough stands out brighter), then Remove Selected: each frequency in the box comes down to what it is just before and after it, so what stood out goes and what was under it stays.

Each makes a new file the clip plays (undoable); Take Off Repairs goes back to the original.

Put a clip at an exact place (spot)

Right-click a clip > Spot... and type where it starts: a bar (17), a bar and beat (17.3), with ticks (17.3.480, 960 a beat), or a time (1:23.5, 83.5s, 0:01:23.5). With several clips selected, the earliest goes there and the others keep their places relative to it. One undo step.

Add and name sections (intro, verse, chorus)

Put the playhead where a section starts and choose Track > Add Section Marker, Alt'Option', or right-click the sections lane > Add Section Marker Here. A section runs to the next marker. Each kind of section has a colour of its own (the usual names all differ: a bridge never looks like the chorus), and every one of a kind looks the same ("Chorus 2" too).

Loop, duplicate or delete a whole section

Right-click a section in the sections lane:

  • Loop This Section sets the loop to it and turns looping on.
  • Duplicate Section repeats it right after itself on every track (a second chorus).
  • Delete Section (and Everything in It) removes it and closes the gap.

Try the song in another order (arranger chains)

Track > Arranger Chains opens a window with the song's sections (from its markers) as buttons: click them in the order you want them played, a section as often as you like (a chorus twice) and leaving out the ones you don't (the bridge cut). The chain shows as a list: Move Up, Move Down, Remove and Clear change it. Double-click the tempo in the transport bar's display and type a new one; pick a time signature from the box next to it. Both apply at the playhead (from the start, for a song with no changes). MIDI stays on its beats; ordinary audio stays where it is in time. The display always shows the tempo and signature at the playhead.

Tap the tempo

Transport > Tap Tempo, ShiftT: tap along at least twice and the tempo at the playhead is set from your taps. A run of taps is one undo step. The first tap only starts the count: the status line says to keep going. To set the tempo by typing, double-click the tempo in the transport bar. For a whole free-time take, see "Fit the tempo to a band take".

Add tempo changes and ramps (the tempo lane)

View > Tempo Lane, G, shows the lane under the ruler. Double-click (or right-click > Add Tempo Change Here) to add a tempo change. Fit the tempo to a band take played without a click (Conduct Tempo)
  1. Choose Transport > Conduct Tempo.
  2. Play the take from the start and tap ShiftT on every beat (or every bar).
  3. Stop: say what each tap was ("Each tap was a beat"), then Make the Tempo.

The bar lines now fall on the played downbeats, the tempo lane shows the speeding up and slowing down, and the audio hasn't moved, so the click and MIDI follow the band. One CtrlZCmdZ undoes it.

Find the tempo of a recording

Right-click an audio clip > Detect Tempo...: for a loop or a take played to a steady beat (six seconds or more), it suggests a tempo, to a tenth of a beat per minute; set the song's tempo from it, or make the clip follow the song. It hears the beat from where the notes start, and the bar from how they repeat, so strummed eighths don't come out twice as fast; between two that fit equally well it picks the more usual tempo (a song at 70 could come out as 140). Choose Set Song Tempo to set the song to it, or Clip Follows Song to stretch the clip to the song's tempo instead. For a band take played without a click, see "Fit the tempo to a band take".

Real-sounding rooms, halls and plates (Convolution Reverb)

Add Built-in > Convolution Reverb (on a bus, as above; it starts fully wet). Space picks the room: Small Room, Studio Room, Drum Room, Chamber, Plate, Short Plate, Concert Hall or Cathedral. Make a knob move by itself (modulators: LFO, envelope follower, steps...)

Squash only the part of the sound that's too loud (Multiband Compressor, Dynamic EQ)

For palm mutes that boom, a bass whose low end jumps around, or cymbals that get harsh, use the + slot > Built-in:

  • Multiband Compressor: the sound is split into 2-4 bands (Bands, with Split 1-3 where they meet) and each band has its own Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release and Gain; each row shows how much its band is being turned down.   • Dynamic EQ: four bands (Low Shelf, Bell or High Shelf, or Off), each with a Frequency and Q, that move only while that part of the spectrum is over its Threshold, by the Ratio, up to the Range (below 0 cuts, above 0 boosts). Process parts of the sound separately (Split)

The + slot > Built-in > Split puts paths side by side in one slot, each with plugins of its own, then mixes them back together. In its window, Split by:

  • Nothing: every path gets the whole sound (parallel processing): a clean and a distorted bass, or a squashed copy of the drums under the drums. 2-4 paths; two paths are 6 dB louder than one, so turn one down.
  • Frequency: each path gets a band (2-4, with Split 1-3 where they meet), and the bands add back up to the sound as it was: distort the bass's highs and keep its lows clean.
  • Mid and side: the mid (what's the same on both sides) and the side (what's different) each get a path, then are put back: widen only the sides, or EQ the middle on its own.
  • Left and right: one path each.

Each path has a Level knob (automatable), M (mute) and S (solo, to hear one band), and its plugins: + adds one, click opens it, right-click for the same menu as a track's slots. Plugins in a path are automated, MIDI-learned and mapped to smart controls like any other, and plugins with latency stay lined up with the other paths. Save the whole Split (paths and plugins) with Save Preset...; it's kept in track templates, and a copied track gets a Split of its own.

For an installed plugin, right-click its slot > Compare: Switch to B and Compare: Copy A to B. Each switch is an undo step. The other set is kept while the song is open, not in the song.

Duck the bass with the kick (sidechain)

Put a Compressor (or another plugin with a sidechain input) on the bass, right-click its slot > Sidechain From and choose the kick track. The kick now drives the compressor, ducking the bass on every hit. None turns it off.

Automate volume, pan or a plugin

Click a track's A button to show its automation lanes (Volume, Pan, sends). To find what's already automated, press A (View > Next Track with Automation, or the A button under the track headers): it scrolls to the next track that has any automation and opens its lanes, the status line naming what's automated; again goes on to the one after, ShiftA back. While a track's automation is showing, touch any of its knobs or faders (in a plugin window, the mixer or the header; a third-party plugin's own knobs while its window is in front) and the lane shows that one, ready to draw, as in Logic; View > Automate Last Touched Control, AltAOptionA, opens the lane for the last one you touched even when the lanes are hidden. Click in a lane to add a point, drag points (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid), right-click or AltOption-click a point to delete it. Pick another parameter, including any plugin knob or send level, from the lane's menu. The mode box sets Read (play back), Touch (record while you hold a control, then return to what was there), Latch (record from the first move until stop) or Write. Ride a fader or plugin knob while playing to record it.

Draw automation shapes over a range

Select a marquee range (or clips), show the automation (A), right-click the lane > Draw Shape: Fade In, Fade Out, Swell, Duck or Sine, just in that range. Reverse and Invert work on the range too, and Copy Lane to Track puts the same automation on another track's matching parameter. With Edit > Automation Moves with Clips on, moving a clip takes the automation under it along.

Send a track to another output

Right-click a track > Output > an output pair (or Master, the main mix). Turn on more outputs in Settings > Audio Settings... first. Useful for outboard summing, a separate headphone feed or a second set of speakers.

Compare my mix with a reference track

Mix > Choose Reference Track... and pick a finished song: the mix's loudness is measured. Then Mix > Listen to Reference (A/B), AltROptionR, or Mix / Ref on the master strip, switches between your mix and the reference at the same place in the song. With Match Reference Loudness on, they play at the same loudness so louder doesn't sound better; Measure Mix Loudness Again after changing the mix. The reference also shows as a lane above the tracks (its waveform from the start of the song, its name in the header): click the lane to switch between it and the mix (View > Reference Lane, AltShiftGShiftOptionG, hides it). Remove Reference Track stops using it. The reference never goes into an export. A reference file inside the song's folder is kept by where it is in the folder, so it's found when the song moves to another computer.

Check the mix in mono, at low volume, and on the analyser
  • Mix > Mono (or Mono on the master strip): hear the mix summed to mono.
  • Mix > Dim (or Dim): 20 dB quieter.
  • Mix > Master Off (Matched Loudness) (or Master Off): see below.
  • Mix > Analyser: the stereo correlation under one of four views, chosen at its top right: Spectrum (bass on the left), Spectrogram (the spectrum over the last seconds, louder brighter, the newest at the right), Vectorscope (where the stereo sits: mono is a line up the middle, wide stereo a round cloud, something out of phase a line across) and Level History (the level over the last minute, to see a chorus that doesn't lift).
  • The loudness readout in the transport bar shows short-term and integrated LUFS and the true peak (red at -1 dBTP or above); click it to reset.

None of these change an export.

Correct my room or headphones (the listen bus)

Mix > Listen Bus... (or Listen on the mixer's master strip) holds plugins that only change what you hear from the speakers or headphones, such as room or headphone correction (Sonarworks SoundID Reference, for example). + Plugin adds one from your scanned plugins, On switches it off to compare, Edit opens its window and x takes it off. They come after everything else (Mono, Dim, the reference) and are never in an export, a bounce or a freeze; the meters show the mix without them. They're kept for this computer, not the song, with their settings. Listen on the master strip is lit while one is on.

Hear what the master's plugins do (Master Off)

Mix > Master Off (Matched Loudness), AltMOptionM, or Master Off on the master strip plays the mix without the plugins on the master (EQ, compressor, Limiter, whatever's there), turned up or down to the loudness it has through them. So you hear what the master does to the sound, not just that it's louder. The first time, and after any change to the song, it renders the mix twice to measure the loudness (over the loop if looping, else the whole song), which takes a few seconds; the status line says how much it turned the mix by. Press it again for the master back. It changes only what you hear (the click too): exports, the assistant's analysis and the saved song keep the master, and there's nothing to undo. A plugin added to the master while it's on isn't bypassed: switch it off and on again.

Bounce a track to audio (Bounce in Place)

Select tracks and choose Track > Bounce in Place (CtrlBControlB), or right-click a track > Bounce in Place: what they play, effects and all, becomes audio on new tracks and the originals are muted. With a marquee range on the track, Bounce Range in Place does just those bars. One undo step.

Plugins7
Find my installed plugins (scan)

Settings > Plugins... scans for installed plugins: VST3, Audio Units and CLAP; VST3 and CLAP on Windows (from C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 and the usual CLAP folders). The scan runs in helper processes, so a plugin that crashes while being scanned doesn't take Take 5 with it. After scanning, effects appear under Plugins in the + slot menu and instruments under Track > New Instrument Track... > Plugins. Scan for Plugins... is also at the bottom of the + slot menu.

Save and load plugin presets

Right-click a plugin's slot > Save Preset... and give it a name; Load Preset lists the saved ones for that plugin. Works for built-in and third-party plugins, on top of the plugin's own presets. Presets are kept in Take 5's settings folder, so they're there in every song. To save a whole track's plugins and settings at once, use right-click the track > Save as Track Template....

A plugin crashed (amber light with a !)

Every third-party plugin runs in a helper process of its own ("Take 5 Plugin Host"), so when one crashes or hangs, the song keeps playing: the plugin's light turns amber with a "!", audio passes straight through it, and a message says what happened. Click the slot to reload it with its last settings, kept up to date in the background. Settings > Run Plugins in Their Own Processes turns this off for all plugins (they then load inside Take 5); right-click a slot > Run in Its Own Process sets it for one.

Use Intel-only plugins on an Apple silicon Mac

Intel-only plugins run under Rosetta in their own helper process: they're found by the scan and work like any other. Their slot menu says "Runs under Rosetta (an Intel-only plugin)". Nothing to set up: Rosetta must be installed (macOS offers it the first time an Intel app runs).

Put an effect on one clip only (clip effects)

For a reverb or echo throw on one word, de-essing one line, or another amp on one part, without automating the whole track: right-click the audio clip > Clip Effects > Add an Effect... and pick it, as for a track. It plays on that clip's audio only, before the track's own effects, and goes with the clip when it's moved, copied or split. Clip Effects lists the clip's effects: each has Open, Bypass and Remove. A clip with effects says "fx 1" (how many) on its name strip. The clip's fades are on its audio, before its effects, so a reverb or delay on it rings on past the clip's end.

Render a clip's effects into it, and take the render back

Right-click a clip with effects > Clip Effects > Render Into the Clip: the clip as it plays, its effects included (a reverb's tail too: the clip gets longer by as much as it rings on, up to the next clip on the track), becomes a new file in the song's Audio folder and a plain clip in its place ("rendered" on its name strip). It saves CPU, and frees the effect to edit the audio further. Clip Effects > Take Back the Render puts the clip back as it was, its effects playing live again, at any time, even after saving and reopening.

Use outboard gear on a track (Hardware Insert)

Add Built-in > Hardware Insert to a track. Choose the interface output your pedal or outboard unit is connected to and the input it comes back on, and press Measure so it stays in time with the other tracks. The track then plays through the gear; bypassing the insert plays it dry.

Finishing and sharing24

Finishing and exporting9
Export the mix as WAV, MP3 or FLAC

File > Export..., CtrlECmdE. Tick one or more formats (WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3: one file each), the bit depth (16 or 24-bit) or MP3 bit rate, the sample rate, and Whole song or Loop range. Fill in Title, Artist, Album and Year for MP3 tags (the artist is remembered). The export respects mute and solo and leaves out the click, the reference, Mono and Dim. The export window shows each file as it's written (its name and the folder it goes in) with a progress bar and Cancel. When it's done it says Export finished, with the files and their integrated loudness, true peak and loudness range (the status line says it too): Open in Explorer (Open in Finder) opens the folder with the file selected, Close Window closes it. MP3 works out of the box and Windows: the LAME encoder is built in.

Export at a loudness target for streaming

In File > Export..., set the loudness to -14 LUFS (streaming), -16 LUFS (podcast/Apple) or -23 LUFS (broadcast) instead of As mixed. The export is brought to that loudness with the true peak kept under -1 dBTP. For a louder master, put the Limiter on the master and push its gain while watching the TP reading in the transport bar.

Master the song for streaming (with the assistant)

Ask the assistant: "master this for streaming, punchy", "master it like the reference, -14 LUFS". It measures the mix (loudness, true peak, and the balance in third-octave bands, compared band by band with the reference track if you've chosen one, at matched loudness), sets up the master's EQ, compressor and Limiter from what it measured, measures again, and adjusts until it's within 0.5 LU of the target with the true peak at or under -1 dB. It's one request, so one Undo takes it all back. Then use Master Off to hear whether the master helps.

Export stems

In File > Export..., choose Stems (one file per track, folder and bus) and a folder. Stems leave out their sends (those are in the bus stems), so dropped into another DAW at the same start they add up to the mix. Drum channels and tracks inside a VCA get stems of their own.

Export each section as its own file

Add section markers first, then in File > Export... choose Each section (one file per section): one numbered file per section, from marker to marker. Take 5 asks for a folder to put them in. The option is greyed out until the song has section markers (see "Add and name sections (intro, verse, chorus)").

Export just part of the song

Set a loop range by dragging in the ruler and choose Loop range in File > Export.... To save what each track plays in a marquee range (with its effects) as separate files, use File > Export Range as WAV....

Put songs together as an album or EP

File > Album... opens a window: the album's Title, Artist and Year, and its songs. Add Songs... adds songs (their .tracktionedit files) at the end; Move Up, Move Down and Remove put them in order. For the selected song, Then: a gap of some seconds before the next, or crossfade over some seconds into it; Trim (dB) turns it up or down by hand after matching. Match to sets the loudness every song is brought to (-14 LUFS for streaming, -16 a little gentler, -11 or -9 loud; never over -1 dB true peak, a song that can't get there under it is left a little quieter and says so), or Off to leave them as mixed. Measure shows each song's loudness now. Render Album... asks for a folder and writes:

  • a WAV per song ("01 Hearts Explode.wav"...), matched, with its title, artist, album, year and track number in it;
  • one WAV of them all, in order with the gaps and crossfades, with a marker where each song starts;
  • a cue sheet (.cue) beside it, to burn a CD or for a distributor.

Each song is rendered from its file, through its own master, so a change to a mix is in the next render (the open song is saved first if it's one of them). Save... keeps the album as an .t5album file next to the songs; Open... brings it back.

Hand a song to Bitwig, Studio One or another DAW (DAWproject)

File > Export DAWproject... saves the song as a .dawproject file, the open format Bitwig, Studio One and others read: the tracks and folders (as submixes), buses (as effect channels), MIDI clips and their notes, audio clips (the audio files go inside it) with their fades, each channel's fader, pan, mute and sends, the automation of faders, pans and sends, the markers, and the tempo. Installed VST3 plugins go across with their settings; CLAP and AU plugins by name (load their settings again there). The built-in EQ and Compressor become the other DAW's own; Take 5's other effects (the amp, the delay...) can't go across, and are listed in each track's comment. Afterwards Take 5 says what didn't go across. For Pro Tools, Media Composer or Resolve, export an AAF instead.

Export an AAF for Pro Tools, Resolve or Media Composer

File > Export AAF... writes the song as an AAF session: audio tracks with their clips, fades and crossfades, clip gain, volume, pan and the markers, with the audio inside the AAF or as WAV files beside it. Clips an AAF can't describe (stretched, warped, reversed, looped, pitch-shifted, with envelopes) go out rendered as they sound. MIDI, muted tracks and clips, plugins, sends and automation don't go, and the export says so. A song imported from an AAF that started at a timecode starts there again. For MIDI parts, use File > Export MIDI File....

Sharing with bandmates5
Put my name on parts and comments

Settings > Your Name...: the name on the parts you send and the comments you write ("Alan: retake this bar"). Set it once on each computer, before you send parts or leave comments, so bandmates can see who played what. Tracks a bandmate sends you arrive as alternatives named after them.

Send a song to a bandmate (song package)

File > Share with Bandmates > Save Song Package... makes one .take5song file with the song and only the audio it uses. Send it any way you like. Double-clicking it (or File > Open Song...) opens it as a song of its own in Music/Take 5 ("<song> (2)" if the name is taken), with its plugins, takes and drum kits.

Work on a song through a shared band folder
  1. On your computer: File > Share with Bandmates > Share in Band Folder... and choose a folder your band shares (iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, Syncthing). A folder named after the song appears there with the package and a Parts folder.
  2. A bandmate double-clicks the package there: it opens as their own copy.
  3. They record a part, then Share with Bandmates > Send Parts...: their new tracks are ticked; Send to Band Folder.
  4. Take 5 checks the folder every 20 seconds or so. When parts arrive, "<name> sent parts" asks to import them: Import, Later (asked again next time the song opens) or Don't Import.

Nobody's song file is ever overwritten: only .take5song and .take5parts files go in the shared folder.

Import parts a bandmate sent

Double-click a .take5parts file, or File > Share with Bandmates > Import Parts.... New tracks land as new tracks, where they were played (on the same bars even if the tempo changed); a new version of a track you already have arrives as an alternative of it, named after who played it (right-click the track > Alternatives). CtrlZCmdZ takes them out again.

Leave a comment at a point in the song

Right-click the sections lane > Add Comment Here...: a dot with your initial, signed with your name. Hover over the dot to read it (who, what, where); right-click it > Remove Comment. Comments go to bandmates with the parts you send.

Backups, history and recovery10
Undo several steps at once

Edit > Undo, CtrlZCmdZ, and Redo, CtrlShiftZShiftCmdZ; the menu names the step. Edit > Undo History lists the last steps: choose one to go back to before it in one go. Fader, pan, send and plugin-knob moves are undoable too (one step per drag), and each assistant request is a single step. Saving isn't a step: after a save, Undo still undoes your last edit. Insert Silence, Delete and Close Gap and deleting a range are one step each. For versions from earlier saves, see "Go back to an earlier save".

How autosave and backups work

While there are unsaved changes, Take 5 saves a recovery copy every 2 minutes. Each File > Save (CtrlSCmdS) keeps the previous save in the song's Backups folder (at most one every 5 minutes, the newest 20). The song is also saved after each recorded take.

Recover after a crash

If Take 5 stopped without saving (a crash, a power cut, a force quit), the next time you open the song it offers the recovery copy: Recover Changes brings your unsaved work back; Open Last Save opens the song as last saved and drops the recovery copy. A recovered song counts as unsaved until you save it, so closing it asks whether to save; the recovery isn't offered again.

Go back to an earlier save
  • File > Revert to Backup lists earlier saves by time; pick one.
  • File > Song History... lists backups and snapshots, newest first. Select one and press Listen (or double-click it) to hear 30 seconds of that version from the playhead (or the loop) without changing the song. Restore... makes it the song again; the song as last saved is kept as a backup.
Bring back a deleted track

File > Song History..., pick a save from before you deleted it, then Bring Back Track: tracks you've deleted since are listed under "Deleted since then". It comes back where it was, with its clips and plugins. You can also bring back a copy of how a track used to be. CtrlZCmdZ takes it away again.

Save a named snapshot

File > Save Snapshot... (or Save Snapshot in Song History) keeps the song as it is now under a name ("before the rewrite"), unsaved changes included. Snapshots show marked SNAPSHOT in File > Song History... and are kept until you delete them from the song's Snapshots folder.

Keep a second copy on another disk or in the cloud

Settings > Second Copy of Backups... (or Second Copy... in Song History): choose a folder on another disk or one a cloud service syncs. After each save, a folder named after the song there gets its song file, backups, snapshots and audio. If the disk isn't connected, the status line says the copy wasn't made.

Start songs from a template

Set up a song the way you like to start (band tracks, inputs, buses, click), then File > Save as Song Template...: tracks, plugins, buses and settings, no clips. File > New Song from Template starts from it, and Settings > Template for New Songs makes File > New Song always use it (None (one audio track) goes back).

Tidy up a song folder
  • File > Collect Audio into Song copies audio the song uses from elsewhere into its Audio folder, so the folder works on its own.
  • File > Clean Up Unused Audio... lists files nothing uses and moves them to the Trash (Recycle Bin on Windows).
Find files a song is missing

When a song opens and some of its files aren't where it says (it came from another computer, or a folder was moved or renamed), a message lists them: its clips' audio, the Capture Player's captures and cabinet IRs, the reference track. The rest of the song plays. Find in Folder... looks through a folder you choose, and the folders in it, for each by its file name and points the song at what it finds (one undo step); Later leaves them. File > Find Missing Files... does it any time.

Help17

The assistant4
Set up the assistant

The assistant makes changes you ask for in plain words, through Claude Code on your own Claude plan.

  1. Install Claude Code from claude.com/code.
  2. Open Terminal (on Windows, a command prompt), type claude, and sign in once.
  3. In Take 5, View > Assistant, CtrlAltAOptionCmdA. If it was open before Claude Code was installed, press Look Again (also in its ... menu).

The panel's ... menu shows where Claude Code is.

Ask the assistant to change the song

Type a request in the assistant panel and press Return: "compress the bass a bit", "make the chorus 2 dB louder", "add a bar of silence before the outro", "pan the rhythm guitars wider", "transpose the arp up an octave", "set the tempo to 120". It says what it changed. Each request is one undo step: CtrlZCmdZ takes the whole request back (Edit > Undo names it "Assistant: ..."). While it works, Send becomes Stop: it stops after the step it's on. A reply too long for the panel opens at its start.

Ask the assistant about the song or about Take 5

Ask questions too: "why does the mix sound muddy?" makes it analyse the mix before answering, without changing anything. It can also answer "how do I..." questions from this guide, find menu commands and their shortcuts, run commands (show the mixer, start the tuner, turn on the loop), and read and change settings. It asks before changing anything that could cut the sound, such as the audio device or buffer size. It knows every plugin you have, built in and installed: it can add one by name, turn any of its knobs ("set the reverb's mix to 30%"), load or save its presets, and put another instrument on a track ("give the keys a grand piano sound"). It won't open, close or save songs, quit, or undo for you. Click one of its messages and press CtrlCCmdC to copy it; right-click for Copy Conversation.

What the assistant can and can't do

It works only on the open song and Take 5's own commands and settings: it can't open files, run other programs or touch anything outside the app. Only the song's details (track names, levels, plugins, notes, measurements) go to Claude, never audio. It remembers the conversation until you choose ... > Clear Conversation or open another song. If Claude Code isn't installed, isn't signed in, or the internet is off, the panel says so and the song isn't touched.

Troubleshooting10
I can't hear anything
  1. Check Settings > Audio Settings...: the right output device, its output channels ticked, and press Test for a tone.
  2. Check the master fader and that Mix > Dim isn't on; check nothing is soloed (Mix > Unsolo All) or muted (Mix > Unmute All).
  3. Check the track's output (right-click > Output) is Master or an output you're listening to.
  4. A deactivated track (greyed out) is silent: right-click > Activate Track.
  5. If something went extremely loud, the output mutes itself for 2 seconds to protect your ears and the status line says so.
I can't hear my guitar or mic while recording
  1. In Settings > Audio Settings..., the interface is the input device and its input channel is ticked.
  2. The track's input menu shows that input (not "No input").
  3. The track is armed (R): its meter should move when you play. If not, check the interface's gain and cable.
  4. Input monitoring (the headphones button) is Auto or On, not Off.
  5. On a Mac, Take 5 must be allowed the microphone: System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone.
  6. Some interfaces have a direct-monitor knob or switch; if you hear yourself twice, set monitoring to Off.
My recording is late or out of sync with the click

Take 5 moves recordings earlier by the latency the interface reports. If takes still land late or early, measure the real round trip: a cable from an output straight to an input, Settings > Recording Latency..., Measure (see "Measure recording latency so takes line up"). Plugins that delay the sound are compensated on playback; while recording, Transport > Low-Latency Mode keeps what you hear immediate. Bluetooth headphones lag too much to record with.

I hear crackles, clicks or dropouts
  1. Raise the buffer size in Settings > Audio Settings... (for example 128 to 256, or 512 for mixing).
  2. On Windows, use the interface's ASIO driver rather than Windows Audio or DirectSound.
  3. Find the heavy ones in View > CPU Use (or click the CPU reading at the bottom right), then freeze or deactivate them (right-click a track > Freeze or Deactivate Track).
  4. Clicks at clip edits: turn on Edit > Snap Edits to Zero Crossings, or add short fades.
  5. Clicks only in a mix that's very loud may be clipping: watch the master meter and the TP readout.
My MIDI keyboard doesn't play anything
  1. Plug it in before starting Take 5 (or reopen Settings > Audio Settings...) and check it's ticked under the MIDI inputs there.
  2. Play an instrument track (Track > New Instrument Track...), not an audio track, and arm it (R).
  3. The instrument track's input should be "All MIDI Ins" or your keyboard.
  4. Check the instrument makes sound: click a key on the piano roll's keyboard, or a Drum Machine pad.
  5. A bundled instrument only plays its own range: Musical Typing dims keys out of reach.
  6. Notes stuck on? Transport > Panic, or double-click the play button.
My keyboard plays some notes twice

Some keyboards with worn contacts send a note twice. Settings > Ignore Notes Sent Twice By and tick the keyboard: a doubled note sounds and records once, while fast repeated notes still work. For notes already recorded, MIDI > Remove Doubled Notes.

Notes or sounds are stuck on

Transport > Panic, or double-click the play/stop button: every plugin is reset and all notes, sounds and the sustain pedal are released on every track and MIDI input, including plugins in their own processes. If a note keeps sticking, check the keyboard's sustain pedal and cable, and see "My MIDI keyboard doesn't play anything".

A plugin is missing from the menu
  1. Scan again: Settings > Plugins.... A plugin that crashes during the scan is skipped.
  2. Check its format:, VST3, Audio Units or CLAP; on Windows, VST3 or CLAP.
  3. Check it's installed where plugins go (on Windows, C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 for VST3s).
  4. Instruments are listed under Track > New Instrument Track... > Plugins, effects under the + slot > Plugins.
Space or R doesn't do anything

Single-key shortcuts don't work while you're typing in a text box (a track name, a value box): click an empty part of the tracks first. While Musical Typing is open, letters play notes. If you changed the key set, check Settings > Key Commands... or the cheat sheet (Ctrl/Cmd/).

Send a bug report

Help > Show Log Files opens the folder with this launch's log selected ("Take 5 <date> <time>.log"). Send it to Alan with what you did; it already has your computer, system, audio device and everything the app did. For a plugin problem, send "Plugin Hosts.log" from the same folder too. If Take 5 won't start, the logs are in ~/Library/Logs/Take 5 and %APPDATA%\Take 5\Logs on Windows. Help > Detailed Log writes more, for chasing a problem.

Windows and Mac differences3
Shortcuts and menus on Windows

Where this guide says Cmd, use Ctrl; where it says Opt, use Alt (Ctrl+S saves, Ctrl+Z undoes). The two shortcuts that are Control on the Mac, Bounce in Place and Mute Notes, are Ctrl+B and Ctrl+M on Windows. The menu bar is inside the Take 5 window. Finder is Explorer, and the Trash is the Recycle Bin. On some European keyboards, Ctrl+Alt is AltGr, which types characters like @ and { in text boxes: if a Ctrl+Alt shortcut gets in the way, change it in Settings > Key Commands....

Where Take 5 keeps things
  • Songs: Music/Take 5 (Mac), Music\Take 5 (Windows).
  • Settings, templates and presets: ~/Library/Take 5 (Mac), %APPDATA%\Take 5 (Windows).
  • Logs: ~/Library/Logs/Take 5 (Mac), %APPDATA%\Take 5\Logs (Windows).
  • Backups: in each song's own Backups folder, on both.
What's different on Windows
  • Audio: ASIO (with your interface's driver) or Windows Audio, instead of CoreAudio.
  • Plugins: VST3 and CLAP (Audio Units are Mac-only).
  • Printing sheet music opens the PDF in your PDF viewer to print from.
  • The Bluetooth-headset microphone switch and Rosetta for Intel-only plugins are Mac-only.
  • The first launch may show SmartScreen's "Windows protected your PC": More info, Run anyway.
  • About Take 5 and Check for Updates... are in the Help menu (on the Mac, in the Take 5 menu), and an update installs itself and opens Take 5 again; on the Mac you drag the new one into Applications.

Everything else (songs, templates, song packages, parts) works the same on both. A song folder copied from one to the other opens with its audio: a song saved on Windows names its audio files the Windows way, and the Mac reads that too (saved again, it's written the way both read).
