The assistant

Set up the assistant

The assistant makes changes you ask for in plain words, through Claude Code on your own Claude plan, or free through Google's Gemini. With Claude Code:

  1. Install Claude Code from claude.com/code.
  2. Open Terminal (on Windows, Command Prompt), type claude, and sign in once.
  3. In Take 5, View > Panels > Assistant, CtrlAltAOptionCmdA. Take 5 looks for Claude Code when it opens: if you installed it while Take 5 was open, press Look Again in the panel (shown while there's nothing to run on), or ... > Look for Claude Code Again, or reopen Take 5.

With Gemini (no Claude plan needed):

  1. Settings > Assistant..., then Get a Free Key...: sign in to Google AI Studio with a Google account and create a key (no card needed).
  2. Paste it into Gemini key, set Use to Gemini (or leave it on Automatic, which uses Claude Code when it's installed, else Gemini when there's a key) and press Save. With Use on Claude Code it never falls back to Gemini.
  3. Open the assistant: View > Panels > Assistant.

Gemini model is the model it asks (gemini-flash-latest, the newest Flash, unless you change it; left empty, it's that); Choose Model... (with the key typed in) lists the models your key can use, the newest Flash and Flash-Lite first, to pick one. After Save, the status line says what the assistant runs on; saved while it's working on a request, it changes over once that's done. Gemini's free tier has limits per minute and per day, each model its own: when the model's limit is reached it asks the lighter Flash-Lite model instead (and says so in the chat), and when Flash-Lite's is reached too it says so, or waits a moment when the wait is short. When Google's servers are busy ("high demand") it asks again a couple of times, then asks Flash-Lite; with no answer within a minute it switches to Flash-Lite at once. Once Flash-Lite has answered for it, the next 10 requests that get an answer go straight to Flash-Lite, then it tries your model again. If Flash-Lite is busy too, or doesn't answer within a minute either, it says to try again in a few minutes. Google may use what's sent on the free tier to improve its products. The key is kept in Take 5's settings on this computer (not encrypted) and never written to the log. The panel's ... menu shows what it runs on, and has Assistant Settings.... The assistant needs a song open.

Ask the assistant to change the song

Type a request in the assistant panel and press Return: "compress the bass a bit", "make the chorus 2 dB louder", "add a bar of silence before the outro", "pan the rhythm guitars wider", "transpose the arp up an octave", "set the tempo to 120". It says what it changed. Each request is one undo step: CtrlZCmdZ takes the whole request back (Edit > Undo names it "Assistant: ..."). A menu command it runs is an undo step of its own, and settings it changes (the audio device, the theme, the click) aren't undone. While it works, the box shows what it's doing and for how long, and Send becomes Stop: it stops after the step it's on, keeping what it changed so far (one undo step). A reply too long for the panel opens at its start. With Gemini, a request that takes more than 25 steps stops: ask for less at once.

Ask the assistant about the song or about Take 5

Ask questions too: "why does the mix sound muddy?" makes it analyse the mix before answering, without changing anything. It can also answer "how do I..." questions from this guide, find menu commands and their shortcuts, run commands (show the mixer, start the tuner, turn on the loop), and read and change settings. It asks before changing anything that could cut the sound, such as the audio device or buffer size, and changes it only once you've said yes. Besides those, it can switch MIDI inputs and outputs, the click's sound and output, the count-in and pre-roll, and the theme. It knows every plugin you have, built in and installed: it can add one by name, turn any of its knobs ("set the reverb's mix to 30%"), load or save its presets, and put another instrument on a track ("give the keys a grand piano sound"). It won't open, close or save songs, quit, or undo for you. Click one of its messages and press CtrlCCmdC to copy it (as plain text); CtrlACmdA copies the whole conversation; right-click for Copy and Copy Conversation.

What the assistant can and can't do

It works only on the open song and Take 5's own commands and settings: it can't open files you choose, run other programs or touch anything outside the app (it writes only into the song's folder: a DAWproject export, a TONE3000 tone it downloads). Only the song's details (track names, levels, plugins, notes, lyrics, measurements), the guide's text and your audio devices' names go to Claude (or Gemini), never audio. It remembers the conversation until you choose ... > Clear Conversation, open another song, save the assistant's settings, or quit (with Gemini, a request that failed or was stopped isn't part of what it remembers, though what it changed stays in the song). If Claude Code isn't installed, isn't signed in, Gemini's key is wrong or its limit is reached, or the internet is off, the panel says so: a request that fails before it starts leaves the song alone; one that stops partway keeps what it changed (one undo step). On Windows, a request to Claude Code can be about 32,000 characters at most.

Troubleshooting

I can't hear anything

  1. Check Settings > Audio Settings...: the right output device, its output channels ticked, and press Test for a tone.
  2. Check the master fader and that Mix > Dim isn't on; check nothing is soloed (Mix > Unsolo All) or muted (Mix > Unmute All).
  3. Check the track's output (right-click > Output) is Master or an output you're listening to.
  4. A deactivated track (greyed out) is silent: right-click > Activate Track.
  5. If something went extremely loud (over +12 dB, or broken audio from a plugin), the output mutes itself for 2 seconds to protect your ears and the status line says so.

I can't hear my guitar or mic while recording

  1. In Settings > Audio Settings..., the interface is the input device and its input channel is ticked.
  2. The track's input menu shows that input (not "No input").
  3. The track is armed (its R button, not the R key): its meter should move when you play. If not, check the interface's gain and cable. (An input feeds one track at a time: choosing it on another track takes it from this one.)
  4. Input monitoring (the headphones button) is Auto or On, not Off. It belongs to the input: every track on that input follows it.
  5. On a Mac, Take 5 must be allowed the microphone: System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone.
  6. Some interfaces have a direct-monitor knob or switch; if you hear yourself twice, set monitoring to Off.

My recording is late or out of sync with the click

Take 5 moves recordings earlier by the latency the interface reports. If takes still land late or early, measure the real round trip: a cable from an output straight to an input, Settings > Recording Latency..., Measure (see "Measure recording latency so takes line up"); it's kept for each input, so measure each one you record from. Plugins that delay the sound are compensated on playback; while recording, Transport > Low-Latency Mode keeps what you hear immediate. Bluetooth headphones lag too much to record with.

I hear crackles, clicks or dropouts

  1. Raise the buffer size in Settings > Audio Settings... (for example 128 to 256, or 512 for mixing).
  2. On Windows, use the interface's ASIO driver rather than Windows Audio or DirectSound.
  3. Find the heavy ones in View > Panels > CPU Use (or click the CPU reading at the bottom right), then freeze or deactivate them (right-click a track > Freeze or Deactivate Track).
  4. Clicks at clip edits: turn on Edit > Edit Options > Snap Edits to Zero Crossings, or add short fades.
  5. Clicks only in a mix that's very loud may be clipping: watch the master meter and the TRUE PEAK readout.

My MIDI keyboard doesn't play anything

  1. Plug it in: Take 5 finds a MIDI keyboard within a few seconds, even while it's open, and listens to every one unless it's switched off in MIDI > MIDI Inputs (ticked: listened to). If it still plays nothing, unplug it and plug it back in, or quit and reopen Take 5. A Bluetooth one has to be paired first: Bluetooth MIDI... in Settings > Audio Settings... (Mac only).
  2. Play an instrument track (Track > New Instrument Track...), not an audio track, and arm it (its R button).
  3. The instrument track's input should be "All MIDI Ins" or your keyboard.
  4. Check the instrument makes sound: click a key on the piano roll's keyboard, or a Drum Machine pad.
  5. A bundled instrument only plays its own range: Musical Typing dims keys out of reach.
  6. Notes stuck on? Transport > Panic, or double-click the play button.

My keyboard plays some notes twice

Some keyboards with worn contacts send a note twice. Settings > Ignore Notes Sent Twice By and tick the keyboard (kept for that keyboard): a second note-on for a key still held, within 40 ms, is dropped, so a doubled note sounds and records once while fast repeated notes still work. For notes already recorded, open them in the MIDI editor and choose MIDI > Remove Doubled Notes (notes on one key starting within 40 ms become one).

Notes or sounds are stuck on

Transport > Panic (it has no key until you give it one), or double-click the play button: every plugin is reset and all notes, sounds and the sustain pedal are released on every track and MIDI input, including plugins in their own processes. If a note keeps sticking, check the keyboard's sustain pedal and cable, and see "My MIDI keyboard doesn't play anything".

A plugin is missing from the menu

  1. Scan again: Settings > Plugins..., then Options... > Scan for new or updated VST3, AudioUnit or CLAP plug-ins, and choose the folders to look in. Each plugin is scanned in a separate process: one that crashes (twice) or takes more than 2 minutes is skipped, listed at the end, and tried again next time. Options... also removes plugins from the list (the selected ones, or those no longer there).
  2. Check its format:, VST3, Audio Units or CLAP; on Windows, VST3 or CLAP.
  3. Check it's installed where plugins go:, Library/Audio/Plug-Ins (in your home folder or the disk's); on Windows, C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 (or CLAP), or the same in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Common.
  4. Instruments are listed under Track > New Instrument Track... > Plugins, effects under the + slot > Plugins.

Space or R doesn't do anything

Space plays and stops, R records. Single-key shortcuts don't work while you're typing in a text box (a track name, a value box): click an empty part of the tracks first. With no song open, there's nothing to play. If you changed the key set, check Settings > Key Commands... or the cheat sheet (Ctrl/Cmd/). (While Musical Typing is on, the letter keys of its keyboard play notes; Space and R still work.)

Send a bug report

Help > Show Log Files opens the folder with this launch's log selected ("Take 5 2026-10-31 14-05-12.log": the date and time it opened). Send it to Alan with what you did; it already has your computer, system, audio device, MIDI inputs, how the last launch ended and everything the app did. The last 20 launches' logs are kept. For a plugin problem, send "Plugin Hosts.log" from the same folder too. If Take 5 won't start, the logs are in ~/Library/Logs/Take 5 and %APPDATA%\Take 5\Logs on Windows. Help > Detailed Log (ticked while on, and remembered) writes more, the plugins' processes too, for chasing a problem.

Windows and Mac differences

Shortcuts and menus on Windows

Where this guide says Cmd, use Ctrl; where it says Opt, use Alt (Ctrl+S saves, Ctrl+Z undoes). The two shortcuts that are Control on the Mac, Bounce in Place and Mute Notes, are Ctrl+B and Ctrl+M on Windows. Return is Enter. The menu bar is inside the Take 5 window (open its menus with the mouse; Alt and F10 don't). File > Exit, Ctrl+Q, quits (on the Mac, Take 5 > Quit Take 5). Finder is Explorer, and the Trash is the Recycle Bin. In the key sets, where a Mac key and a Control key become the same Ctrl key, the second command gets Ctrl+Alt instead (Set B's MIDI Editor, Ctrl+Alt+=; Mute Notes in Sets C and F, Ctrl+Alt+M).

Where Take 5 keeps things

  • Songs: Music/Take 5 (Mac), Music\Take 5 (Windows).
  • Settings, templates and presets: ~/Library/Take 5 (Mac), %APPDATA%\Take 5 (Windows).
  • The demo song: the Windows installer puts it in Music\Take 5\Take 5 Demo; on the Mac, copy it from the disk image.
  • Logs: ~/Library/Logs/Take 5 (Mac), %APPDATA%\Take 5\Logs (Windows).
  • Backups: in each song's own Backups folder, on both.

What's different on Windows

  • Audio: ASIO (with your interface's driver), Windows Audio or DirectSound, instead of Core Audio.
  • Plugins: VST3 and CLAP (Audio Units are Mac-only).
  • MIDI: no Bluetooth MIDI keyboards (pairing is Mac-only), and for MIDI between apps you need a virtual MIDI port tool (the Mac has the IAC Driver built in).
  • Printing sheet music opens the PDF in your PDF viewer to print from.
  • The Bluetooth-headset microphone switch and Rosetta for Intel-only plugins are Mac-only.
  • The first launch may show SmartScreen's "Windows protected your PC": More info, Run anyway.
  • About Take 5, Check for Updates... and Check for Updates at Startup are in the Help menu (on the Mac, in the Take 5 menu), and an update installs itself and opens Take 5 again; on the Mac you drag the new one into Applications. The installer adds Take 5 to the Start menu (and, if you like, the desktop); uninstall it from Settings > Apps.
  • A new song's folder name loses dots and spaces at its end (Windows doesn't allow them).

Everything else (songs, templates, song packages, parts) works the same on both. A song folder copied from one to the other opens with its audio: songs are saved the way both read (a song saved by an older version on Windows is read on the Mac too). Audio kept outside the song's folder (by a full path, such as C:\... or a network drive) isn't found on the other system: File > Collect Audio into Song first.
