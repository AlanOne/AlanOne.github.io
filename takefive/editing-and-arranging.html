Tracks

Add an audio track

Click + Track at the top of the track headers (beside the feel and snap boxes), or Track > New Audio Track, CtrlAltNOptionCmdN. An audio track records from one input (a guitar, a mic) or holds audio files. The new track goes at the end, named "Audio 1", "Audio 2"..., with the next colour, and takes the first input no other track uses (or none: pick one in its input menu). Track > Other New Tracks > New Tracks for Unused Inputs adds a track for every input that hasn't got one, for recording a band. Adding a track is one undo step.

Rename, colour, reorder or delete tracks

  • Rename: double-click the track's name (or right-click the header > Rename).
  • Colour: right-click the header > Colour, one of eleven (blue first, grey last). Track > Colour Tracks by Instrument colours every track, and gives it a picture, from what its name says it is (kick, bass, vocal...).
  • Reorder: drag a header up or down; a line shows where it goes. Several selected tracks move together. The same works in the mixer, dragging a strip by its name.
  • Delete: right-click the header > Delete Track, or select tracks and press Delete. Deleting and moving are each one undo step.
  • Select tracks: click a header; Shift-click or CtrlCmd-click adds or removes tracks. A right-click acts on the selected tracks when the one you click is among them, else on that one alone.
  • Height: View > Zoom > Taller Tracks / Shorter Tracks, Ctrl↑Cmd↑ / Ctrl↓Cmd↓: all tracks, in steps of 20 points, between 60 and 280.

Group tracks into a folder (a drum bus)

Select the tracks and choose Track > Group > Group into Folder, CtrlShiftDShiftCmdD, or right-click a header > Group Selected Tracks into Folder. The folder, named "Folder", takes the first track's place. A folder is also a group bus: everything inside plays through its fader, pan, mute and plugins (put the drum bus compressor there). Click its chevron to collapse or expand it (collapsed, it shows a mini row per track). Drag a track onto the middle of a folder's header to put it in, or onto its top or bottom edge to put it above or below. Right-click the folder > Ungroup Folder removes the folder and keeps the tracks where it was. Track > Group > Group into VCA is the other kind: a folder whose fader moves its tracks' levels without mixing them through it (see "Move several faders together"). Grouping and ungrouping are one undo step each.

Copy tracks with all their settings

Select track headers and Edit > Copy, CtrlCCmdC, then Edit > Paste, CtrlVCmdV: new tracks after the last selected one (in its folder, if it's in one; at the end with none selected), with the same input, plugins and their settings, automation, fader, pan, mute, sends and colour, but no clips. Edit > Paste Tracks with Clips, CtrlShiftVShiftCmdV, brings the clips too. Copying a folder brings its tracks. An input feeds one track at a time, so a copy only gets the input if no other track uses it. Each paste is one undo step.

Save and reuse a track setup (track templates)

Right-click a track's header > Save as Track Template... and name it: its plugins and their settings, automation, fader, sends, colour and input are saved, not its clips (a guitar track with your amp sim, a vocal chain). With several tracks selected (including the one you right-click), or a folder, they're saved together. In any song, Track > Other New Tracks > New Track from Template adds them. Templates are kept with Take 5, not the song, so every song can use them. For whole-song setups, see "Start songs from a template".

Try different versions of a track (alternatives)

Right-click an audio or instrument track > Alternatives keeps several versions of a track's clips to switch between, on the same plugins, settings and automation (only the clips differ):

  • New Alternative (Empty) starts a blank one ("Alt 2"...) to record a different part.
  • Duplicate as New Alternative copies the current clips to edit.
  • Picking a name switches to it. The first is "Main".
  • Rename..., and Delete This Alternative (its clips go with it; greyed for the last one).

Once a track has two or more, its header shows which one is playing. Alternatives are saved with the song and undoable. A bandmate's version of a part arrives as an alternative named after them.

Show only some tracks in a big session (hide tracks, track sets)

The button at the bottom of the track headers (it says "All 75 tracks", or "14 of 75 tracks shown", lit while any are hidden) and View > Show Tracks choose what's shown in the tracks and the mixer:

  • Show All Tracks brings every one back (also View > Show All Tracks).
  • Under Show Only, the sets, each with how many tracks: your own, then ones made from the song: each folder's tracks, and tracks grouped by what their names say they are (Drums: kick, snare, toms, hats, overheads, rooms...; Bass, Guitars, Keys, Vocals, and Buses and FX; each only when two or more tracks fit). One click shows just those.
  • Show Only the Selected Tracks and Hide the Selected Tracks; or right-click a track > Hide Track.
  • Save What's Shown as a Set... names what's shown now (a name you've used replaces that set; "Drums" of your own wins over the one made from the names); Delete a Saved Set takes one away.

Hidden tracks still play and are in exports: hiding only tidies the view. What's hidden and your sets are kept in the song; showing and hiding aren't undo steps. Hiding a folder hides what's in it; showing a track in a folder shows the folder too.

Keep a track in view while scrolling (pin it)

Right-click a track > Pin to the Top (stays in view): it moves above the other tracks, under a line, and stays there while they scroll (a lead vocal while editing the band, a guide track). Unpin puts it back in its place. Pinning is kept in the song, and isn't an undo step.

Find what's eating the CPU (CPU Use)

Click the CPU reading at the bottom right of the window, or View > Panels > CPU Use, and play the song: a window lists each track's share of the audio's time (and the master's), the busiest first, with each of its plugins that uses any under it, and a bar for each (red when one takes a quarter of the time alone). It updates three times a second while playing; nothing is measured while the song is stopped. Double-click a plugin to open it; right-click it to Bypass It (or Turn It Back On), freeze its track or deactivate the track (see below).

Free up CPU by deactivating or freezing a track

  • Right-click a track > Deactivate Track: its plugins are unloaded (a plugin running in its own process is closed) and the track is silent, greyed out and left out of exports, its settings kept. Activate Track brings it back exactly as it was. Deactivating a folder takes its tracks with it. Undoable. Use it for a track you're not using now but want to keep.
  • Right-click > Freeze: the track's clips are recorded through its plugins as they sound and the plugins switched off; it still plays, from the recording. Its fader, pan and sends stay live (move them freely). The header says Frozen. If you change its clips or plugins it turns to an amber Changed: choose Freeze Again (it's changed since). Unfreeze brings the plugins back. Use it for a finished track with heavy plugins. Only audio and instrument tracks freeze; drum channel tracks (and a Drum Machine split into them) can't.

Write notes for a song or a track

View > Panels > Notes, CtrlAltPOptionCmdP, opens a side panel with the song's notes (lyrics, ideas, what's left to do) and the selected track's notes (mic, amp settings, which take to keep; the box waits until a track is selected). They're saved with the song as you type (not undo steps).

Editing audio

Select, move and copy clips

  • Click a clip to select it; Shift-click adds or removes clips. Drag across empty lane space to select clips on several tracks (Shift adds).
  • Drag a clip to move it, also onto another track: an outline shows where it lands. Several selected clips move together. The clip's start lands on the grid (a clip that was off the grid jumps onto it); hold CtrlCmd while dragging to ignore the grid. A clip can't go before the start of the song.
  • Edit > Copy / Cut / Paste, CtrlCCmdC / CtrlXCmdX / CtrlVCmdV: paste goes at the playhead, on the tracks the clips came from, and replaces what was there.
  • Edit > Select All, CtrlACmdA, selects every clip.
  • Edit > Delete, Backspace or Delete, removes them (selected tracks too).

A clip moved over another wins: the one underneath is trimmed back to a 10 ms crossfade (a MIDI clip cut exactly, with no crossfade), or removed if it's almost all covered.

Split a clip

  • Put the playhead where to cut, select the clip and Edit > Split at Playhead, CtrlTCmdT.
  • Or choose the Scissors Tool (press T for the tool menu) and click the clip where to cut.

With Edit > Edit Options > Snap Edits to Zero Crossings on (it is by default, and kept with the song), cuts and trims land where the waveform crosses zero (within 5 ms), so they don't click; each selected clip at its own crossing. Clips that are stretched, reversed, looped or pitch-shifted are cut exactly where asked. The two halves meet exactly, with no crossfade. With the Scissors, clicking a selected clip cuts all the selected clips there.

Trim the start or end of a clip

Drag a clip's left or right edge. Trimming follows the snap grid (hold CtrlCmd, to ignore it) and, with Edit > Edit Options > Snap Edits to Zero Crossings on, lands on a zero crossing. Trimming only hides audio: drag the edge back out to get it back (as far as the recording goes; a clip can't be shorter than 10 ms). Trimmed over a neighbour, the trimmed clip wins and the neighbour is cut back to a 10 ms crossfade. The trim edges are the outer few pixels of a clip (a very narrow clip has none: zoom in). To slide the audio inside a clip without moving the clip, see "Nudge, repeat and slip clips".

Add fades and crossfades

Drag the fade handle at a clip's top-left or top-right corner (just under the name) to fade in or out. Right-click the clip > Fade In Shape / Fade Out Shape: Linear, Fast (convex), Slow (concave) or S-curve; choosing a shape on a clip without a fade gives it a beat-long one (at most a third of the clip). Fade drags don't snap, and a clip's fade in and fade out together can't be longer than the clip. Only audio clips have fades. Fade lengths can also be typed in the Inspector (I). Overlapping clips crossfade by themselves (10 ms; drawn as an X); drag the later clip's fade-in handle at the join to make the crossfade longer: it stays centred on the join, as long as there's audio either side, and the status line shows its length.

Change a clip's volume (clip gain) or normalise it

Right-click an audio clip > Gain: +6, +3, +1, -1, -3 or -6 dB, Normalise (to -0.1 dB) or Reset to 0 dB (the menu shows the gain now: "Gain (-3.0 dB)"). Normalise sets the gain from the loudest sample in the part the clip plays (fades and envelope aside), each selected clip on its own. Clip gain goes from -60 to +24 dB; type it, or drag it (-24 to +24), in the Inspector. The clip's gain shows in its name strip after the name. Settings in the clip menu apply to every selected audio clip (MIDI clips in the selection are left alone).

Draw a volume or pan envelope on a clip

Right-click an audio clip > Volume Envelope (or Pan Envelope): a line over the clip. Click the line to add a point, drag points to shape it (the status line shows the value; points don't snap and can't pass their neighbours), AltOption-click a point to remove it. Volume goes from silence up to +6 dB, on a scale like a fader's; pan has left at the top. The envelope moves and copies with the clip. The envelope commands act on the clip you right-click; Clear Envelopes removes both from every selected clip.

Nudge, repeat and slip clips

  • Edit > Nudge Left / Nudge Right, Alt←Option← / Alt→Option→: move the selected clips one grid step (10 ms with the grid off); never before the start. Their automation goes with them when Edit > Edit Options > Automation Moves with Clips is on.
  • Edit > Repeat, CtrlRCmdR: a copy of the selection right after it (several clips keep their layout), selected, replacing what's there; press again for more.
  • Slip: AltShiftOptionShift-drag a clip to move the audio inside it while the clip stays put (not snapped, and only as far as the recording goes).
  • Loop: drag the right end of a clip's name strip (its top-right corner) to repeat the clip as a loop, on the grid; right-click > Unloop puts it back to one pass.

Use the snap grid, triplets and swing

The Snap box above the track headers ("Snap: Beat" to start with) sets the grid for moving, trimming and nudging: Off, Bar, Beat, 1/8, 1/16, or the triplets 1/4 T, 1/8 T, 1/16 T. Hold CtrlCmd while dragging to ignore it. The feel box next to it is Straight, Swing 54, 58, 62, 66 or 70% (on the Beat, 1/8 and 1/16 grids; bars and triplets don't swing), or a groove from a played part (right-click a MIDI clip, or an audio clip, > Use as Groove; it applies when its grid is the snap grid). Snapping and quantising follow the feel.

The lines in the tracks (and the ticks in the ruler) are the snap grid's own: a line at every place a drag can land, swing included, bars strongest, then beats, then the steps between them. Zoomed out, where they'd crowd together, only the beats are drawn, then only the bars. With the grid off, bars and beats show; on Bar, only bars.

Use the edit tools (pointer, marquee, scissors, glue, mute, zoom, draw, play)

Press T (Edit > Tools > Tool Menu) to choose what the mouse does in the tracks: Pointer Tool (select, move, trim and fade), Marquee Tool (select a range of time across tracks), Scissors Tool (click a clip to split it), Glue Tool (click the selected MIDI clips to join them), Mute Tool (click a clip to mute or unmute it; muted clips are dimmed), Zoom Tool (click to zoom in there, AltOption-click to zoom out, drag across what you want to fill the view), Draw Tool (drag across an empty part of an instrument track: a MIDI clip that long, on the grid) and Play Tool (hold the mouse down anywhere in the tracks: it plays from there until you let go, then goes back; not while recording). Fade work is an ordinary mouse gesture with the pointer. A tool acts on the clip you click, or on all the selected clips when that one is among them. The Glue Tool joins MIDI clips; for audio, use Track > Bounce in Place.

Select a range across tracks and edit it

Choose the Marquee Tool (press T and pick it), then drag across one or more tracks; its edges snap to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, not to). Dragging a range deselects any selected clips, so the commands below act on the range alone. Its edges cut clips exactly (not at zero crossings). With a range:

  • Edit > Delete, Cut, Copy and Paste act on just that range.
  • Edit > Delete and Close Gap, ShiftBackspace: remove it and move what's after earlier (the tracks' automation too; over every track, the markers, tempo, chords and lyrics as well).
  • Edit > Insert Silence: push everything from the range's start later by its length (see "Insert bars of silence").
  • Transport > Set Loop to Selection, CtrlUCmdU: loop it.
  • Track > Bounce in Place (CtrlBControlB): bounce just that range.
  • File > Export Other Formats > Export Range as WAV...: what each track plays there, with its effects, one file per track.
  • View > Zoom > Zoom to Fit, Z: fit it in view.
  • Drag inside it to move what's in it (clips cut at its edges, and its automation) to another place on the same tracks, snapped to the grid: what was where it lands is replaced, and the automation where it was holds the values either side. Hold AltOption to copy it instead (a razor edit).
  • Drag its right edge to stretch what's in it from its start: audio is time-stretched with its pitch kept (each clip's Stretch Mode; it stops following the tempo), MIDI notes spread out (or squeezed together), and the automation with them. Longer, it covers what came after on those tracks; shorter (down to 50 ms), it leaves a gap. Warped clips are left as they are.

Each is one undo step. Click an empty lane to clear the range.

Delete so the gap closes (ripple editing)

Turn on Edit > Edit Options > Ripple Edits: deleting (or cutting) clips moves the later clips on the track earlier to close the gap; their automation stays where it is. It's off whenever a song opens. Or use Edit > Delete and Close Gap, ShiftBackspace, once, on selected clips or a marquee range (a range takes its automation along).

Reverse, change channels or export a clip

Right-click an audio clip:

  • Reverse plays it backwards.
  • Channels: Stereo, Mono (Left + Right), Swap Left and Right, Left Only, Right Only.
  • Export as WAV... saves the clip as heard (gain, fades, envelope, reversed or warped, its clip effects with their tail), without the track's effects, as a 24-bit WAV in the song's Exports folder; with several clips selected, a file each in a folder.

Remove silence between phrases (Strip Silence)

Right-click an audio clip (a vocal, a tom track) > Strip Silence and choose Quiet recordings (-50 dB), Normal (-40 dB) or Loud or noisy (-30 dB): the quiet parts are cut out, leaving a clip for each phrase or hit. Gaps shorter than a quarter of a second are kept, and each piece keeps a moment before the sound (10 ms) and after it (80 ms), so nothing is clipped off; there are no fades. The status line says how many clips are left; one undo puts them all back. Clips that follow the tempo, play reversed or loop are skipped.

Make audio follow the song's tempo

Right-click an audio clip (a drum loop, say) > Follow Tempo: it stretches with the song's tempo and tempo changes, keeping its pitch, and stays locked to the beat. Its own tempo is taken to be the song's where it starts; for a loop recorded at another tempo, right-click it > Detect Tempo... > Clip Follows Song (see "Find the tempo of a recording"). Ordinary audio stays where it is in time when the tempo changes; MIDI always stays on its beats.

Right-click it > Stretch Mode for how it's stretched (following the tempo, warped or pitch shifted): Standard (all-round, the default), Percussive (the sharpest hits: drums, picked guitar) or Smooth (bass, vocals, pads: softer attacks). All three stay on the beat; stretched a long way (half speed, say) hits soften and smear in any of them.

Tighten the timing of a drum take (warp and audio quantise)

  • Select audio clips and press Q (Edit > Quantise), or right-click > Quantise Audio to Grid: their hits move all the way to the snap grid (1/16 with the grid off), with its swing or groove; hits less than half a millisecond off stay. Finding the hits can take a few seconds on a long clip. MIDI clips in the selection are left alone (quantise them in the MIDI editor). On clips in an edit group, Q opens Quantise Group instead (below).
  • For hand edits, right-click > Warp (it also turns on Follow Tempo): click in the clip's top strip to put a marker on the nearest hit, drag to pin the audio there and move it; drag a marker to move it (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid); AltOption-click a marker to remove it. Remove Warp Markers starts over.

Edit a multi-mic drum kit as one (edit groups)

Select the drum tracks (or their folder) and choose Track > Group > Group for Editing, CtrlShiftGShiftCmdG. Their headers show G1 (G2 for the next group). Now clicking a clip on one mic selects the clips at the same time on all of them, so a cut (CtrlTCmdT), move, nudge, delete or quantise does the same to the whole kit; trimming, fading or slipping one clip does the same to the others; a marquee range over one track covers the group; and comping a take on one mic (the takes lanes) comps the same take on the others. Edit > Edit Groups On turns grouping off for a moment (to fix one mic on its own; the badges go faint); Track > Group > Ungroup for Editing ends it.

Tighten a multi-mic drum take without phase problems (Quantise Group)

Quantising each drum mic on its own moves each by its own hits, so the overheads and rooms drift away from the close mics and the kit sounds smeared. With the kit grouped (above), select a clip on any mic and press Q (or Edit > Quantise Group...); to do only a section, drag a marquee over it first. In the window:

  • Tick the tracks to find the hits on: the kick and snare are ticked to start with (the inside kick and top snare mics).
  • Move them by: Cutting before each hit, crossfading the joins slices every mic 4 ms before each hit and moves the slices, with 6 ms crossfades: the hits stay exactly as played and the mics exactly in phase (the usual choice for drums). Warping (stretching between the hits) stretches the audio between the hits instead: no joins, the mics within a millisecond of each other.
  • Strength (100%: all the way to the grid) and Sensitivity (50% to start; higher finds ghost notes too), both remembered. Hits within 30 ms of each other on different mics count as one.

It uses the arrange view's grid and feel (swing, groove). One CtrlZCmdZ puts it all back.

Jump to the next drum hit (Tab)

Transport > Go To > Go to Next Hit, Tab, moves the playhead to the next hit (transient) on the selected tracks (or on the selected clips' tracks; with nothing selected, any track); ShiftTab goes back to the one before. Handy for cutting at hits (CtrlTCmdT, splits there) and checking a quantise.

Slow down, speed up or transpose a clip

Right-click an audio clip:

  • Varispeed (tape: faster is higher): 50, 67, 75, 80, 90, 95, 100, 105, 110, 125, 133, 150 or 200%, like a tape machine; length and pitch change together. The clip's start stays and its end moves (check it doesn't now run over the next clip), and it stops following the tempo.
  • Pitch Shift (keeps the length): -12 to +12 semitones, in the clip's Stretch Mode.

Both apply to every selected clip.

Correct the pitch of a vocal

Double-click an audio clip (or right-click > Pitch Correction...) to open the Pitch editor under the tracks: sung notes as bars and the pitch as a line. Tick Correct: after a moment the corrected line sits on the notes of the key and the clip plays in tune, the voice still sounding like the singer. Choose which notes are in tune (Song key, Chromatic or Notes I choose), and set how far notes go (Correction, 100% to start), how quickly (Speed, 0 to 400 ms, 40 to start: 0 is the robotic effect, 100-200 ms keeps vibrato) and how much natural drift stays (Humanise, 30% to start). Recorded clips open it; imported ones too. Drag a note to send it to another note (hold AltOption, to move it in cents); double-click one to leave it as sung. Untick Correct to hear the original.

Fix single notes in the Pitch editor (split, join, vibrato, timing)

Right-click a note in the Pitch editor:

  • Split the Note Here (a melisma or run found as one note, tuned to one pitch) or Join with the Next Note (a legato note found as two).
  • Octave Heard Wrong: the note's real pitch is an octave above or below what's shown.
  • Vibrato (none, half, as sung, more, or as Speed decides), Drift (straightened, half, as sung, or as Humanise decides) and Tilt (a rise or fall through the note, a half or a whole semitone): for that note only, whatever Speed and Humanise say.
  • Formants (bigger or smaller voice, -3 to +3) and Level (louder or quieter by 3 or 6 dB).
  • Leave This Note as Sung, Back to the Nearest Note in Tune, Reset This Note.

To move a note's timing, drag its start or end (the pointer shows arrows), or Shift-drag the note to move it whole: it snaps to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, not to). That's written as warp markers on the clip, the audio half a second either side pinned so only the note and the gaps beside it stretch.

Tune a screamed or gritty vocal

In the Pitch editor's More menu, Pitch Heard In says how readily pitch is heard: Screams Left Alone hears pitch only in clean singing, so a scream or growl isn't touched (no warbling); Clean Voice, Normal (the default), Gritty or Breathy Voice and Rough Voice hear it in rougher and rougher singing. To leave one stretch alone (a scream, a spoken line), drag across it in an empty part of the editor and right-click > Leave This Stretch Alone (Correct This Stretch Again undoes it). Leave Notes Already in Tune (Flex-Tune: Off, 10, 20, 30 or 50 cents) leaves notes that close to a note in tune where they are, so only the ones that are off move.

Formants, the chord lane, breaths and levels for a vocal

In the Pitch editor's More menu:

  • Formants (Whole Clip): the whole clip's voice bigger (-) or smaller (+), -4 to +4, without changing its pitch (a double that sounds like another singer).
  • Follow the Chord Lane (Chord Snap): each note goes to the nearest note of the chord playing there, not of the key (where the chord lane has chords).
  • Breaths and Esses: the breaths turned down 6, 10 or 15 dB (or breaths 10 dB and esses 4 dB), as dips in the clip's volume envelope (which you can then adjust).
  • Level the Notes: quiet notes up and loud ones down, Gently (half way) or Fully (all to one level), also in the envelope.
  • Correct the Track's Other Clips the Same Way: a comp's clips with the same settings (not the note-by-note changes).
  • Show Other Tracks Behind: the pitch of doubles and harmonies at the same time, faintly, to match them.

Write lyrics along the song (the lyrics lane)

View > Lanes > Lyrics Lane shows a lane under the chords. Click a line to select it (it lights up); Shift-click another to select the lines between, CtrlCmd-click to add or take away one. Drag to move the selected lines together (on the grid; hold CtrlCmd, to ignore it). CtrlCCmdC, CtrlXCmdX and Delete copy, cut and delete the selected lines; CtrlVCmdV pastes them at the playhead, as far apart as they were (the words go on the system's clipboard too, to paste into a message). Right-click for Add a Line Here..., Change, Copy, Cut, Paste Here, Delete, Paste Lyrics from Here... (a whole song's words, a line each, a bar apart, to drag into place after), Clear All Lyrics, Lyrics Window... and Lyrics Prompter. Hyphens split a word into its syllables for the lead sheet ("beau-ti-ful").

Double-click the lane (or View > Lanes > Lyrics Lines...) for the Lyrics Lines window: every line with where it's sung (bar|beat and the time), the one being sung lit. Click a position or the words to type new ones: a position takes a bar ("17"), a bar and beat ("17.3") or a time ("1:23.5") and moves the line there; empty words take the line out. Add Line puts one where you double-clicked (or a bar after the selected line) with its words ready to type; Delete takes the selected lines out; Go To puts the playhead on the selected line; Paste Words... pastes a whole song's words from it. The rows selected there are the lane's selected lines too, so Copy and Paste work on them. While the song plays, the lane's header shows the first word of the line being sung. View > Lanes > Lyrics Prompter shows the words big, for the singer: the line being sung, the next under it, and a bar across for how far through the line the song is. It opens by itself when recording starts if the song has lyrics; put it on a screen the singer can see. On the lead sheet (Notation, and the printed and PDF parts) the lyrics go under the first part that isn't drums, a syllable a note.

Make a harmony from the lead vocal

Open the lead's clip in the Pitch editor and choose More > Make a Harmony Voice: a third, fifth or sixth above, or a third, fourth or octave below. The notes (as corrected) move that many notes of the key (or the chord, following the chord lane), keeping half the lead's own vibrato so it sounds like a second singer, onto a new track under the lead ("Lead Harmony (3rd above)"). Undo takes it away.

Tighten double-tracked guitars or stacked vocals (align to a reference take)

Pick the take the others should follow (usually the tightest). Select all the takes (Shift-click them), then right-click the reference > Align Selected to This Clip > Tight (100%), Natural (70%) (a little of each player's looseness kept) or Loose (40%). Recorded or imported audio clips only. The hits (palm mutes, picked notes, consonants) of each other take are paired with the reference's nearest, within 80 ms, and moved onto them with warp markers; between hits the audio is stretched, with the Percussive stretch mode so the attacks stay sharp. A hit with no partner nearby (a note only one take plays) is left where it is. Move Whole Clips (no stretching) only slides each clip by their typical difference, for takes recorded together that are just a little apart. It takes a few seconds per take; one undo takes it all back, and the warp markers can be moved by hand afterwards (see warping above). The clip's name strip says "warp" once it's aligned.

Match a double's pitch to the lead vocal

Select the doubles (or gang vocals) and the lead, right-click the lead > Match Pitch of Selected to This Clip > Tight, Natural or Loose. Each double is pulled toward the lead's pitch moment by moment, wherever they're singing the same note (within two semitones, so a harmony is left alone), keeping its own formants, so it still sounds like a second voice. The double plays a new file in the song's Audio folder ("matched" on its name strip); right-click it > Remove Pitch Match puts its own audio back. Do it after aligning the timing.

Take out a squeak, a click, hum or mouth clicks (Repair)

Right-click a recorded clip > Repair:

  • Remove Mouth Clicks: lip smacks and tongue clicks on a close-miked vocal, found and smoothed over (the voice around them left alone).
  • Remove Hum (50 Hz: Europe) / (60 Hz: the Americas): mains hum and its harmonics, in narrow notches.
  • Spectral Repair...: the clip's spectrogram (time across, pitch up, louder brighter). Drag a box over the sound to take out (a chair squeak, a pick click, a cough stands out brighter), then Remove Selected: each frequency in the box comes down to what it is just before and after it, so what stood out goes and what was under it stays.

Each makes a new file the clip plays (undoable); Take Off Repairs goes back to the original. The spectral window is titled "Spectral Repair" with the clip's name.

Move the playhead

  • Click anywhere in the lanes: on empty space, a clip, a folder, a section, a chord, a lyric or the tempo. The playhead goes there, snapped to the grid (hold CtrlCmd, for exactly where you clicked), and the click does what it does there too (a click on a clip still selects it). A click in the ruler puts it exactly where you clicked. Clicks (in the ruler too) don't move it while recording.
  • Or only by dragging it: turn off Settings > Click in the Tracks Moves the Playhead. Then a click in the tracks selects and edits as usual and leaves the playhead where it is; to move it, drag the playhead's line in the tracks (the pointer becomes a two-way arrow over it), and it stays wherever you let go (on the grid; hold CtrlCmd, for exactly there). A click in the ruler still moves it. Kept between launches.
  • From the keys (in Transport > Go To, and Go to Start in Transport): - Left / Right, or , and . : back or on a bar. Back from inside a bar goes to its start first. - Shift← / Shift→: back or on a grid step (a beat with the grid off). - Page Up / Page Down: back or on by a screen (the width of the arrange view). - Alt,Option, / Alt.Option.: the start of the section before or the next one. - Return or Home: the start. End: the end of the song (where its last clip ends). - Tab / ShiftTab: the next or previous drum hit (see "Jump to the next drum hit (Tab)").
  • When the playhead moves out of view (from a key, Go to Start, or the assistant), the arrange view scrolls to show it. While playing, it pages along as the playhead reaches the edge.
  • Another key set (Settings > Key Commands...) puts some of these on that set's keys (the sections, the end, the bars on the numpad).

Follow the playhead while playing

View > Follow Playhead, CtrlShiftFCmdShiftF, on to start with: while the song plays, the tracks page along when the playhead reaches the right edge. Editing while it plays (moving or trimming a clip, dragging a marquee) or scrolling along the song yourself pauses it, so the view stays where you're working. It follows again on the next Play or Stop, or when you move the playhead (a click, a key). Zooming doesn't pause it. Turned off, the tracks stay where they are while playing.

Hear the audio as I drag the playhead (scrub)

Grab the playhead in the ruler (the pointer turns to a two-way arrow over it), or AltOption-drag anywhere in the ruler, and drag: the audio under it plays in tiny loops as it moves, to find a hit, a word or a click by ear. Let go and the playhead stays there (the song plays on if it was playing).

Put a clip at an exact place (spot)

Right-click a clip > Spot... and type where it starts: a bar (17), a bar and beat (17.3), with ticks (17.3.480, 960 a beat), or a time (1:23.5, 83.5s, 0:01:23.5). With several clips selected, the earliest goes there and the others keep their places relative to it (MIDI clips too). One undo step. Unlike a drag, it doesn't trim the clips it lands on: check for overlaps.

Zoom and scroll

  • Zoom: View > Zoom > Zoom In / Zoom Out, Ctrl=Cmd= / Ctrl-Cmd- or Ctrl→Cmd→ / Ctrl←Cmd←, CtrlCmd-scroll, the scroll wheel over the ruler, or pinch on a trackpad (a Windows touchpad's pinch works too). It zooms around the pointer, or around the playhead when the pointer is near it, so the playhead stays put.
  • Drag up or down in the ruler to zoom (down zooms in), moving sideways in the same drag to scroll. A drag sideways sets the loop range as before. Double-click the ruler to zoom to fit, as Z does (the range, the selected clips or the whole song; again to go back).
  • Scroll: the wheel scrolls through the tracks, Shift-scroll scrolls sideways along the song, and a trackpad's two fingers scroll both ways at once. Drag with the middle mouse button anywhere in the lanes to pan both ways.
  • Taller or shorter tracks: AltOption-scroll or CtrlShiftCmdShift-scroll, as well as View > Zoom > Taller Tracks / Shorter Tracks. In the piano roll and the drum editor, the same makes the note rows taller or shorter, the row under the pointer staying where it is.
  • View > Zoom > Zoom to Fit, Z: fit the marquee range, the selected clips or the whole song; press Z again to go back.
  • View > Zoom > Previous Zoom, ShiftZ, goes back to how it was zoomed before, and again further back (each zoom is kept once it has settled, however many wheel or pinch moves made it); View > Zoom > Next Zoom, AltShiftZOptionShiftZ, goes on again.
  • Each song opens zoomed and scrolled as it was when it was last closed (kept in Take 5's settings, not the song, so zooming never asks to save). The main window comes back at the size and place it was when Take 5 was quit, full screen if it was.

Bring in audio files

  • Drag audio (or MIDI) files from Finder or Explorer onto a track: a green ghost shows where it lands. A new track is named, coloured and given an icon from the file's name: "KICK_IN_01.wav" arrives as "Kick In", red, with a drum (drums red, bass purple, guitars orange, keys green, vocals blue...); stems from another studio arrive looking like a session (a name that says nothing about an instrument stays as it is). AAF and other DAWs' sessions get the colours and icons, keeping their track names. Track > Colour Tracks by Instrument does it to the song's tracks now (keeping their names). Several files: the first on that track, the rest on new tracks below; hold AltOption to put them all on that track one after another.
  • Drop files between tracks, below the last one or into an empty song for new tracks there.
  • File > Import Audio or MIDI..., CtrlShiftIShiftCmdI: asks whether to place them at the playhead or at bar 1, on new tracks (one each) or one after another on the selected track, and for MIDI files whether to keep the song's tempo or use the file's.
  • View > Panels > Audio File Browser, F: browse folders, click a file to hear it, drag files in. Right-click a file > Tags... to tag it (kick, punchy, room: kept for every song) and the places list then has Tagged: kick...; right-click > Find Similar Sounds lists the sounds in the folder (and the folders in it) most like it, by how long, bright, sharp and noisy they are: a kick finds kicks, a hat finds hats, for drum replacement's samples. Click one to hear it.

Files are copied into the song's Audio folder.

Arranging

Add and name sections (intro, verse, chorus)

Sections show in the sections lane under the ruler (View > Lanes > Sections Lane, ShiftG). Put the playhead where a section starts and choose Track > Add Section Marker, Alt'Option': the marker goes at the nearest bar, named "Section 1", "Section 2"... Or right-click the sections lane > Add Section Marker Here (on the grid where you clicked). A section runs to the next marker; the last one to the end of the song. With the playhead where a section already starts, the status line says so and nothing is added. Double-click a section to rename it, or right-click > Name for Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Solo, Breakdown or Outro (Rename... for your own name, Add Comment Here... for a note at that point). Drag a marker's left edge to move it (it snaps to the grid). To jump from section to section, use Transport > Go To > Previous Section / Next Section, Alt,Option, / Alt.Option.. Each kind of section has a colour of its own (the usual names all differ: a bridge never looks like the chorus), and every one of a kind looks the same ("Chorus 2", "Verse B" and a "Refrain" too).

Loop, duplicate or delete a whole section

Right-click a section in the sections lane:

  • Loop This Section sets the loop to it, turns looping on (and punch off) and puts the playhead at its start: for rehearsing or recording takes over just that part.
  • Duplicate Section repeats it right after itself on every track (a second chorus).
  • Delete Section (and Everything in It) removes it and closes the gap.
  • Remove Marker Only keeps the music and removes the marker.

The clips (cut at the section's edges), takes, automation on every track and the master, chords and lyrics go with the music: a duplicated section has its own copy of them, and after a deleted one they move up to close the gap. So do tempo and time-signature changes: a duplicated section starts at its own tempo and changes where it does, and after a deleted one the song goes on at the tempo that played after it. Each is one undo step.

Try the song in another order (arranger chains)

Track > Arranger Chains opens a window with the song's sections (from its markers) as buttons: click them in the order you want them played, a section as often as you like (a chorus twice) and leaving out the ones you don't (the bridge cut). The chain shows as a list, each section with its bars and where it starts in the chain: Move Up, Move Down, Remove and Clear change it. Play Chain plays the song in that order without changing it (the song's playhead jumps along to where each section is in the song; the button turns to Stop, and pressing Play on the song stops the chain). New, Duplicate, Rename... and Delete manage several chains to compare, and the drop-down switches between them; they're kept in the song, and each change is undoable. A section whose marker you delete drops out of the chains. Export as WAV... saves a chain's version as a file (in the song's exports, "<song> - <chain>.wav" to start with). Flatten into Song... (after asking) rewrites the song in the chain's order: every track's clips (cut at the sections' edges, with short fades there so the joins don't click), automation, markers, chords and lyrics. Sections the chain leaves out are gone; one undo takes it all back. Anything before the first marker is the section "Start". A song that changes tempo or time signature can't use chains yet.

Insert bars of silence

Press T and choose Marquee from the tool menu, drag a range as long as the gap you want, starting where the gap should go, and choose Edit > Insert Silence: everything from the range's start moves later by its length, on the tracks the range covers, their automation too (the gap holds the value it had there); a clip across the start is split there. With nothing selected, the status line says how. Over every track, it's the whole song that makes room: the section markers, tempo and time-signature changes, chords, lyrics and takes after it move too. Over some tracks, only those tracks move. Edit > Delete and Close Gap, ShiftBackspace, does the opposite: it takes the range out and pulls what's after it back (over every track, the whole song's). One undo step each.

Set the tempo and time signature

Double-click the tempo in the transport bar's display and type a new one (20 to 400 BPM); pick a time signature from the box next to it (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/8 or 12/8; the tempo lane has more). Both change the tempo or signature the playhead is in: the whole song, for a song with no changes, or that stretch of it (to add a change, use the tempo lane). MIDI stays on its beats; ordinary audio stays where it is in time, so a recording no longer lines up with the bars (audio clips set to follow the tempo are stretched with it). The display always shows the tempo and signature at the playhead. Each change is one undo step.

Tap the tempo

Transport > Tap Tempo, ShiftT: tap along at least twice and the tempo the playhead is in is set from your taps (the last eight, averaged). A pause of two seconds starts the count again. A run of taps is one undo step. The first tap only starts the count: the status line says to keep going. To set the tempo by typing, double-click the tempo in the transport bar. For a whole free-time take, see "Fit the tempo to a band take".

Add tempo changes and ramps (the tempo lane)

View > Lanes > Tempo Lane, G, shows the lane under the ruler. Double-click (or right-click > Add Tempo Change Here) to add a tempo change; it starts at the tempo already playing there. Drag a point up or down for the tempo, in whole BPM (Shift: tenths), or sideways to move it (CtrlCmd, ignores the grid); a drag goes one way or the other, as it starts. A change can't be dragged past its neighbours, and the first one (the song's starting tempo) stays at the start. Double-click a point, or right-click > Set Tempo..., to type a tempo; AltOption-click it, or right-click > Remove This Change, to delete it. Right-click a point > Ramp to Next for a smooth speed-up or slow-down to the next change. Right-click or double-click the lane's top strip for a time signature from that bar (twelve to choose from), or to remove one.

Fit the tempo to a band take played without a click (Conduct Tempo)

  1. Choose Transport > Conduct Tempo (choose it again to stop without making anything).
  2. Play the take from the start and tap ShiftT on every beat (or every half bar, or bar), the first tap on a downbeat.
  3. Stop: say what each tap was ("Each tap was a beat", half a bar or a bar), then Make the Tempo (Keep Tapping to go on, Discard to throw the taps away). It needs two taps or more.

The bar lines now fall on the played downbeats, the tempo lane shows the speeding up and slowing down, and the audio hasn't moved, so the click and MIDI follow the band. The taps are corrected for the interface's delay, and the bars are in the song's starting time signature. One CtrlZCmdZ undoes it.

Find the tempo of a recording

Right-click an audio clip > Detect Tempo...: for a loop or a take played to a steady beat (six seconds or more), it suggests a tempo between 60 and 200 BPM, to a tenth of a beat per minute (or says none was found). It hears the beat from where the notes start, and the bar from how they repeat, so strummed eighths don't come out twice as fast; between two that fit equally well it picks the more usual tempo (a song at 70 could come out as 140). Choose Set Song Tempo to set the song's starting tempo to it, or Clip Follows Song to stretch the clip to the song's tempo instead. For a band take played without a click, see "Fit the tempo to a band take".

Make linked copies of a MIDI part or an audio clip

Select a clip (MIDI or audio) and choose Edit > Repeat Linked, CtrlAltROptionCmdR, or right-click it > Repeat Linked: a copy right after it, linked (shown by a link before its name).

  • MIDI: editing the notes of any copy changes them all.
  • Audio (the chorus guitar used three times): a fade, gain, pan, reverse, pitch, warp marker, envelope or clip effect on one is on all of them. Each keeps its own place, length, trim, name, colour and mute.

Right-click one > Unlink (it says how many others it's linked to) to vary it on its own. Plain Repeat, CtrlRCmdR, makes an independent copy.
